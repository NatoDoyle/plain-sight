# Sales

> The arena where someone is paid to get you to buy, now. Most selling is legitimate and most salespeople are fine — persuasion is not manipulation. The line is crossed at deception about the product, price, or terms, and at *engineered pressure designed to stop you thinking*. Knowing the classic closes and your consumer rights turns the pressure off.

## The arena

A salesperson's incentives (commission, quota, the close) are structurally misaligned with your interest in deciding calmly and comparing options. The capturing levers are **time pressure** (decide before you can deliberate or comparison-shop), **information asymmetry** (they know the real cost/margins; you don't), **commitment** (small agreements that escalate), and **reciprocity** (the gift, the "let me do you a favor"). Certain arenas concentrate the pressure: **car dealerships and timeshares** are the canonical high-pressure environments, along with door-to-door (solar, home improvement), funeral, gym memberships, and payday/subprime lending. The defensive frame: separate the *merits* of the purchase from the *pressure* to make it now, and use the deliberation the pressure is designed to prevent.

## Prevalence & base rates

Most transactions and salespeople are honest; selling is a legitimate, ubiquitous activity. Mainstream sales methodologies (SPIN, consultative/customer-centric selling, even the assertive Challenger approach) are largely about *understanding and addressing customer needs* — not coercion. So a base rate to hold: enthusiasm, discounts, scarcity that's real, and confident pitching are **not** manipulation. Manipulation is the marked exception, defined by deception and engineered pressure (see Caveats), not by the existence of a sales pitch.

## Top tactics in this arena

- **[[manufactured-urgency]]** — "today only," "this price expires when you leave," the manufactured deadline that prevents deliberation (exploits [[scarcity-urgency]]).
- **[[four-square-payment-packing]]** — the car-dealership worksheet that scrambles price / trade-in / down payment / monthly payment to shift focus to an easily-padded monthly number, then stuffs finance-office add-ons.
- **[[lowballing]]** — secure commitment to an attractive offer, then worsen it (the academic mechanism is *commitment-and-consistency*, Cialdini et al. 1978; the showroom "the manager won't approve that price" is its real-world variant).
- **[[thats-not-all-decoys]]** — Cialdini-family compliance closes: **door-in-the-face** (huge ask → "concession" to the real ask), **that's-not-all** (pile on a bonus before you can answer), and **decoy/asymmetric-dominance** options that steer the choice.
- **[[timeshare-playbook]]** — the captive presentation: a free-gift hook (reciprocity), a 90-minute pitch that runs for hours, and "sign today or lose this price."
- **[[charm-offensive]]** / **[[future-faking]]** / **[[mirroring-false-identity]]** — likeability and rapport as the close's lubricant; promised futures ("you'll use it every weekend").
- **[[lying-typologies]]** — bait-and-switch, omission of fees/terms, paltering about the product (where persuasion crosses into illegal deception).

## Arena-specific dynamics

The signature pattern is the **high-pressure close**: build rapport → manufacture urgency/scarcity → secure a small commitment → escalate (add-ons, financing) → block the exit ("let me get my manager"). The timeshare grind and the car-dealership four-square are the fully-developed forms. When a "sale" is actually fraud (fake product, nonexistent value), it becomes [[con-anatomy]]; when an opportunity is sold as income, see [[mlm-lifecycle]].

## Red-flag checklist

- The deadline is the main reason to buy ("today only") and pausing is discouraged.
- You're steered to a monthly payment instead of the total price; fees appear late.
- An agreed price mysteriously needs "manager approval" and creeps upward ([[lowballing]]).
- You can't leave, sleep on it, or comparison-shop without losing "the deal."
- Free gifts arrive before the ask (reciprocity hook); a "90-minute" presentation has no end.
- Specific factual claims about the product/price/terms that you can't verify — and aren't put in writing.

## Legal protections & reporting

*US, jurisdiction-labeled; not legal advice; some rules are in flux.* **Puffery** (subjective exaggeration — "best deal in town") is legal; **specific factual falsehoods are not**, and are **deceptive** under **FTC Act §5** (likely to mislead a reasonable consumer + material) and **state UDAP laws** (which, unlike federal §5, often give consumers a *private right of action*). **Cooling-Off Rule (16 CFR Part 429):** a 3-business-day right to cancel certain off-premises sales — **$25+ at your home, $130+ at other temporary locations** (hotel rooms, fairgrounds) — but **not** online, phone, mail, or in-store purchases (many states add their own home-solicitation rules). **Bait-and-switch is illegal.** **Auto-renewal/"negative option":** the FTC's "Click-to-Cancel" Rule was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit (July 2025) and is not in force** — auto-renewal is instead governed by **ROSCA** (federal) and state auto-renewal laws, and the FTC continues to pursue deceptive-cancellation practices under §5. Industry-specific: the **FTC Funeral Rule** (itemized pricing), the **Telemarketing Sales Rule** + **Do Not Call Registry**. (Penalty figures are inflation-adjusted yearly.)

## Exit & resources

The master counter is **time**: "I don't make decisions this size on the spot — I'll sleep on it" defeats nearly every engineered close ([[verification-rituals]]). Know your cooling-off rights (above); get every material claim **in writing**; comparison-shop away from the pressure; and bring a second person/opinion for big-ticket buys. Report deceptive sales to the **FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov)** and your **state attorney general / consumer-protection office**; for cars, the state DMV/consumer agency; for auto-renewal traps, dispute via your card issuer and cite ROSCA/state law.

## Caveats

- **Selling is not manipulation, and persuasion is legitimate.** A salesperson making a confident case, offering a real discount, or pointing out a genuine deadline is doing their job ([[epistemic-guardrails]]). Don't treat ordinary commerce as predation.
- **The line is deception + engineered pressure + exploitation:** (a) false/misleading claims about the product, **price, or terms**; (b) undisclosed pressure designed to *prevent deliberation* (manufactured urgency, marathon presentations, commitment traps); (c) targeting vulnerable buyers (elderly, grieving, financially desperate). Puffery, enthusiasm, and real scarcity are not that.
- **A real deadline is information, not a trap** ([[genuine-deadline]]): genuine inventory limits, true sale-end dates, and time-sensitive offers exist. The flag is the *arbitrary, resetting, or unexplained* deadline used to stop you thinking — not every deadline.
- **High price or hard bargaining ≠ fraud** ([[hard-bargaining]]): an aggressive negotiating posture you can walk away from is disclosed hardball, not manipulation.
