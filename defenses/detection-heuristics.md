# Detection Heuristics

> The first-pass screen for "is something off here?" — a small set of signals that tells you to **slow down, verify, and consult**, never a test that convicts a person. The cardinal rule: no single flag means manipulation; what matters is a *conjunction* of pressure, urgency, emotion, isolation, and secrecy that **survives a reality-check with someone you trust**.

## What it counters

This is the keystone recognition layer — the most-referenced defense in the KB, the declared counter to the broadest range of tactics because it fires *before* you've identified which specific tactic is in play. It is the front-line screen against the pressure-and-speed tactics ([[manufactured-urgency]], [[scarcity-urgency]], pressure closes), the emotion-exploiting levers ([[emotional-flooding]], [[fear-exploitation]], [[guilt-leverage]], [[emotional-blackmail]]), the trust-and-identity fabrications ([[charm-offensive]], [[mirroring-false-identity]], [[love-bombing]], [[future-faking]]), the reality-distorters ([[gaslighting]], [[darvo]]), and the whole architecture of [[scams-fraud]] and [[con-anatomy]]. It pairs with — and hands off to — [[verification-rituals]] (confirm the specific claim) and [[boundary-scripts]] (hold the line once you've decided).

## How to execute

Run the screen as a *prompt to pause*, not a verdict. Five questions; the more that answer "yes," and the more they co-occur and *persist*, the higher the suspicion.

**1. The pressure-and-isolation conjunction (the core screen).** Ask whether the situation combines:
- **Pressure** to commit, decide, or comply now;
- **Urgency** — an artificial or unexplained deadline ([[manufactured-urgency]]);
- **Emotion** — you're flooded (fear, infatuation, guilt, excitement) past calm deliberation ([[emotional-flooding]]);
- **Isolation** — you're being steered away from advisors, or told not to involve others ([[isolation-tactics]]);
- **Secrecy** — "don't tell anyone," "this is just between us";
- **(in money contexts) an unusual, irreversible payment** — gift cards, wire, crypto.

Any one is usually innocent. **Three or more, co-occurring, is the signature.** This conjunction is the backbone of every official scam-warning list (FTC, FBI/IC3, AARP) [1], and the felt experience of being *rushed past your own judgment* is itself the cue.

**2. Read the felt-sense tells.** Your internal state is data (a prompt to check, not proof):
- **FOG** — a fog of Fear, Obligation, and Guilt around a request (Susan Forward's term) [2];
- **Confusion or fog *after* a conversation** — you can't reconstruct how you ended up agreeing ([[gaslighting]], [[cognitive-overload-confusion]]);
- **A gap between someone's words and your gut**, or between their words and their *actions*;
- **Walking on eggshells**; relief when they leave the room.

**3. Judge actions, not words; accept no excuses** (George Simon) [3]. Covert manipulation hides in the gap between stated intentions and behavior. Weight the pattern of conduct over the explanation; *"past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior."* Don't get sidetracked into litigating motives you can't see — respond to what is actually being *done*.

**4. Notice the counterfeit trigger** (Robert Cialdini) [4]. The compliance levers — a favor that obligates ([[reciprocity]]), a closing window ([[scarcity-urgency]]), a badge of authority ([[authority]]), a crowd "everyone's doing it" ([[social-proof]]) — are legitimate shortcuts that get *faked*. When you feel the automatic pull to comply, ask: *is this trigger genuine, or manufactured to move me?* Cialdini's rule for reciprocity captures the whole move — a genuine favor deserves a favor, but *a trick deserves no favor*; once you redefine the "gift" as a compliance device, the obligation dissolves.

**5. Know the predatory-approach signals** (Gavin de Becker's pre-incident indicators) [5], useful with strangers and fast-moving new contacts: **forced teaming** (an unearned "we"), **charm and niceness** ("niceness does not equal goodness"), **too many unsolicited details**, **typecasting** (a small insult to bait you into proving them wrong), **loan-sharking** (unsolicited help that manufactures debt), the **unsolicited promise**, and **discounting your "no."**

**The master move:** the screen's only output is *slow down → verify → consult*. A positive screen never means "this person is a manipulator." It means "this is a situation to take to [[verification-rituals]] and to talk over with someone outside it." The single most reliable counter, named by the FTC itself, is **"slow down and talk to someone you trust"** [1] — which is exactly the step manipulation works to prevent.

## When to use it

Continuously and lightly, as ambient situational awareness — especially around **high-stakes or irreversible decisions** (money, commitments, access, signatures), **new or fast-escalating relationships**, **unsolicited contact**, and any moment you notice you're being hurried. Escalate to a deliberate, structured pass ([[manipulation-audit]]) when the screen lights up on several signals at once.

## When NOT to use it

- **Not as a character verdict on a specific person.** The screen flags *situations to check*, never grounds to diagnose or condemn an individual. Treat a hit as "verify," not "convict."
- **Not as a substitute for verification.** Detection is the smoke alarm; it tells you to look, not what you'll find. Don't act on the *suspicion* — act on what independent checking confirms ([[verification-rituals]]).
- **Not turned inward as self-surveillance or outward as a tool to police loved ones' every move** — that way lies the over-detection failure mode below.

## Failure modes & risks

- **Over-detection / paranoia (the main risk).** Every signal here has a high benign base rate: forced teaming looks like ordinary friendliness, charm like most kind people, "too many details" like an anxious or neurodivergent over-explainer, unsolicited help like genuine generosity, urgency like a real deadline. A screen that fires on *single* cues produces mostly false positives and, used relentlessly, corrodes trust and relationships ([[epistemic-guardrails]]). The defense against this is built into the screen: require *conjunction + persistence + survival of a reality-check*, not any one flag.
- **Manufactured intuition.** De Becker warns that real fear (an involuntary signal in the presence of a present danger cue) is different from self-generated *worry* and *anxiety* [5]. Anxiety can masquerade as "a bad feeling about someone" — which is why the screen routes to *verification*, not to acting on the feeling alone.
- **Mislabeling the neurodivergent, the culturally different, and the simply awkward.** Directness, flat affect, over-disclosure, gift-giving customs, and haggling norms all trip these cues without any manipulation present ([[epistemic-guardrails]]'s cultural-variation and incompetence-not-malice notes).
- **Retro-fitting.** Once primed, people find "red flags" in everything retrospectively. Guard the direction of inference: signals → *check*, not outcome → *re-narrate*.

## Evidence

Graded `supported` — but as a *synthesis of practitioner and regulatory heuristics, not a validated instrument*, and this matters: **there is no empirically validated screen for interpersonal-manipulation detection.** The components vary in support. The scam-warning conjunction has strong regulatory consensus grounded in aggregate complaint data (FTC/FBI-IC3/AARP) [1]. Cialdini's compliance principles rest on a large, broadly replicated experimental literature (`established`/`supported`) [4]. De Becker's pre-incident indicators [5] and Simon's covert-aggression signs [3] are influential *clinical/practitioner* frameworks, not validated predictors — they describe ordinary friendly behavior as readily as predatory behavior, which is precisely why they must be used as conjunction-and-verify prompts. Forward's FOG [2] is a popularized clinical construct. The information-literacy world has analogous heuristics (e.g., Caulfield's **SIFT** for online claims) but they target sources, not interpersonal manipulation, and are unvalidated for this use. Honest bottom line: these signals reliably tell you *when to slow down and verify*; they do not, alone, prove manipulation.

## Caveats

The entire value of this defense is in the conjunction and the hand-off. A single red flag is rung-1 evidence of an ordinary situation; the screen earns its place only when several signals co-occur, persist after you signal discomfort, and **survive being talked over with someone outside the situation** — the one move (slowing down, consulting a trusted third party) that is both the best detector and the thing manipulation most reliably tries to stop. Used as a hair-trigger it makes you paranoid and unjust; used as a prompt to verify it is the most valuable habit in the KB. It informs; [[verification-rituals]] confirms; [[boundary-scripts]] acts. (Distinguished from [[verification-rituals]]: detection is *noticing something may be off*; verification is *independently confirming a specific claim or identity*.)

## Sources

1. FTC, "How To Avoid a Scam" — the four signs: scammers *pretend* to be from a known organization; say there's a *problem or prize*; *pressure* you to act immediately ("they don't want you to … talk to someone you trust"); and tell you to *pay* in a specific, hard-to-reverse way (gift cards, wire, crypto). Corroborated by FBI/IC3 and AARP Fraud Watch (urgency + secrecy + irreversible payment + isolation). Regulatory-practitioner; aggregate complaint data, not a validated psychometric screen.
2. Forward, S., with Frazier, D. (1997). *Emotional Blackmail.* HarperCollins — **FOG** = Fear, Obligation, Guilt, the "blinding FOG" a blackmailer creates. Clinical/popular construct.
3. Simon, G. (1996/2010). *In Sheep's Clothing.* Parkhurst Brothers — recognition signs of covert aggression and responses: judge actions not words, accept no excuses, stay on the issue, set enforceable limits; "past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior." Clinical/practitioner.
4. Cialdini, R. (1984/2007/2021). *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* — automatic "click-whirr" responses triggered by single features that compliance professionals *counterfeit*; defense = detect the faked trigger and refuse ("a trick deserves no favor"; for scarcity, treat the arousal as a cue and ask use-vs-merely-possess). Established/supported experimental literature.
5. de Becker, G. (1997). *The Gift of Fear.* Little, Brown — survival signals / pre-incident indicators (forced teaming, charm & niceness, too many details, typecasting, loan-sharking, unsolicited promise, discounting "no"); intuition as "knowing without knowing why"; true fear vs. self-generated worry/anxiety. "Niceness does not equal goodness" (verbatim); "charm is a verb" is a paraphrase of his point. Clinical/practitioner; not a validated predictive instrument.
