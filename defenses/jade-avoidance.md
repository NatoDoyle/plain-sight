# JADE Avoidance

> **J**ustify, **A**rgue, **D**efend, **E**xplain — and the technique is to *stop doing all four* when a bad-faith person pushes on your "no." Every justification you offer a manipulator is treated as an opening to negotiate, attack, or reopen the debate; the more you explain, the more the boundary erodes. State your answer once, decline to litigate it, and disengage. The crucial limit: this is for **bad-faith actors only** — in honest relationships, explaining yourself is exactly what you *should* do.

## What it counters

The conversational tactics that weaponize your explanations: [[circular-conversation]] / word-salad, [[moving-goalposts]], [[darvo]], [[bad-faith-argumentation]] (sealioning, gish gallop), and the guilt/obligation leverage of [[guilt-tripping]] and [[emotional-blackmail]]. It's the verbal sibling of [[gray-rock]] and a core move within [[boundary-scripts]].

## How to execute

The discipline is *brevity + repetition + disengagement*, drawn from validated assertiveness training (the **"broken record"**; Smith's *When I Say No, I Feel Guilty*):

- **State your position once**, briefly and plainly. Offer a genuine clarification *if* one is actually asked — then stop.
- **Don't justify, argue, defend, or explain further.** Additional reasons are ammunition; you don't owe a bad-faith actor a case for your "no."
- **Broken record**: calmly repeat the same line without escalating detail — *"I've given you my answer." … "I'm not going to argue about this."*
- **Disengage** rather than be pulled into the loop: end the conversation, change the subject, or leave.

Defensive (not aggressive) phrasings: *"No, that doesn't work for me." · "I'm not able to do that." · "I've already answered." · "We're not going to agree on this, and that's okay." · "I'm not discussing this further."*

## When to use it

With people arguing in **bad faith** — where the other party isn't seeking mutual understanding but an opening: circular arguers, goalpost-movers, DARVO/blame-reversers, sealioners, high-conflict exes and co-parents, and anyone who treats every explanation as leverage.

## When NOT to use it

**This is the load-bearing caveat.** Healthy relationships *require* explanation, negotiation, and repair. JADE-avoidance is a tool for bad-faith actors — **not** a license to stop communicating, dodge legitimate accountability, or stonewall people who deserve an honest conversation. "Don't over-explain to a manipulator" is **not** "never explain yourself." Over-applied to ordinary conflict it becomes avoidance, stonewalling, and contempt — and it corrodes the good-faith relationships worth keeping. (Many popular JADE articles omit this; the omission is the danger.)

## Failure modes & risks

- **Misused as an accountability shield.** "I don't JADE" can become a way to refuse fair questions and never answer for anything — itself a control move. Reserve it for bad faith, not for whenever you're challenged.
- **Reads as cold/evasive in good faith.** With people who deserve dialogue, flat non-engagement damages trust and looks like [[silent-treatment|stonewalling]].
- **Can escalate some abusers.** Like other disengagement tactics, abruptly withholding the argument an abuser is used to can provoke escalation — in a violent/coercive relationship, pair it with a safety plan ([[dv-safety-planning]]).

## Evidence

Graded `supported`, with an honest split. The **acronym "JADE" is a practitioner/recovery-community coinage** (widely attributed to Al-Anon and spread via Out of the FOG / codependency / NPD-abuse communities) with **no direct empirical study** — that layer is `folk`. But what it actually instructs — **assertive brevity, the broken-record technique, declining to over-justify** — is standard **assertiveness training** (Smith, *When I Say No, I Feel Guilty*, 1975; the DESC script, Bower & Bower, 1976), which has real (if thin and aging) empirical support as an evidence-based skill set (Speed, Goldstein & Goldfried, 2018, "a forgotten evidence-based treatment"). So: trust the *components*, treat the catchy acronym as a mnemonic, not a validated protocol.

## Caveats

- **Bad faith is the precondition.** Confirm you're dealing with a manipulator, not a partner having a bad day, before withholding explanation (*normal relationship conflict*).
- **It's about *over*-explaining, not communication itself.** The healthy version still says the one clear sentence; it just doesn't get dragged into round two.
- **Not a character verdict or a weapon** — it governs *your* engagement, not a label you pin on someone.

## Safety notes

Like other disengagement tactics, refusing the argument an abuser is used to can provoke escalation. If there is any history of violence or coercive control, **pair this with a safety plan and outside support, not improvisation** ([[dv-safety-planning]], [[gray-rock]]): leaving and withdrawing are higher-risk moments.
