# Universal Red Flags

> The handful of warning signs that recur across *every* kind of manipulation — intimate, family, workplace, sales, scams, cults. None is proof on its own, and most have innocent explanations; what matters is the **cluster, the pattern over time, and the felt-sense**. The single most useful rule: **manufactured urgency is itself a red flag — slow down, and the pressure to decide *now* is usually the tell.**

## What it counters

A first-line recognition layer across the whole knowledge base: [[manufactured-urgency]], [[isolation-tactics]], [[love-bombing]], [[gaslighting]], [[darvo]], [[boundary-testing]], and the scam/[[con-anatomy]] family. It feeds the more structured [[detection-heuristics]], [[manipulation-audit]], and [[verification-rituals]].

## How to execute

Watch for the recurring cross-domain tells (cluster + pattern matter more than any single one):

- **Manufactured urgency / pressure** to act before you can think or check — the strongest, most universal flag (the FTC's own counter: *honest people give you time to decide*) [1].
- **Isolation** — being moved away from other perspectives ("don't tell anyone," "your friends don't get us," "keep this between us").
- **Too-good-to-be-true / asymmetric benefit** — the offer, the romance, the opportunity that's all upside and you're the lucky exception.
- **Secrecy** and **"no" not respected** — your boundaries or refusals get pushed, punished, or ignored ([[boundary-testing]]).
- **Inconsistency between words and actions**, and **blame-reversal** when you raise it ([[darvo]]).
- **The felt-sense** — confusion after interactions, dread, walking on eggshells, feeling drained or "crazy." Treat this as an **alarm to investigate, not a verdict.**

de Becker's *Gift of Fear* names specific pre-incident signals worth knowing: **forced teaming** ("we're in this together"), **charm/niceness** ("niceness is not goodness"), **too many details**, **typecasting** (a small insult to bait you into disproving it), **loan sharking** (unsolicited help that manufactures obligation), the **unsolicited promise**, and **discounting your "no"** [2].

**When a flag (or cluster) trips:** slow down (urgency is the tell) → get an **outside perspective** → **verify independently** (your own channel, not theirs — [[verification-rituals]]) → **name the pattern** → **document** → and treat the felt-sense as **permission to disengage**, no explanation owed.

## When to use it

Continuously and early — as a low-cost ambient filter on new people, offers, groups, and asks, especially during [[crisis-windows]] when you're more exposed.

## When NOT to use it

As a **verdict or a diagnosis**. A red flag is a prompt to look closer and verify, not a conviction. Don't run a checklist *at* a partner mid-argument, and don't use it to confirm a suspicion you've already settled on.

## Failure modes & risks

- **Over-detection / paranoia**: treating every flag as proof turns into suspicion of everyone and corrodes trust ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).
- **Weaponization**: "you're showing red flags" is itself usable as a control move; the accusation carries the same burden as any claim.
- **Intuition's bias**: the felt-sense is a real signal but not infallible — it can misfire on unfamiliarity or prejudice, so pair it with verification rather than acting on it blind [2].

## Evidence

Graded `supported`, mixed by component: the **FTC scam signs** are regulatory/established [1]; **coercive-control** red flags (isolation, monitoring, "no" not respected) are well-supported (Stark) [3]; **DARVO** has experimental support (Harsey & Freyd) [3]; **de Becker's signals** are influential *clinical/practitioner* observations, not RCT-validated [2]. Two items to hedge: the **"cycle of abuse"** hot/cold pattern is contested (not universal — coercive control is often constant, not cyclical), and **love-bombing** as a formal construct is thinly evidenced. The "trust your gut" framing is powerful but overstated (intuition is biased — Kahneman) — hence *alerting cue, then verify*.

## Caveats

- **Red flags are heuristics, not proof.** Most have innocent explanations — a generous neighbor isn't "loan sharking," a detailed talker isn't lying. It is the **conjunction and persistence** that signal manipulation ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).
- **Normal relationships have conflict, bad days, and clumsiness** (*normal relationship conflict*): the distinguishing markers point the *other* way — repair attempts, respect for your "no," support for your outside ties, and consistency over time.
- **Describe behavior, don't label the person.** Use the flags to decide *your* next move (verify, slow down, disengage), not to diagnose someone.
- If the flags point to abuse or danger, this is recognition only — for what to do, see [[dv-safety-planning]] and the **National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233** (US).
