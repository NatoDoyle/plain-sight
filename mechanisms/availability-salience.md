# Availability & Salience

> Your sense of how common, likely, and important things are is built from *whatever comes to mind first* — and what comes to mind is set by vividness, recency, distinctiveness, emotional charge, and self-relevance, not by actual frequency. Whoever curates what's salient in your field has chosen the sample your mind will mistake for the world.

## The lever

**Retrievability → judgment.** The **availability heuristic**: people estimate frequency and probability by the ease with which instances come to mind — in Tversky & Kahneman's lists, the gender with more famous names was judged more numerous [1]. The strong real-world leg is risk distortion: dramatic causes of death (homicide, tornado) are overestimated and quiet ones (diabetes, asthma) underestimated, tracking news coverage rather than mortality tables [2]. Honesty requires splitting the construct: the celebrated **ease-of-retrieval** paradigm (Schwarz 1991 — listing 12 examples of your own assertiveness feels hard, so you rate yourself less assertive) is *contested*: large direct replications were null, and the supportive meta-analysis concedes publication bias may cut the effect by a third [3]. The observer-side cousin is the **frequency illusion** (folk grade): once you notice or name a thing, you see it "everywhere" — Zwicky's 2005 coinage, analyzed as selective attention plus confirmation bias, with essentially no direct experimental literature of its own [4]. The **Baader-Meinhof phenomenon** is the same illusion under its older folk name (coined 1994 by a newspaper-letter writer, a decade *before* Zwicky); register the synonym, nothing more [4].

**Salience → attention and memory.** What grabs attention gets encoded, what got encoded comes to mind first, and what comes to mind first feels frequent — the loop that joins the clusters. **Attentional bias**: threat and emotionally salient stimuli capture attention preferentially — real at group level (d ≈ 0.45 in anxious samples) [5], but resting on a broken instrument: dot-probe "bias scores" showed no internal reliability above zero across 36 task variants in 9,600 participants [6]. The memory half is one principle wearing four codex names — *distinctiveness against a backdrop*. The parent is the **Von Restorff effect** (isolation effect, 1933): the item that differs from its list is disproportionately remembered — nine decades of replication; Hunt's corrective: difference-in-context driving retrieval, not mere pop-out [7]. Its children: the **bizarreness effect** — bizarre items beat common ones *only* in mixed lists, chiefly in free recall, and at the neighbors' expense [8]; the **humor effect** — funny items likewise win recall in mixed lists [9], with a separate, better-evidenced persuasion lever: humor occupies exactly the processing that would have generated counterarguments while transferring positive affect — and measurably *reduces* source credibility [10]; and the **picture superiority effect** — pictures are remembered better than words (recognition of thousands of images near ceiling), with dual-coding and distinctiveness accounts still contesting the mechanism [11]. Its exploitation-relevant sequel: a merely decorative, non-probative photo inflates belief in the accompanying claim ("truthiness") — a small, conditional effect [12].

**Two amplifiers pick the winners.** The **self-relevance effect** (self-reference effect): material encoded about *you* beats semantic and other-referent encoding — a 129-study meta; no magic self-module, just everyone's best-elaborated schema doing ordinary depth-of-processing work unusually reliably [13]. And the **negativity bias**: bad is stronger than good — more weight, attention, processing, and memory for negative information, present in infancy and physiologically confirmed across 17 countries [14][15]; in field data, each additional negative word in a headline raised click-through ~2.3% while positive words *lowered* it [15]. (Codex housekeeping: negativity bias is the Cognitive Bias Codex's only dual-listed entry — Q1.2 *and* Q4.2 — and this file is its single KB home; see [[bias-codex-index]].)

The manipulation-relevant chain: salience buys attention → attention buys memory → memory buys judged frequency and importance → judged frequency buys fear or desire. Each link is separately supported; the chain as deployed in fear-led marketing is ecological extrapolation, and this file labels it as such.

## Evidence base

- **Availability heuristic — established** (core retrievability effect; media-tracking risk misestimation) [1][2], with two honest dents: the classic letter-position demo never replicated (frequency estimates were regressed but roughly accurate), and part of the classic over/underestimation pattern is regression to the mean [2]. The **ease-of-retrieval** felt-ease mediator — *contested* [3].
- **Attentional bias — supported at group level** [5]; converging paradigms (weapon focus, emotion-induced blindness) survive their individual critiques. Individual measurement — *contested*: near-zero dot-probe reliability, and no baseline dot-probe bias even in clinically anxious RCT samples [6].
- **Frequency illusion / Baader-Meinhof — folk**: near-universally reported, components (selective attention, confirmation bias) separately established, but no dedicated experimental literature under the compound name [4].
- **Bizarreness effect — supported, moderator-dependent**: mixed lists, mainly free recall, zero-sum against neighbors; "it's not surprising, it's complex" is the original authors' own verdict [8].
- **Humor effect — supported**: the memory side is mixed-list-dependent like bizarreness [9]; the counterargument-disarming side has direct experimental and meta-analytic support, including the credibility penalty [10]. No failed-replication scandal — but no modern registered-replication stress test either.
- **Von Restorff effect — established**: ~90 years of replication across materials, ages, and paradigms [7].
- **Picture superiority — established** as a phenomenon (though no single canonical meta-analysis exists — a gap worth naming); mechanism contested (dual coding vs conceptual distinctiveness); attenuates at very fast presentation and in young children [11]. Truthiness is a separate, small belief effect with published boundary nulls [12].
- **Self-relevance effect — established**: d ≈ 0.5–0.65 depending on comparison; shrinks against intimate-other encoding, and is culturally moderated — under interdependent self-construals the self-schema takes in close others, so in Chinese samples mother-reference behaves like self-reference (shared neural signature; the encoding advantage extends to close others) [13].
- **Negativity bias — established** for the core asymmetry [14][15], but not lifespan-universal: in older adults, attention and memory shift toward *positive* material (the age-related positivity effect, meta-analytically supported) — worth remembering wherever [[elder-targeting]] is assumed to run on fear alone [14]. Its application claims need fences: fear appeals are effective but modest (d ≈ 0.29), one-vivid-anecdote persuasion is small (identifiable-victim meta r ≈ .05; that entry's codex home is [[probability-blindspots]]), and negative political campaigning is more memorable but *not* more persuasive [16]. Construct critiques — what "stronger" means, and diagnosticity confounds — are real [17].

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **a curated sample of your attention presented as the state of the world.**

- **Fear-led selling and insurance scares** — one vivid anecdote deployed *in place of* a rate ("just last week a customer who skipped the coverage lost everything"); pitches timed to the post-disaster window while the memory is fresh [2]; scareware manufactures the vivid instance outright — fake infection alerts and rigged "scans" ($163M FTC judgment; tech-support fraud ≈ $1.46B in 2024) [18] ([[fear-exploitation]] owns the threat-rescuer structure this feeds; [[manufactured-urgency]] compresses the clock; [[elder-targeting]] concentrates the harm).
- **Atrocity propaganda and availability entrepreneurs** — a single vivid incident replayed until the felt base rate detaches from the statistics entirely (probability neglect); "availability entrepreneurs" engineer which examples a population can retrieve [19] ([[propaganda-devices]]; [[fud-competitor-tactics]] is the commercial version — vague risk made mentally available around a rival).
- **Doom feeds and engagement farming** — negativity monetized: negative headline words causally raise clicks, positive words lower them [15]; reaction-weighted ranking has amplified alarm content — Facebook scored all five emoji reactions, anger included, at five times a like from 2017 until internal research correlating anger-heavy content with misinformation led it to cut the anger weight to zero in 2020 [21] ([[dark-patterns-social-urgency]] runs the adjacent interface plays). [[smear-campaigns]] ride the same asymmetry — calibrated: negative material reliably wins attention and memory, not automatically persuasion [16].
- **Salience-engineered choice architecture** — the one glowing "MOST POPULAR" tier among grey siblings; alarm-styled banners and siren thumbnails: the Von Restorff effect applied by a counterparty — what stands out was chosen for their margin, not your interest [7] ([[advertising-marketing]]; [[framing-anchoring]] owns the comparison-set cousin).
- **The joke that swallows the objection** — the claim delivered inside comedy so scrutiny relaxes; you can retell the joke but not the terms; attack content laundered as memes with "it's just a joke" as the exit; interpersonal put-downs wearing the same disguise [10]. A mundane feed is the mixed-list condition of everyday life: expect the weird bits, not the terms, to be what you remember [8].
- **Imagery-heavy scam material** — fake check photos, doctored profit screenshots, deepfake endorsements, one wrenching charity image: the picture persists after the caption's hedges are forgotten, and a non-probative photo inflates felt truth [11][12] ([[ai-enabled-manipulation]], [[disinformation-playbooks]]).
- **Weaponized personalization** — spear-phishing success rose from 16% to 72% when the same lure merely appeared to come from a known friend [20]; sucker lists, romance-scam mirroring, and breached-password "proof" in extortion mail all use accurate self-relevant detail to buy encoding depth and felt credibility at once [13][18] ([[cold-reading]] harvests the lever live, seeding details the sitter encodes as uncanny).
- **Seeded noticing** — "once you see it, you'll start seeing it everywhere": recruiters, conspiracy pitches, and psychics plant a frame and let the target's frequency illusion deliver confirming sightings free of charge ([[cult-conversion-funnel]]'s early "signs" stage). Retargeted ads exploit the same misread — manufactured repetition experienced as organic ubiquity. The "my phone is listening" experience is the illusion's flagship case: a 17,260-app audit found no covert audio exfiltration — it did find covert *screen* recording; the real tracking is duller and worse [20].

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Salience is how honest communication gets through: warning labels, exit signs, vivid teaching examples, a doctor's advice tailored to your history, public-health fear appeals with real efficacy paths [16] — all pull these levers *for* the audience. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **substitutes the vivid for the true** (anecdote systematically deployed in place of available statistics, with a transaction hanging on the resulting fear), **personalizes asymmetrically** (they know you, you can't verify them, and an extraction follows), **lets imagery stand in for checkable evidence**, or **engineers distinctiveness against the chooser's interest**. The disclosure test: an honest communicator survives "what's the base rate?" and can say why this example, this image, this highlight.

## Recognition

- **You can cite a story but not a rate** — your fear tracks how recently you heard the anecdote, not any number you could name.
- **Typical phrases:** "This happened to a family just like yours." "You've seen the news — do you really want to take that chance?" "Everyone's talking about it." "I keep seeing it everywhere — it can't be coincidence." "Lighten up, it's just a joke." "As someone who [your trait]…" — mail-merge intimacy.
- **Packaging outruns content** — alarm-styled subject lines, siren thumbnails, weirdness out of proportion to information; the funny or shocking bits are the only bits you can recall afterward, and the fine print is what they displaced.
- **One option jumps out and the rest blur** — afterwards you can describe only the highlighted one, and the choice felt obvious without your having compared anything.
- **The message feels written for you** — name, town, recent purchase quoted back; the jolt of being known is doing the persuading.
- **Felt-sense indicators:** "I couldn't look away"; "I can't get that ad out of my head"; one criticism outweighing ten compliments for hours; the world feeling statistically worse after a scroll session than any figure you could cite; coincidences feeling aimed at you.

## Resistance

1. **Ask for the denominator** ([[verification-rituals]]): "how often, out of how many?" — and get the base rate from a party who earns nothing from your fear. A story offered where a rate was requested, or irritation at the question, is itself the disclosure ([[detection-heuristics]]).
2. **Strip the salience, then re-read**: restate the joke-wrapped or image-wrapped claim in flat text and evaluate *that*; re-read choice screens as a plain list with the styling ignored; reverse-image-search the photos; treat screenshots of balances and results as zero evidence.
3. **Date-stamp the noticing**: distinguish "I see it more" from "it happens more" — note when you first noticed versus when it could plausibly have started. A few sightings a week is what ordinary coincidence looks like across thousands of daily perceptions.
4. **Price personalization at zero**: accurate personal detail is cheap (data brokers, scraped profiles, breaches) — "they know me" is not evidence of legitimacy. Verify identities out-of-band, on channels you found yourself.
5. **Decide outside the vividness window**: give it 24 hours — genuine risks survive the wait; manufactured ones depend on your deciding now. Make no purchases and no commitments inside the fear window.
6. **Audit the diet, not just the item**: a source whose output is uniformly alarming is making an editorial choice — real risk landscapes are mixed. Notice your own click gradient too: negativity is partly demand-side [15].

## Caveats

- **Availability is often calibration, not bias.** Retrievability tracks real frequency well for local, frequently experienced events — that is why the heuristic exists [1]. Judging from a genuinely common, recent local risk is calibration: buying flood insurance after a flood can be *correct* updating, and because people underweight rare risks known only from experience, a vivid warning can fix an underestimate (seatbelts, smoke alarms, backups) rather than exploit one. Vivid recent events sometimes *are* representative.
- **Threat vigilance is the system working** — eyes snapping to a genuine fraud alert or a real diagnosis is adaptation, not defect [5].
- **Negative weighting is often rational** — negative acts are frequently more *diagnostic* than positive ones (one betrayal reveals more than one kindness) [17]; a single credible allegation about a babysitter *should* outweigh many pleasant interactions. Whistleblowers, auditors, and honest critics are negative by role.
- **Base rates: most salience is innocent.** Vivid stories are usually sincere experience-sharing; salience-seeking is the entry fee of any attention market, and almost all of it is ordinary marketing, art, or humor, not fraud. The markers are *pairings*: anecdote-for-statistic plus a transaction on the fear; personalization plus unverifiable identity; imagery plus no checkable substance ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).
- **Measurement humility.** Never attribute an "attentional bias" to a specific individual — the instruments cannot support it [6]. And keep severity proportional: fear appeals d ≈ 0.29, identifiable-victim r ≈ .05, negative campaigning not more persuasive [16] — these are levers, not mind control.
- **Bias names are themselves weaponizable.** "That's just your negativity bias" and "you're imagining it — classic Baader-Meinhof" can be used to override specific, checkable claims — reality-denial wearing a psychology citation ([[gaslighting]]'s pattern). Never explain away a stalking or harassment target's "I keep seeing the same person" with the frequency illusion: stalkers appear everywhere *by design*; the right response is documentation ([[documentation-practices]]) — and if surveillance through devices is a live possibility, [[stalkerware-monitoring]] carries the safety-flagged guidance — not bias-naming. Name a bias only when the input is vivid-but-nondiagnostic, never to dismiss specifics.
- **Seam with [[fluency-familiarity]]** (the declared distinguished-from): this file owns *what comes to mind first and what grabs attention* — noticing; fluency-familiarity owns *what feels true because it processes easily* — believing-because-easy (illusory truth, mere exposure). The contested ease-of-retrieval paradigm sits exactly on that seam [3]. Other boundaries: [[fear-exploitation]] owns the hot channel (threat-and-relief structure, the threatener-as-rescuer); this file owns the cold channel (what vivid threat does to attention and felt base rates). Framing-anchoring owns reference-point *placement*; this file owns which instances are retrievable at all.
