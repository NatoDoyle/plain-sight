# Choice Simplification

> Evaluating an option properly is expensive, so the mind answers a cheaper question instead — *how good is this for its type*, *how much is a portion*, *was I thorough* — and hands back the answer as if it were the expensive one. The substitution is usually right, which is why it is invisible when it is wrong, and why whoever chose the frame you are evaluating in has already done most of the deciding. Eight codex rows land here; **two of them carry the file**, and the tally below says what the other six are rather than filling the slots.

## The lever

**A cheap attribute substituted for an important one.** The one properly-sourced spine in this cluster is Hsee's **evaluability hypothesis**: separate evaluations of objects "are often influenced by attributes which are easy to evaluate rather than by those which are important" [1]. Fullness, completeness, tidiness, roundness, category-position and thoroughness are all instantly judgeable; unit price, total quantity, terms, and whether a result would change anything are not. When the easy attribute is correlated with the important one — which it usually is — the substitution costs nothing, and that is the reason it exists. The manipulation-relevant fact is narrower and structural: **the substitution is set by the evaluation mode, and the evaluation mode is usually the counterparty's choice.** How many options you see, what counts as one serving, what counts as enough checking, and what gets discussed first are all frame decisions, and none of them is yours by default.

The honest tally, stated up front because this cluster does not survive being presented as eight comparable "biases." **One** member has effect-level evidence up to Cochrane standard and generalizes plausibly into pricing (unit bias). **One** is a strong, well-cited original whose headline operationalization survived a large multi-lab direct replication, but whose dominance-reversal demonstration did **not** replicate in separate evaluation (less-is-better). **One** is a small, old, single-programme literature that nonetheless yields the best one-sentence defensive test in the file — and whose label is weaponizable against people doing due diligence (information bias). **One** is established cognitive science and flatly not a manipulation lever (functional fixedness). **Two rows covering one concept** are a 1957 satirical anecdote with no located empirical test (bike-shedding / Law of Triviality). **One** returned zero hits across three independent indexes (the Delmore effect). **One is not a bias** — it is a normative principle of theory choice that the codex miscategorized (Occam's razor). A KB that manufactured a manipulation story for all eight would be doing precisely the laundering [[epistemic-guardrails]] exists to forbid.

**Less-is-better effect.** Hsee's three demonstrations, all in *separate* evaluation: a person giving a **$45 scarf** was judged more generous than one giving a **$55 coat**; an **overfilled 7 oz** ice cream serving was valued above an **underfilled 8 oz** serving; and a **24-piece intact dinnerware set** was rated above a **31-piece set containing the same 24 pieces plus extras plus a few broken ones** [1]. The dinnerware case is the structurally sharpest of the three, because option B *strictly dominates* option A — it contains everything A contains and more — and still loses; it is also, as it turns out, the one that failed to replicate. The boundary condition is the entire finding and must never be dropped: the effect "occurred only when the options were evaluated separately, and reversed itself when the options were juxtaposed" [1]. This is a **presentation-mode effect, not a preference** — nobody actually wants fewer plates. **Replication splits by stimulus, and the split matters.** The scarf/coat operationalization holds: it replicated in a Polish study of fifteen JDM effects [3], and less-is-better was among the 28 findings carried by **Many Labs 2**, a preregistered multi-lab replication across 125 samples and 15,305 participants in 36 countries and territories, where it is reported as replicating [19]. The **dinnerware** operationalization does not: the same Polish study reports that "the second operationalization of the 'less-is-better' was only partially successful (Hsee, 1998): we found a significant effect in the joint evaluation condition but not when the items were evaluated separately" [3] — which is the **reverse** of Hsee's own boundary condition, and it is precisely the dominance-reversal demonstration that failed. Grade `supported`: strong original, three demonstrations, a large multi-lab direct replication of the headline effect, but a failed separate-evaluation replication of the dominance reversal and at least one live rival account (see Evidence base). **Seam, declared here and not re-argued:** [[framing-anchoring]] owns distinction bias, the joint/separate machinery, decoys and tier grids — the seller engineering *joint* evaluation to sell a premium on differences you will never feel once you own only one thing. This file owns the mirror direction: the seller engineering *separate* evaluation so that a dominated option can win. Same mechanism, opposite bend, and **which mode is being manufactured tells you which way the pitch is leaning.**

**Information bias.** First, the terminological collision, because it will otherwise wreck every retrieval: in epidemiology and clinical research "information bias" means **systematic measurement or misclassification error** — a completely different construct, and the one that dominates any medical corpus search. The decision-theoretic sense meant here is Baron's: valuing or seeking information that **cannot change the decision**, where no possible result of the test or inquiry would alter the action taken [5]. The evidence is thin and the file will not pretend otherwise — two 1988 papers from one research group in a diagnostic-reasoning paradigm, a modest citation footprint, no located replication programme, no effect size, and abstracts the sweeps could not retrieve [5]. What the entry is genuinely worth is one operational test, which follows directly from the definition and needs no further evidence to be useful: **name in advance what you would do differently under each possible result. If every branch leads to the same action, the search is not information-gathering.** Two applied readings, both explicitly labeled: *diagnostic-test overuse* is real but its causes are mostly structural — liability exposure, fee-for-service incentives, patient expectation, ordering-by-panel — and this file does not claim a cognitive bias explains it [5][6]. *"Just one more check" as stalling* is the KB-relevant shape and it is an **extrapolation**, not a finding: compulsive checking is far better characterized by the reassurance-seeking literature, where the mechanism is anxiety maintenance through negative reinforcement and the counter is response prevention, not "notice the information is useless" *(routing note — no citation retrieved this sweep)*. The better-evidenced neighbour runs the other way entirely — deliberate **information avoidance**, the refusal of information people expect to find unwelcome, has a substantially larger literature than information bias does [6].

**Unit bias.** Geier, Rozin and Doros's claim is that "a unit of some entity (with certain constraints) is the appropriate and optimal amount" — the served unit read as the correct amount rather than as one available quantity [7]. Their design is what makes it strong: participants took **much greater weights of Tootsie Rolls and pretzels when offered a large rather than a small unit size, and substantially more M&M's with a large rather than a small spoon**, while being free to take as many units as they liked **at no cost** [7]. The unit was not a constraint. It was a suggestion, and the suggestion won. Magnitude, and this is the best-evidenced number in the file: a Cochrane review found that **people consistently consume more when offered larger portions, packages or tableware**, with a small-to-moderate effect on *selection* (SMD 0.42, 95% CI 0.24–0.59, 13 comparisons from 10 studies, 1,164 participants, moderate-quality evidence), present in adults (SMD 0.55, CI 0.35–0.75) but **not in children** (SMD 0.14, CI −0.06 to 0.34), and estimating that eliminating larger portions could cut **144–228 kcal per day (8.5–13.5% of a 1,689 kcal baseline)** among UK children and adults [8]. A separate meta puts it at **+35% consumption for a doubling of portion size**, explicitly **curvilinear** (diminishing as portions grow), robust across settings, and **weaker among children, women, and overweight individuals, for non-snack items, and where more attention is paid to the food** [9]. Two grades, kept separate because they are separate claims: the **portion/package/tableware size effect is `established`**; **unit bias as the *explanation* of it is `supported` and mechanistically contested** — a competing segmentation account is live enough to be a paper title, presenting the same quantity in multiple smaller units raises expected satiety in a direction a simple unit-as-norm story does not predict, and merely providing take-away containers substantially attenuated the effect [10]. Note also that the authors' own generalization — "unit bias is a general feature in human choice" — is an argument they make, not a finding they report [7], which matters for everything in the next section about tiers and bundles.

**Functional fixedness.** Duncker's candle problem: given a candle, matches, and a box of thumbtacks, few people see the box itself as a shelf to tack to the wall [11]. Adamson's 1952 direct replication is a rare thing to be able to cite for a 1945 result, and it isolates the manipulation — presenting the box **full** (Adamson's "preutilization") makes participants less likely to consider it for any other use, while presenting it empty improves solving [11]. The construct then does everything a construct should: it has a developmental boundary (five-year-olds do not show it; by about seven, children treat an object's intended purpose as special) and it generalizes cross-culturally, appearing among the **Shuar of Ecuadorian Amazonia**, whose material environment contains few specialized artifacts — from which the authors conclude that "design function may universally be the core property of artifact concepts in human semantic memory" [12]. Grade `established`, and distinguish it from the *Einstellung* effect (Luchins's water-jar problem), which fixes a primed **procedure** rather than a primed **object function** [11]. **And then say the honest thing plainly: this is not a manipulation lever.** No literature connects it to influence, compliance, or deception. Nobody extracts money or consent from you by priming an object's design function; a manipulator cannot induce it, aim it, or profit from it the way they can aim urgency or reciprocity. At most they decline to relieve it. Its real value here is **defense-side and design-side**: fixedness is a plausible reason a target cannot spontaneously generate their own option list, which is an argument for a helper *enumerating* resources by what they can do rather than asking "so what could you do?" — see Resistance. The tempting extension to *role* fixedness ("he's my husband," "HR is for paperwork," "the joint account is for bills") is an **analogy to the artifact literature, not an application of it**; no study tests it, this file labels it as an analogy, and the KB's real coverage of not-being-able-to-see-the-option lives in [[double-binds]] and [[no-contact-exit-planning]].

**Bike-shedding effect / Law of Triviality.** Two codex rows for one idea, and the idea is a joke — in the technical sense that its source is a joke. Parkinson's 1957 committee approves a £10m nuclear reactor in two and a half minutes and then spends forty-five minutes on a £350 bicycle shed; the committee, the members, and every figure in the anecdote are **fictional**, published in a book of humorous administrative essays, with no data offered and none claimed [13]. Four independent index searches for an empirical base returned nothing: Europe PMC's handful of exact-phrase hits are all rhetorical asides inside commentary pieces, Crossref returns no empirical study, Semantic Scholar returns zero, and OpenAlex's single "bikeshedding" title is a zero-citation magazine commentary [14]. A standard group-dynamics textbook asserts that behavioural research supports the law without detailing any original study [14]. **Grade `folk`, and state the reason: no direct empirical test was located.** What is real, and is the reason the row stays in the file at all, is the *procedural* pattern — **agenda burial**, where a consequential item is placed behind or beneath an item guaranteed to consume the room. That tactic is observable without any psychology, and the better-evidenced literature for it is agenda control and hidden profiles, not Parkinson: experimental and field demonstrations that agenda structure determines committee outcomes, and the shared-information bias by which groups disproportionately discuss what members already hold in common and under-discuss uniquely held information [15]. Both of those citations were flagged unverified by the sweeps and are printed with that hedge [15]. The most principled *explanation* on offer for why trivial items absorb meetings is in fact this file's own spine — trivial items are **easy to evaluate**, so everyone can form an opinion instantly [1] — and that is an inference, clearly labeled, not a tested claim.

**Delmore effect.** The claim is that people articulate goals more clearly for their *less* important tasks. The KB could not find a research base for it. OpenAlex title search: zero results. Crossref: zero relevant results — the hits are the poet Delmore Schwartz and the country-musician Delmore Brothers. Europe PMC exact phrase: zero hits [16]. **No originating paper, no named researcher, no paradigm, no measurement, no secondary review** was located by any of the four sweeps. Grade `folk`, and note the precise wording this file uses: not "under-researched," which would imply a small literature exists, but *none was found*. Nothing was manufactured to fill the row, and no manipulation relevance is claimed. If the underlying intuition is worth keeping, the honest substitutes are properly-evidenced constructs that say something adjacent but different — goal-setting theory on the superiority of specific over vague goals, and construal-level theory, under which psychologically distant and abstract objectives are *represented* more abstractly and therefore **sound** vaguer, which is not a claim that anyone avoids specifying what matters [17].

**Occam's razor.** Not a bias. *Entia non sunt multiplicanda praeter necessitatem* is a **normative principle of theory choice** — among hypotheses that account for the evidence equally well, prefer the one with fewer unsupported assumptions — with a long philosophy-of-science literature and formal descendants in model selection (AIC, BIC, minimum description length, Bayesian Occam factors) [18]. A cognitive bias is a systematic deviation *from* a normative standard; the razor **is** part of the standard. Its presence in a bias list is a category error, and it is the single clearest instance in the whole codex of an entry that does not belong, which is why this file names the error rather than reproducing it. Relevance: **not-a-lever**, and no `evidence:` grade is assigned to a normative principle. Two adjacent things are real and are routed rather than annexed. First, parsimony is not always truth-tracking — in phylogenetics, maximum parsimony is a statistically inconsistent estimator under certain branch-length conditions, converging on the *wrong* tree as data accumulate (long-branch attraction) [18]; simple explanations have documented failure regimes even as principles. Second, the genuinely bias-shaped neighbour is an **explanatory simplicity preference** — people over-weighting simplicity relative to probability, accepting a one-cause story over a two-cause one even where base rates favour the latter [18] — and that lever already has homes: [[certainty-needs]] owns the hunger for a clean total answer, [[pattern-illusions]] owns explanatory pattern-seeking, and [[probability-blindspots]] owns the base-rate half of the same argument. What belongs to *this* file is one sentence about the **misuse** of the razor, in How it's weaponized.

## Evidence base

The file grade is `supported`, and it reflects the cluster's two load-bearing members (less-is-better and unit bias) rather than an average across eight rows. Members carry their own grades. Sorted honestly:

- **Portion/package/tableware size effect — `established`.** Cochrane review with authors' conclusions stated as consistency across studies, moderate-quality evidence for selection (SMD 0.42), an adult/child split with children's CI crossing zero, and a quantified population estimate [8]; independent meta-analytic magnitude at +35% per doubling with curvilinearity and a moderator map [9]; a 2025 independent confirmation in a different country, food and lab [10]. Carry the reviewers' own limits: the evidence concerns **immediate and short-term** selection and consumption, it is uncertain whether reducing portions at the *smaller* end of the range works as well, they could not draw clear implications for alcohol or tobacco, and cigarette length showed no difference on low-quality evidence [8]. It does not establish long-run weight or spending outcomes, and this file does not claim it does.
- **Unit bias as the mechanism — `supported`, contested.** The heuristic account is one of several: a direct competing test is titled "unit bias or segmentation effect?", presenting the same food in multiple smaller units *raises* expected satiety, take-away containers substantially attenuate the effect, and single-unit foods are estimated with greater error than multi-unit ones [7][10]. Effect-level evidence far outstrips mechanism-level evidence, and the two grades are kept apart deliberately. One lineage hygiene note: parts of the well-known food-cue literature adjacent to this area have been retracted, and neither the Cochrane review nor Geier et al. depends on that lineage — the file cites neither it nor any figure derived from it [10].
- **Less-is-better — `supported`, and the replication picture is split by stimulus.** The parent joint/separate-evaluation reversal is among the better-replicated effects in judgment and decision making [2]; the specific less-is-better demonstrations are single-lab, hypothetical willingness-to-pay, no incentives. The Polish multi-effect conceptual replication resolves it by operationalization rather than as one verdict: the **scarf/coat** version was among the seven successfully replicated, while the **dinnerware set** version (Hsee's C14) was only *partial* — verbatim, "we found a significant effect in the joint evaluation condition but not when the items were evaluated separately" [3]. That is the reverse of the boundary condition the effect is usually quoted for, and it lands on the exact stimulus this file's exploitation reading leans on — see the concession in The lever. Note also that less-is-better replicated in the Many Labs 2 multi-lab project [19], which is a stronger evidential base than "single-lab" implies and is why the row holds at `supported` rather than dropping: the *phenomenon* has multi-lab support, while the *dominance-reversal stimulus* has a failed separate-evaluation direct replication. A live rival account also exists and cuts against calling the effect an error at all: in several of Hsee's stimuli, "less" carries a genuine signal about the reference class — an intact set signals quality control, a full small container signals abundance — so preferring it under separate evaluation may be rational inference from the only cue available [3]. Convergent oddity worth one line: less-is-better has been reported in **capuchin monkeys**, whose authors propose a *contrast-like* account rather than evaluability, which strengthens the phenomenon and weakens the monopoly of the explanation [4].
- **Information bias — `supported` for the lab demonstration only, and thin.** One research programme, 1988, diagnostic-reasoning vignettes, ~83–125 citations depending on index, no located modern or preregistered replication, no effect size, abstracts unretrievable this sweep [5]. Three skeptical corrections belong in the entry rather than in a footnote. (1) "Useless" is **model-dependent**: whether a test can change a decision depends on the decision model the experimenter assumed, and a decision-maker uncertain about their own priors may reasonably want a result that bears on whether those priors are right. (2) Information has legitimate **non-instrumental** value — reassurance, planning, closure, the ability to justify a choice to others — which belief-based-utility accounts treat as a preference rather than a mistake; calling it a bias inherits a normative commitment the file should disclose [6]. (3) Both real-world applications the codex invites are **over-attributions**: test overuse has stronger structural drivers [5], and "just one more check" is better characterized by reassurance-seeking *(routing note — no citation retrieved this sweep)*.
- **Functional fixedness — `established`, and `not-a-lever`.** 1945 original, 1952 direct replication with an isolated manipulation, developmental boundary, cross-cultural generalization, and a working debiasing intervention [11][12]. One magnitude figure surfaced by a single sweep — roughly a quarter of participants solving a written version initially, rising to about half under representation-focused interventions — is carried as approximate and single-sourced [12]. This is the strongest-evidenced entry in the cluster and has the least manipulation relevance, which is a useful pairing to have on the record.
- **Bike-shedding / Law of Triviality — `folk`, no research base located.** Satirical origin with invented figures [13]; four negative index searches, with the only academic returns being rhetorical citations and an opinion column [14]. The *tactic* (agenda burial) is real and is grounded in agenda-control and hidden-profile work instead — both flagged unverified by the sweeps and printed with that hedge [15].
- **Delmore effect — `folk`, and the KB states that it could not find a research base at all** [16]. Zero hits across OpenAlex, Crossref and Europe PMC. Treat as plausibly apocryphal.
- **Occam's razor — no grade; not a bias** [18]. A normative principle miscategorized by the codex. The real nearby lever (explanatory simplicity preference) is cited with an explicit verification hedge and routed to [[certainty-needs]] and [[pattern-illusions]] rather than claimed here [18].
- **Applied bridges, labeled.** Subscription tiers, token and credit bundles, minimum order thresholds and billing increments are **this KB's extension of unit bias by analogy**; the studies measured food, drink and tableware [7][8][9]. Role fixedness is an analogy to the artifact literature, not an application [11]. The evaluability explanation of bike-shedding is inference [1]. Each is labeled at the point of use.

## How it's weaponized

Start with the negatives by name, because they are a third of the cluster. **Functional fixedness, the Delmore effect, and Occam's razor are not manipulation levers.** Nobody is going to work you by priming an object's design function, by exploiting a construct with no located research base, or by your preferring parsimonious theories. Two tempting inferences are wrong and the file refuses both: a manipulator does not *cause* your inability to see an option — they benefit passively from resources being presented only in their conventional roles, which is a routing note, not a mechanism; and "the simplest explanation" being deployed against you is a rhetorical move, not you exhibiting a bias.

What remains is real and concentrates in one place: **whoever chose the mode you are evaluating in.**

- **Engineered separate evaluation.** You are shown exactly one option, and the impressive attribute is the one you can judge in a second while the important one requires arithmetic you have not done. The dinnerware structure is the tell in its purest form — something has been **removed** to make an offer look cleaner, and the removal is presented as refinement [1]. One concession, because it lands on this exact stimulus: the dinnerware dominance-reversal demonstration has a *failed separate-evaluation direct replication* — the conceptual replication found the effect in joint and not in separate evaluation, the reverse of the boundary condition it is usually quoted for [3]. The exploitation reading here is therefore carried by the **mechanism** (evaluability: whoever sets the mode sets which attribute you can judge), which has multi-lab support [2][19], and not by that stimulus. Treat the dinnerware case as the clearest *illustration* of the shape, not as its evidential base. Observable forms: the single quote with no comparator, the "this is the package for someone like you" close, the broker who shows one property per viewing, the site that resists a side-by-side of its own products, the proposal whose visible virtue is tidiness. Requests to compare meet social friction rather than a refusal: *"they're all much the same," "you don't want to be shopping around," "I've already narrowed it down for you."* [[framing-anchoring]] owns the opposite engineering — the spec-sheet showroom, the trim ladder, the decoy grid — and the pair is the point: **the mode is not the tell; who chose it is.**
- **Whoever sets the unit sets the amount.** The documented anchor is food, drink and tableware [7][8][9]. The KB's extension, labeled as such, is everywhere a vendor controls granularity: subscription tiers where the middle one reads as the normal one, credit and token bundles denominated in an internal currency with no printed exchange rate to money, "packs" and multi-buys, minimum order values and free-delivery thresholds, twelve-month minimum terms, class packs and session blocks, and the professional billing increment. The signature to recognize is that **your actual requirement is never elicited** — nobody asks how many seats, gigabytes or sessions you need; you are shown units and asked which. The decoy/compromise half of tier grids belongs to [[framing-anchoring]] and [[thats-not-all-decoys]]; route it rather than re-deriving it here.
- **"Thoroughness" as a stall.** Information-gathering kept nominally open so the decision it precedes never has to be made — the institutional way a complaint dies without anyone refusing it, and the interpersonal *"let's not decide anything until we've talked to X,"* indefinitely renewed. The tell is asymmetry: the person whose conduct is at issue is the one requiring more information, the bar for "enough" moves each time you meet it, and what is demanded is unobtainable in principle (proof of what someone was thinking, certainty it will work out). Related and distinct: the **disclosure-overload** mirror, where a counterparty supplies an endless stream of decision-irrelevant "context" so that diligence *feels* discharged while the decision-relevant facts stay buried — that is [[cognitive-overload-confusion]]'s territory and it routes there, with [[information-asymmetry]] owning the selective-disclosure half.
- **Selling the check.** A test, audit, report or verification step recommended by the party who is paid for it, whose result would not change what happens next — background-check upsells, "assessments" nobody acts on, and the paid "verification" stages of advance-fee scams, which exist to extract a fee *and* to manufacture the feeling of due diligence ([[scams-fraud]], [[phishing-pretexting]]). The two-branch question defeats the whole family in one sentence, and it is in Resistance.
- **Agenda burial.** The consequential item scheduled last, or immediately after an item designed to consume the room; papers circulated too late to read; the material clause in schedule 4; a spend or policy change tabled "for noting" beneath something everyone has an opinion about; approval arriving at the end of a long session from a tired room. The depletion half of that belongs to [[cognitive-overload-confusion]] and the clock half to [[manufactured-urgency]]; what this file adds is the evaluability reason it works — the trivial item is the one everyone can evaluate [1] — clearly labeled as inference, with the bike-shed *name* carrying no evidential weight at all [13][14]. One occurrence is a bad meeting. The pattern is the finding, and the question that separates them is **who consistently benefits from it**.
- **The razor pointed backwards.** *"The simplest explanation is that you're overreacting."* Most invocations of simplicity are sincere and usually right — the tell is not the invocation but whether the simple story covers the documented specifics. This is the misuse and it is genuinely common: the razor adjudicates only between explanations that fit the evidence **equally well**, and "you're too sensitive" typically does not explain a documented pattern of specific dated incidents. An explanation that omits half the evidence is not simpler, it is worse. [[probability-blindspots]] already carries the structurally identical warning in the base-rate vocabulary — the base rate sets the prior, documented specifics are the evidence that moves it — and this file cross-references that sentence rather than deriving it twice. The counter is one line plus a record: *"simplest among explanations that fit — does yours fit all of it?"*, then [[documentation-practices]].

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Every one of these is a normal feature of commerce and conversation before it is anything else. Goods have to be sold in units; units make prices comparable, logistics possible, and planning feasible. Separate evaluation is the ordinary condition of life — you almost never get a side-by-side for a job, a flat, a doctor or a partner, and judging against category norms is the only available method, not an error. Agendas have to have an order. Wanting more information before committing is prudent and is this KB's own standing advice. And the unit lever is routinely used *for* people: the authors of the founding study frame small portions as an effective consumption control **because** of unit bias, which is also the logic of standard drink measures, default pack sizes and portion-controlled packaging [7][8].

The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]) is not the frame; it is **who set it and whether you may change it**. Exploitation *removes the other mode* (single-option presentation where a side-by-side would be trivial to provide, or a comparison grid where you will only ever live with one), *sets the unit before eliciting the requirement*, *keeps the information-gathering open while benefiting from the delay*, or *allocates meeting time inversely to consequence and calls it an agenda*. The disclosure test is unusually clean here: an honest counterparty can answer "can I see all the options on one page?", "what would you do differently if the result came back the other way?", "what is the price per unit?", and "can we take item 9 before item 4?" without irritation — and does not lose the deal when you take the frame apart.

## Recognition

These are prompts for suspicion, not diagnostics. Most of this cluster produces no distinctive felt sense at all, and the structural tells at the end are the actual test.

- **"For what it is, that's very good."** You can produce the judgment instantly and confidently without knowing the absolute value of anything. The question asked was *how much is this worth*; the question you answered was *how good is this for its type* — and the type was supplied to you, before the price.
- **The broken-plates feeling.** An offer containing something visibly flawed feels worse than the same offer with the flawed part removed, even though removing it removes value. Anything defective contaminates the whole [1].
- **You finish the unit.** Not "I want this much" but "this is *a* portion" — the packaging did the deciding and the decision never surfaced. Leftovers register as waste rather than restraint. In pricing: the middle tier feels *correct*, and you notice you never computed what you actually need.
- **Research that has stopped converging.** Early on, each new fact moved your view; now each one confirms what you already concluded and you keep going. You cannot name a result that would make you decide differently. The searching feels like diligence and functions like anxiety management — the reward is in the searching, not the finding. (Wanting more information before committing is usually prudent — this bullet is only about a search where you cannot name any result that would change what you do. It is never a reason to stop checking something you have not yet checked. See Caveats.)
- **You leave the meeting satisfied and cannot say what was decided about the large thing.** The room came alive at the trivial item; you finally had something to contribute, which was pleasant. The minutes later record the big item as "approved as circulated."
- **The stuck feeling that lifts the instant someone else says the obvious thing** — the phenomenology of fixedness, which is a limitation, not an attack, and says nothing about anyone's intentions.
- **Typical phrases from the other side:** "It's the top of the range." · "Most people in your position take the mid tier." · "We took out the features nobody uses." · "Comes as a pack of ten." · "Top up in 500-credit increments." · "This test will give you peace of mind." · "Have you *really* looked into it?" · "Let's not decide until we have all the facts." · "There's more you don't know yet." · "I think we can take that one as read." · "While we're all here, can we just settle…" · "You're overthinking it — the simple explanation is…"
- **Structural tells, which are the actual diagnostics.** Who chose whether you see one option or several, and does asking for the other mode meet friction? Can you state the price per underlying unit without arithmetic nobody offered you? Was your actual requirement ever elicited before you were shown units? Was something *removed* to make the offer look cleaner? For every check being demanded or sold: **what would be done differently under each possible result** — and does anyone benefit from the delay? On an agenda: is time allocated by consequence or exhausted by whatever the room can evaluate; does the item that matters arrive with a summary while the trivial one arrives with a discussion paper; and does the same person keep benefiting?
- **Escalation signs:** the bar for "enough information" moves each time you clear it; each completed check generates a necessary next one; timelines extend and nobody ever says no; the unit or tier you were placed on by default becomes the thing that has to be justified for you to change.

## Resistance

1. **Force the missing mode.** The effect "reversed itself when the options were juxtaposed" — that is the counter, taken verbatim from the source [1]. Get a second quote purely to restore comparison, put the shortlist on one page, open the alternatives in the same window. And apply it in reverse where you are being shown a spec grid you will never live inside ([[framing-anchoring]] owns that half).
2. **State the requirement before you see the unit.** How much do I actually need — seats, gigabytes, sessions, grams, months — written down *first*. This is the direct implication of the Geier design: the unit is a suggestion, and it works because you had no independent target [7].
3. **Convert everything to a denominator.** Price per unit, per month, per token, per gram, total cost of ownership. Evaluability failure is defeated by arithmetic, and a vendor making that arithmetic hard is telling you something.
4. **The two-branch question, before any check is bought or any delay accepted.** *"If it comes back A, what happens? If B, what happens?"* Two identical answers mean the check is not a decision input [5]. Ask it of the person recommending the test, and of yourself.
5. **Set the decision date before the information-gathering starts**, and decide in advance what evidence would settle it and what you will do if you cannot get it. This is the stopping rule the KB's own "verify independently" advice needs in order not to become paralysis ([[verification-rituals]], [[detection-heuristics]]).
6. **Read the agenda by money and irreversibility, not by order.** Rank the items yourself beforehand; ask on the record for the consequential one to be taken first; refuse to decide what you have not had time to read and get the refusal minuted. Anything "for noting" that spends money or changes a policy has been misfiled.
7. **Enumerate resources by what they can do, not by what they are for.** This is the one place functional fixedness earns its keep, and it is a *design* instruction for helpers as much as a self-help one: the debiasing that works is representation-focused prompting [12], so a friend, advocate or advisor should hand the person a concrete list of options rather than asking "what could you do?" — the whole content of fixedness is that they cannot generate the list themselves. Applies directly to exit planning ([[no-contact-exit-planning]], [[helping-others]]).
8. **Change the environment rather than your resolve.** The portion literature's own conclusion is that the effect is environmental, so the effective counters are environmental: smaller plates, decanted portions, buying the smaller bundle twice, turning off one-click top-ups [8].
9. **Make the "simple" explanation account for all of it.** Against the razor pointed backwards: produce the dated record and ask whether the tidy story covers the specifics ([[documentation-practices]]). Twelve specific incidents are not more simply explained by "you're sensitive"; that explanation is not simpler, it is shorter.

## Caveats

- **Simplification is mostly adaptive and it is not optional.** Nobody can evaluate every option on every dimension, and the substitutions in this file are usually right because easy attributes usually correlate with important ones. Judging against a reference class, buying in standard units, deferring to expertise on a technical item, and stopping the search somewhere are all how functional people function. A reader who leaves this file distrusting every unit, every category and every single-option decision has been made **worse** at deciding, not better.
- ***Ordinary-categorization* is the contrast, and it does most of the work here.** Sorting options into kinds and carrying expectations about them — "that's a good scarf," "that's a normal starter plan," "that's an entry-level model" — is the necessary cognition, not the error. The reference class is not the problem; **the reference class being supplied to you by the person selling into it, and being non-negotiable, is what makes it a lever.** "Compared to what?" is the whole test, and it is a question about who set the yardstick, not about whether yardsticks exist.
- **Wanting more information before deciding is usually prudent, and "information bias" is the most weaponizable label in this file.** This KB's own standing advice is to verify, document, check independently, sleep on it, and consult someone you trust. A person being pressured to decide fast will be told that their desire to check is a cognitive bias — and that inversion is exactly the shape of the pressure close ([[manufactured-urgency]], [[detection-heuristics]]). The construct is narrow: it concerns information that **cannot change the action**, not information that is merely slow, expensive, or unwelcome to the other party. Information also has real non-instrumental value — reassurance, planning, closure, a record you can produce later [6]. And wanting to understand something already decided, including one's own experience of being harmed, is normal and often necessary; a file that told someone they were being irrational for needing to make sense of what happened to them would be a serious failure. Anxiety-driven checking is a clinical phenomenon with its own literature and its own treatment, and is not evidence that anyone is manipulating you *(routing note — no citation retrieved this sweep)*.
- **Several members here are thin, and the file's vocabulary must not be used to diagnose anyone.** Two rows rest on a satire, one on nothing the KB could locate, one on a principle that is not a bias, and one on a two-paper literature from 1988. "That's just unit bias," "you're bike-shedding," "that's the Delmore effect" are not findings you can apply to a person — and used against someone raising an inconvenient point, "you're bike-shedding this" is a dismissal move in the family of [[tone-policing]] and [[bad-faith-argumentation]].
- **Unit selling is normal commerce, and the same lever is used protectively.** Portion control, standard drink measures and default pack sizes are the *intended benign* application of unit bias, endorsed by the researchers themselves [7][8]. Finishing your plate also has cultural and moral roots — waste aversion, scarcity history, upbringing — which attributing it to a bias flattens. And the non-food extension into tiers and bundles is this KB's analogy, not a measured finding.
- **Less genuinely is often better, and coherence has real value.** Fewer features, fewer moving parts, less to learn and less to maintain are genuine goods; a 24-piece intact set really is better than a 31-piece set with breakage for most household purposes, and being generous within a modest range really is generous [1]. Hsee's demonstration is that the *rating reverses with presentation mode*, not that the preference is senseless. A file that taught people to distrust "smaller" would be teaching them to distrust good design.
- **Bad agenda design is vastly more common than agenda manipulation.** People genuinely contribute where they feel competent; everyone has an opinion about a bike shed and nobody has one about a reactor. Small items sometimes matter more than their price tag — the "trivial" policy line is the one that gets quoted at someone in a disciplinary hearing. Deferring to experts is often correct, and time spent on low-stakes items builds the relationships that make high-stakes ones decidable. One bad meeting is a bad meeting.
- **Fixedness says nothing about any person or situation.** It is universal, present by about age seven, and cross-cultural [12], and using things for their intended purpose is overwhelmingly correct — the "fixedness" is the same competence that lets you operate a door. Do not read stuckness as evidence of manipulation: someone who cannot see a way out is very often facing a hard problem with genuinely few options, which is a material fact rather than a cognitive artifact.
- **The razor is a defence; the manipulation is its counterfeit.** Most odd behaviour has boring causes, most missed calls are missed calls, and this KB's guardrails depend on exactly that prior — the over-detection failure mode is precisely the *failure* to apply parsimony. "You're overthinking it" is very often true and kindly meant. The tell is never the invocation of simplicity; it is whether the simple explanation actually covers the evidence, and whether the person will look at the evidence with you.
- **Seams.** [[framing-anchoring]] owns distinction bias, joint/separate evaluation, decoys, contrast effects and tier grids, and is the home of the engineered-*joint*-evaluation exploit; this file owns the engineered-*separate* case and the dominance reversal, and states the seam here rather than restating the JE/SE machinery [1][2]. [[cognitive-overload-confusion]] owns complexity, volume and depletion as scrutiny-suppressors, which is where disclosure overload and consent-by-exhaustion live. [[probability-blindspots]] owns numeric and probability errors including the base-rate version of the razor misuse. [[certainty-needs]] and [[pattern-illusions]] own the appetite for a single tidy explanation. [[thats-not-all-decoys]] owns the tier-grid tactic; [[dark-patterns-obstruction]] owns default and friction engineering; [[information-asymmetry]] owns selective disclosure. Codex routing for all eight rows in this cluster: [[bias-codex-index]].
