# Cognitive Overload & Confusion

> Working memory is small, attention is finite, and judgment runs on both. Flood the channel — with speed, jargon, contradiction, paperwork, or sheer volume — and scrutiny fails open. Confusion isn't a side effect of manipulative environments; it's load-bearing architecture.

## The lever

Deliberate scrutiny is capacity-limited: cognitive-load research shows performance degrades predictably as working memory saturates [1]. Manipulation exploits this two ways. **Acute disruption**: a moment of confusion makes people *more* compliant with an immediately attached suggestion — the disrupt-then-reframe technique ("that's 300 pennies… it's a bargain!") roughly doubled sales in the original studies and holds up meta-analytically (mean r ≈ .28) [2]; the confusion opens a gap, the reframe fills it before scrutiny restarts. **Chronic friction**: complexity and "sludge" — paperwork, fine print, mazes of steps — function as a selective filter that defeats exactly the verification a fair deal would invite [3][4]. Two honesty notes the KB carries: the **ego-depletion** version of "willpower runs out" failed its major registered replication [1], and the famous **hungry-judge** decision-fatigue result is contested (case-ordering confounds; implausibly large effect) [5] — overload claims here rest on the load and disruption literatures, not those.

## Evidence base

- Sweller (1988): cognitive load theory [1]; Hagger et al. (2016): 23-lab registered replication finds ego depletion ≈ zero — flagged, not relied on [1].
- Davis & Knowles (1999), *JPSP*: disrupt-then-reframe, four studies; Carpenter & Boster (2009), *Communication Reports*: meta-analysis, r ≈ .28, OR ≈ 3.5, stronger in nonprofit than sales contexts [2].
- Sunstein (2019/2021): "sludge" — administrative burden (≈9.78B annual US paperwork hours) as a force that denies people their own entitlements via inertia and friction [3].
- Bar-Gill (2012), *Seduction by Contract*: competition *drives* sellers toward deliberately complex contracts that hide true costs (credit cards, mortgages, cell plans) [4].
- Danziger et al. (2011) hungry-judge effect + Weinshall-Margel & Shapard's case-ordering critique + Glöckner's magnitude analysis: presented as contested [5].
- "Word salad" as an abuse tactic is practitioner/survivor phenomenology (the term is borrowed from Bleuler's psychiatry) — graded folk/clinical; its academically grounded neighbors are DARVO and coercive control's conversational domination [6].

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **a channel flooded just before, or instead of, your scrutiny.**

- **Disrupt-then-reframe in the wild** — odd phrasings, sudden topic shifts, little shocks followed immediately by the suggested conclusion [2]; the pickpocket's bump, the salesman's non sequitur before the close.
- **Speed and volume** — fast-talk closings, the [[four-square-payment-packing]] worksheet shuffling four numbers at once, back-to-back meetings before a signature; the [[timeshare-playbook]]'s hours-long grind *is* an overload protocol.
- **Jargon walls** — complexity as authority costume and audit repellent ([[authority]]'s Dr. Fox conditions; financial products engineered to resist comparison [4]).
- **Sludge and cancellation mazes** — friction selectively placed on exit and refund paths ([[dark-patterns-obstruction]]; Sunstein's point inverted: the same burden that denies benefits keeps subscribers [3]).
- **Conversational exhaustion** — circular arguments, topic rotation, contradiction stacking that ends when the target concedes just to *end it* ([[circular-conversation]], the folk "word salad" [6]; pairs with [[certainty-needs]] — exhausted people seize whatever closure is offered).
- **Gish gallop** — more claims per minute than can be refuted per hour (coined by Eugenie Scott about creationist debating) [7]; the argumentative version of flooding ([[bad-faith-argumentation]]); at media scale, the firehose of falsehood ([[disinformation-playbooks]]).
- **Crisis stacking** — manufactured chaos (emergencies, moving targets, document demands) so the target never has capacity to step back and see the pattern ([[coercive-control]] environments run on chronic overload; [[moving-goalposts]]).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Complexity is often genuine — law, medicine, and tax are hard, and honest experts simplify as best they can. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **adds** complexity where simplicity was available (the contract designed to mislead [4]), **times** confusion to decisions [2], **places** friction asymmetrically (easy to join, maze to leave [3]), and **punishes** requests for time or clarification.

## Recognition

- **You understand less after the explanation than before** — repeatedly, with the same party, on questions that matter to your money or obligations.
- Pressure to decide *inside* the confusion: "don't worry about the details, just initial here" ([[manufactured-urgency]] welded to overload).
- **Asymmetric friction**: two clicks to subscribe, a phone tree to cancel; simple promises, labyrinthine terms [3][4].
- Clarifying questions answered with volume, jargon, or topic changes rather than answers — and irritation at "slowing things down" ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s response-to-resistance).
- **Felt-sense indicators:** mental fog arriving on schedule with this person/process; conceding to *end the conversation* rather than because you agree; dread of the paperwork as a force shaping your choices.

## Resistance

1. **Confusion is a stop sign, not a personal failing** ([[detection-heuristics]]): institute the rule — *I do not decide while confused.* The honest counterparty's response to "I need this in writing, in plain language, with time" is the diagnostic.
2. **Serialize the channel**: one question, one answer, in writing. Written exchange defeats speed, volume, and topic rotation at a stroke ([[documentation-practices]]); "send me that in an email" is overload kryptonite.
3. **Don't debate the gallop** ([[jade-avoidance]]): pick the single load-bearing claim and hold it; refuting everything is the trap's design [7].
4. **Pre-decide exits before entering known overload zones** (dealerships, presentations, "quick calls" that aren't): your cold-state limits, written down ([[emotional-flooding]]'s pre-commitment rule — the two levers stack).
5. **Outsource capacity**: a second reader for contracts, a friend on the phone during pressure calls — overload is per-brain; two brains halve it ([[verification-rituals]]).
6. **Budget the sludge deliberately**: when friction blocks something you're owed (refund, cancellation, claim), schedule it as a task with the friction *expected* — named sludge loses its discouraging power [3].

## Caveats

- **Most complexity is not a plot** — domains are genuinely hard, bureaucracies are sludgy by incompetence and underfunding ([[incompetence-not-malice]]), and bad explainers are usually just bad explainers ([[legitimate-expertise]] welcomes your clarifying questions — that's the discriminator).
- **Confusion is also in the receiver**: fatigue, anxiety, unfamiliarity, and language barriers produce fog without anyone engineering it ([[epistemic-guardrails]]: check your own state before attributing design).
- **Word salad ≠ disordered speech**: the folk tactic-label should never be applied to people with thought disorders, neurodivergent communication, or second-language speakers ([[neurodivergent-communication]]); the tactic-grade requires the conjunction — confusion *timed to accountability or asks*, clearing instantly when the topic turns safe [6].
- **The decision-fatigue literature is contested** [5]; this file's claims stand on load and disruption evidence, and so should yours.
- **Boundary with [[emotional-flooding]]:** flooding suspends judgment via *arousal*, overload via *capacity*; the worst environments run both ([[coercive-control]]). [[circular-conversation]] and [[bad-faith-argumentation]] are the tactic files built on this lever.
