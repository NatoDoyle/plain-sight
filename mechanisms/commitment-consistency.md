# Commitment & Consistency

> Small yeses compound. Once people commit — especially publicly, effortfully, or in writing — they bend their later choices, and even their identity, to stay consistent with the commitment. Manipulators build ladders out of this.

## The lever

Three intertwined drives: **cognitive dissonance** — inconsistency between actions and self-image is aversive, so we revise beliefs to justify what we've already done [1]; **self-perception** — we infer who we are from what we did ("I signed, so I must care") [2]; and the **social value of consistency** — reliable people are trusted, flip-floppers punished, so consistency is performed even against interest. Commitments bind hardest when they are *active, public, effortful, and freely chosen* [3][11]. Money and effort already spent recruit a fourth force: **sunk cost**, the robust tendency to continue endeavors to justify prior investment [4].

## Evidence base

- **Foot-in-the-door.** Freedman & Fraser (1966): homeowners asked to display a huge, ugly "Drive Carefully" billboard agreed 17% of the time cold — but 76% if they'd accepted a small related sticker/petition two weeks earlier [5]. Meta-analysis confirms the effect is real though more modest on average, with moderators (continuity, no payment for the first request) [6].
- **Commitment and action.** Moriarty (1975), Jones Beach: 1 in 5 bystanders intervened in a staged radio theft — 95% intervened if the owner had first asked "watch my things?" and they'd said yes [7] (a single field study, though a dramatic one).
- **Lowballing.** Cialdini et al. (1978): students agreed to a study, then learned it started at 7am — commitments survived the worsened terms far better than when the 7am cost was disclosed upfront [8].
- **Effort justification.** Aronson & Mills (1959): the harsher the initiation, the more members value the group — pain spent becomes proof of worth [9].
- **Sunk cost & escalation.** Arkes & Blumer's theater-ticket field experiment (discounted-ticket buyers attended less — prior payment drove attendance) [4]; Staw's "knee-deep in the big muddy" escalation under responsibility for failing courses of action [10].
- **Engineered confession.** Schein's analysis of Chinese POW indoctrination in Korea: collaboration was built from trivial written statements gradually escalated — writing was the trap's material [12].
- **Replication notes.** FITD and sunk cost are robust; classic dissonance paradigms are more debated in detail but the core motivational finding survives [1][6].

## How it's weaponized

The exploit is a **ladder**: trivial commitment → escalating asks → exit-cost accumulation, with the target's own self-justification doing the climbing.

- **Sales ladders** — yes-momentum scripts and trial closes; "just sign for the demo"; [[foot-in-the-door]] as a named tactic; [[lowballing]] and the finance-office shuffle ([[four-square-payment-packing]]): commit first, terms worsen after.
- **Subscription traps** — free trial → auto-renewal → cancellation maze ([[dark-patterns-obstruction]]); the commitment is harvested, then exit is priced.
- **Cults and high-control groups** — graded involvement: attend a dinner → small confession → public testimony → staff role; each step makes retreat a self-betrayal ([[cult-conversion-funnel]]; effort justification powers initiation rituals and "auditing" ladders) [9][12].
- **MLMs** — public goal declarations, "commit to your why", inventory purchases as identity stakes ([[mlm-recruitment-scripts]], [[mlm-lifecycle]]).
- **Scams** — sunk cost is the con's engine room: pig-butchering escalates deposits; advance-fee scams add "one last fee"; recovery scams re-victimize by invoking what's already lost ("get it back" — [[romance-scam-arc]], [[con-anatomy]]).
- **Relationships** — "after everything we've built" as a wall against leaving; escalating shared entanglements (lease, accounts, secrets) engineered faster than trust warrants ([[escalation-entrapment]], [[boundary-testing]]).
- **Identity leverage** — "you're not a quitter", "I thought you were generous": weaponizing the target's self-concept against their judgment ([[flattery-ego]]).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Consistency is a virtue and commitment devices genuinely help people (savings plans, training programs, public health pledges). The line: legitimate commitment serves *the committer's* declared goals with terms stable and known upfront; exploitation **engineers the first yes to be trivial, escalates terms after commitment, manufactures publicity/effort to raise exit costs, and benefits the ladder's builder** ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]).

## Recognition

- Micro-asks that precede macro-asks ("just fill in your email" → … ); someone keeping *score of your yeses*.
- "You already agreed" used as an argument when terms have changed — the change is the tell ([[moving-goalposts]]).
- Requests to commit *publicly or in writing* early, before information arrives.
- The trapped feeling of defending a position because it's yours, not because it's right; explaining away each new cost.
- Investment escalating on a schedule you didn't set; every exit point arriving just after a fresh deposit of money, effort, or disclosure.

## Resistance

1. **Zero-base the decision** — the single best counter to sunk cost: "Knowing what I know now, if I weren't already in, would I enter?" If no, exit; what's spent is spent either way [4].
2. **Reserve the right to change your mind.** Treat updated beliefs as competence, not betrayal (*principled consistency* is loyalty to values and evidence — not to past statements extracted from you).
3. **Spot the ladder** ([[detection-heuristics]]): trivial first asks from strangers with something to sell are rungs, not requests. Price the *whole* ladder before stepping.
4. **Slow the escalation** ([[verification-rituals]]): cooling-off periods before any upgrade of commitment; re-read original terms whenever asked to "just" extend.
5. **Keep early commitments private and small.** Publicity is the manipulator's ratchet — don't hand it over (decline pledge ceremonies, public goal boards run by interested parties).
6. **Lowball reflex:** if terms worsen after you agreed, the agreement died with the old terms. Renegotiate from zero or walk.

## Caveats

- **Consistency holds society together** — keeping promises, finishing hard things, and loyalty under pressure are virtues; this file targets *engineered* ladders, not character.
- **Perseverance vs sunk cost** is genuinely hard to distinguish from inside: continuing can be right when future expected value is positive. The test is prospective ("from here, is it worth it?"), never retrospective ("I've come so far").
- **Self-chosen commitment devices** (deposits, accountability partners) use the same lever *for* you — mechanism ≠ manipulation; check who built the ladder and who benefits.
- Single small favors or requests are usually just that ([[epistemic-guardrails]]); the diagnostic is the *sequence* and what happens at the exits.
- **Cultural calibration:** the consistency drive binds hardest where *personal* consistency signals integrity (individualist contexts); cross-national compliance research finds collectivist contexts weight in-group expectations more than prior personal commitments (e.g., Petrova, Cialdini & Sills 2007) — the ladder still works, but the rungs differ (*cultural difference*).
- **Boundary with [[dissonance-exploitation]]:** dissonance is the *engine* (the discomfort of inconsistency); this file covers the *ladder built on it* — engineered escalating commitments. The dissonance file covers the broader self-justification spirals (e.g., extracting complicity so the target defends the wrongdoing).
