# Confirmation & Expectancy

> Belief runs ahead of evidence: it picks what you search for, filters what you perceive, lowers the bar for congenial conclusions, survives its own correction, and even scripts what your body reports. Whoever installs the belief first gets your own cognition working for them — which is why so many cons open by handing you a conclusion.

## The lever

Three connected levers, one family.

**Searching and judging.** **Confirmation bias** is "the seeking or interpreting of evidence in ways that are partial to existing beliefs, expectations, or a hypothesis in hand" — Nickerson's definition, explicitly restricted to *unwitting* selectivity: the bias is what happens when someone is trying to be fair and still isn't [1]. (**Myside bias** is the same lever in argument production, and it is notably uncorrelated with intelligence — smart people are not protected [1].) Wason's 2-4-6 task supplied the classic demonstration — most participants announce a wrong rule after testing only triples their pet hypothesis predicts [2] — but the modern reading matters defensively: Klayman & Ha showed this is a **positive test strategy**, a usually-reasonable default heuristic that misfires only under particular structures — which is precisely the structure manipulators build [2]. The quantified limbs: selective exposure to congenial information is a *moderate* preference (meta-analytic d = 0.36, shrinking when accuracy motivation rises) [4]; biased assimilation — grading congenial evidence gently and uncongenial evidence hard — replicates robustly [3]. **Congruence bias** (evidence: supported — a small lab literature, best read as a named sub-case) is the testing arm: probing a hypothesis only with direct tests that would come back "yes" if it were true, never with tests that discriminate between rivals [5]. **Selective perception** (evidence: supported — a famous single demonstration, conceptually replicated since) filters the intake itself: Princeton and Dartmouth students counted a better-than-2:1 difference in infractions in the *same* game film [6], and modern subjects shown identical protest footage "saw" obstruction or peaceful speech depending on allegiance [6]. **Belief bias** judges an argument's logic by its conclusion's believability: invalid syllogisms with believable conclusions get accepted at rates commonly around 70%, against roughly 10% for matched unbelievable ones [7]. And **argument from fallacy** — dismissing a claim as false because one argument offered for it is bad — is a reasoning error identified by logicians, not an empirically measured bias (no evidence grade applies); it sits in this family as the confirmation-seeker's mute button, weaponized below.

**Expectancy.** The **observer-expectancy effect** is the umbrella (Rosenthal's program): an observer's expectation changes what gets elicited, recorded, or scored — Clever Hans was reading his questioners' postures a century ago [8]. Three codex names are synonyms of it. **Experimenter's bias** is the research-register synonym; its icon, the "maze-bright rat" study, is itself shaky (evidence: contested — Barber & Silver's 1968 critique and failed replications) [8], while the solid modern version is the blinding literature: non-blinded outcome assessors exaggerated treatment odds ratios by ~36% on subjective outcomes within the same trials [9]. **Expectation bias** is the synonym with the strongest field record — forensic contextual bias: 4 of 5 expert fingerprint examiners, re-shown prints they had themselves previously matched under context suggesting error, changed their own prior identifications (three to non-match, one to inconclusive) [10]; the FBI's Brandon Mayfield misidentification ran on exactly this circle [10]; and forensic psychologists scored identical case files up to d ≈ 0.85 apart depending on which side they believed had retained them [10]. **Observer effect** here means only the expectancy sense — *not* the physics concept of measurement disturbance; the homonym is itself exploited (below). The calibration that keeps this cluster honest (evidence: supported for target-side effects): expectancy effects on *other people's outcomes* are real but small — teacher-expectancy d ≈ 0.1, near zero once teachers had known their pupils a couple of weeks; self-fulfilling prophecies r ≈ .1–.2, and expectations predict outcomes mostly because they are *accurate* [8]. Expectancy effects on *the observer's own measurements* are the strong half [9], and they land hardest on people with the least independent standing [8]. Two members turn expectancy inward. **Subjective validation**: vague statements accepted as personally, specifically accurate — Forer's students rated one identical astrology-book sketch 4.26/5 as a description of themselves — with acceptance rising when the reading is favorable, framed as "prepared just for you," and delivered by an authority [11]. **Placebo effect**: response expectancies are self-confirming [12] — established for what you *feel*, not for what disease *does*. Against genuine no-treatment controls, effects concentrate in patient-reported outcomes (especially pain and nausea), with no statistical evidence on objective outcomes [12]; open-label placebos still move self-report — expectancy without deception [13].

**Resisting correction.** The **continued influence effect**: information explicitly retracted keeps driving inference even in people who accurately remember the retraction [14]. Meta-analytically, debunking works — belief drops substantially — *and* a residue persists, worse when the misinformation came from a credible source, was repeated, or the target generated their own supporting reasons [15]. Even innuendo suffices: merely *asking* whether someone did something, denials included, leaves incriminating impressions [16]. The **backfire effect** — the claim that corrections *strengthen* misbelief — is the family's cautionary tale (evidence: contested — Wood & Porter 2019): five experiments, over 10,000 subjects, and 52 issues chosen to maximize backfire found none; apparent backfires track unreliable measures; and the original author's own reassessment is that corrections are "typically at least somewhat effective" — they decay, they don't boomerang [17]. Practical upshot, stated plainly: **corrections usually work; do not stay silent for fear of backfire** [17]. **Conservatism** is under-revision: beliefs move in the Bayesian direction but by too little — Edwards' summary: it takes "anywhere from two to five observations to do one observation's worth of work" [18] — with the modern boundary that people under-weight strong, dry, cumulative evidence and *over*-weight vivid weak signals [18]. Finally the **Semmelweis reflex** — reflexive rejection of paradigm-violating evidence — is a folk label (evidence: folk): a countercultural coinage attributed to Robert Anton Wilson (in Leary's *The Game of Life*, 1979), with no measurement literature and a partly mythologized history; the real phenomena underneath it are biased assimilation and motivated skepticism [3], and in the wild the *label* does more manipulation work than the reflex (the Galileo gambit, below).

## Evidence base

The file grade reflects the core construct; members carry their own grades above. Replication status, honestly:

- **Survived cleanly:** subjective validation (replicating continuously since 1949; moderators well mapped) [11]; continued influence (two meta-analyses plus a 2023 direct replication) [14][15]; belief bias (the behavioral pattern, four decades) [7]; conservatism (underinference the dominant direction across ~500 experimental treatments) [18]; biased assimilation [3]; selective exposure (meta-analytic, moderate) [4]; forensic contextual bias (replicated across fingerprint, DNA-mixture, and forensic-psychology settings; acknowledged by NAS 2009 and PCAST 2016) [10]; unblinded-assessor inflation in within-trial designs [9]; placebo on subjective outcomes, including open-label [12][13].
- **Corrected by later work:** the 2-4-6 "irrationality" reading (→ positive test strategy) [2]; the attitude-*polarization* half of Lord, Ross & Lepper — self-report only; directly measured attitudes show no backlash even in large samples [3]; Pygmalion classroom effects (small, fragile, gone after ~2 weeks' acquaintance; teacher expectations mostly accurate) [8]; the Rosenthal & Fode rat study (failed replications — keep the construct, drop the icon) [8]; the backfire effect (largely settled against) [17].
- **Mechanism disputes that don't change the exploit:** belief bias may be a response-criterion shift rather than corrupted reasoning (ROC analyses) — either way congenial bad arguments get accepted, and the optimistic implication is that people *can* check the logic when instructed to [7]; conservatism coexists with base-rate neglect — the direction of error depends on the evidence's strength/weight profile [18].
- **Not empirical constructs:** Semmelweis reflex (folk label; route readers to biased assimilation) and argument from fallacy (informal logic; nothing to replicate).
- **Scale calibration:** hermetically partisan news diets describe roughly a 5% minority, and large 2023 feed-algorithm experiments did not measurably move attitudes [4] — self-sealing information environments are mostly *built by operators* (cults, cons, one person curating your inputs), not the population default.

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **the operator installs the conclusion first, then lets the target's own cognition manufacture, filter, and preserve the evidence.**

- **Seed and feed** — romance and investment fraud plants a flattering hypothesis early ("we have something rare"; "you have a gift for this"), then drips confirming detail while pre-discrediting disconfirmers ("your family won't understand"; "banks lie about crypto transfers"). The victim's own confirmation machinery does the rest — at scale: US romance/confidence-fraud losses reported to the FBI in 2024 ran ~$672M, with pig-butchering booked under a $6.57B investment-fraud line [19] ([[hope-greed]] supplies the wanting; this mechanism supplies the certainty).
- **Verification theater** (congruence bias) — tests engineered to pass whether or not the claim is true: fraud platforms permit small early withdrawals precisely so the "test" can be cited as proof; imposter scams supply their own callback numbers and lookalike sites so "verifying" can only confirm ([[phishing-pretexting]]); "try it 30 days and notice your energy" trials with no control, where regression to the mean guarantees a pass. The signature: the test was proposed by the party being tested.
- **The Barnum economy** (subjective validation) — [[cold-reading]] runs on the sitter's own interpretive labor. Industrialized, it is a regulatory record: the "Miss Cleo" pay-per-call operation settled with ~$500M in consumer charges forgiven [19]; the Maria Duval mail-fraud scheme took $175M+ from 1.3M+ mostly elderly victims with mass-printed "personal visions" [19] ([[elder-targeting]]); personality-quiz funnels and AI "readings" automate the personalization theater.
- **First-narrator advantage** (expectation bias) — get the story in first, to HR, police, courts, or the friend group, and every later ambiguous datum confirms the frame; pre-briefings ("just so you know what you're walking into…") are context injection, and the briefed audience's "independent" reaction is then cited as corroboration ([[smear-campaigns]], [[darvo]], [[victim-posturing]], [[mobbing]]).
- **Mud sticks** (continued influence) — accusations launched knowing the correction never fully claws them back [15]; "just asking questions" delivers the payload with deniability [16]; volume propaganda outruns correction entirely ([[disinformation-playbooks]], [[propaganda-devices]]) — with belief bias doing the wrapping: invalid arguments toward conclusions the audience already wants pass uninspected [7].
- **Prophecy management** (confirmation + expectancy) — high-control groups pre-frame every outcome as confirmation: answered prayer proves the power, unanswered proves your lack of faith ([[spiritual-abuse]], [[thought-terminating-cliches]]); "do your own research" funnels steer targets into pre-curated search terms whose results the operator dominates — self-directed "research" that can only confirm, held harder because it feels independently earned.
- **Boiling-frog escalation** (conservatism) — serial increments each too small to trigger re-evaluation: [[love-bombing]] installs a strong prior that later evidence cannot dent at conservative updating rates; [[boundary-testing]], grooming, and long cons all ride under-updating on accumulating red flags — Madoff's operation survived years of individually-explained-away anomalies [19] ([[ponzi-psychology]]).
- **Expectancy-laundered evidence** — unblinded "diagnostics" that always find the problem: free posture scans, in-home water tests by commissioned sellers, muscle testing. The gravest documented case is facilitated communication, where sincere facilitators unknowingly authored the messages — including false abuse allegations — attributed to non-verbal patients [8]. Sincerity does not prevent authorship. The same machinery powers "clinically studied" claims resting on seller-run, unblinded trials [9][20].
- **The placebo invoice** — inert products sold into subjective-symptom markets (pain, energy, fog, "toxins"): the relief is real; the causal claim is the product. The placebo defense is legally dead — *FTC v. QT, Inc.* rejected "it made people feel better" as a license for deceptive claims [20] — and the lethal edge is displacement: expectancy does nothing to objective disease, which is exactly where sham cures replace real treatment [12].
- **The labels as weapons** — several members are met more often as rhetoric than as bias: the **Galileo gambit** ("they laughed at Semmelweis too") converts expert rejection into evidence of correctness ([[bad-faith-argumentation]]); the fallacy-fallacy as mute button — one flawed argument, one peripheral error in an accuser's account, one weak study, and the whole claim is declared "debunked"; quantum-wellness marketing equivocates on "observer effect" to borrow physics prestige for expectancy products; and "don't bother correcting them — it'll backfire" counsels a silence the evidence does not support [17].

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Priors are not corruption: all competent cognition runs on them, an expert's fast dismissal of a crank is usually pattern recognition, and expectations mostly track reality [8]. Comfort, ritual, and hope have genuine value — and open-label placebo shows benefit does not require deception [13], which closes the sham-seller's favorite excuse: if honesty and benefit coexist, the deception in the sale is doing commercial work, not therapeutic work. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **engineers unfalsifiability** (every outcome confirms), **rigs the test** (verification channels the claimant controls), **injects context** (pre-briefed judges presented as independent), or **bills the expectancy** (causal claims sold on felt relief). Fabricated confirmation — not the mere presence of belief.

## Recognition

- **The "everything fits" click** — every new fact sorts into support, with growing irritation at disconfirming voices; the late-stage marker: you cannot name what evidence *would* change your mind.
- **Your search phrasing contains the verdict** — "is my sister toxic," not "why do my sister and I fight"; "research" instructions that arrive with the sources pre-picked.
- **The test can't fail** — proposed by the party being tested; passable whether or not they're lying; verification routed through channels they supplied; no stated result would count against them.
- **Pre-briefed judgment** — "Just so you know what you're walking into…"; "Everyone who meets him ends up agreeing with me"; your evaluation happened *after* you heard the desired conclusion, and matched it.
- **Living under a formed expectation** — "I knew you'd react this way" (after provoking the reaction); "this is just like you"; the sense that nothing you do changes how it's read — and "everyone agrees," where everyone was briefed by the same person first.
- **The uncanny chill** — "how could they possibly know that?" The chill is the product: you supplied the specifics; the labor was invisible. Ask the teller of any psychic story how many misses there were — they usually can't say.
- **Mud that outlived its correction** — catching yourself reasoning "where there's smoke…" about a claim you know was retracted; the correction stored as "there was some controversy," never as exoneration.
- **Felt-sense indicators:** your model of someone is still the week-one version despite months of contrary data; each red flag explained away singly, never totaled ("it's not like any one thing was *that* bad"); relief when an article confirms you, a flinch before opening one that might not.

## Resistance

1. **Consider the opposite** — the best-evidenced debiasing move: asking "would I rate this evidence the same if it pointed the other way?" eliminated biased assimilation where "be fair and objective" instructions failed [3].
2. **Design the discriminating test** ([[verification-rituals]]): before running any check, name the result that would look *different* if the claim were false — a test that passes either way is theater. Verify only through channels you sourced independently (look the number up yourself), and treat any test proposed by the tested party as presumptively rigged.
3. **Civilian Linear Sequential Unmasking** ([[documentation-practices]]): forensic reform's context-management protocol generalizes — record your own read of the evidence *before* exposure to anyone's framing, then log what changed your mind and when [10]. When a judgment reaches you pre-briefed, ask what the judge was told, and when: a citable methodological objection, not paranoia.
4. **Blind the assessor** [9]: "would this finding survive if the tester didn't know whose sample, body, or answer this was?" Anyone who profits from one outcome and runs their own unblinded test has produced marketing, not evidence.
5. **Aggregate the log, then re-decide from zero** ([[manipulation-audit]]): conservatism is beaten in the aggregate — a written timeline converts a dozen individually-dismissed increments into one high-strength display; then ask "knowing everything I know now, would I *start* this relationship/investment today?"
6. **Run the swap tests**: read the uncannily accurate profile as if written about your neighbor, and count misses as well as hits [11]; restate the persuasive argument with its conclusion reversed and check whether the logic still holds [7].
7. **Correct anyway — and correct well** [17]: backfire is not a reason for silence. Lead with the fact, name the myth once, and supply the alternative causal account that fills the gap the retraction leaves [14][15]; expect decay, so reinforce. For your own reputation: put the true account on the record early, once, in writing — then stop repeating the accusation ([[inoculation-prebunking]] covers the audience-side defense).

## Caveats

- **Firm belief is not confirmation bias** (*warranted-conviction*): conviction proportioned to strong, repeatedly tested evidence *should* be hard to move, and declining to re-litigate a settled question is calibration. The bias is asymmetric standards — easy grading for congenial evidence, hostile grading for uncongenial — not the possession of a view [1].
- **Positive testing is usually adaptive** [2]: running the confirming check first is often just efficient. Flag it only where discriminating tests are cheap, available, and persistently avoided — or where someone has arranged the environment so that only confirmation is obtainable.
- **Divergent accounts are the human base case**: selective perception means two sincere people leave one meeting with incompatible reports (*memory divergence*; [[memory-fallibility]] owns the reconstructive substrate). Divergence alone is evidence of nothing — [[gaslighting]] is the strategic *exploitation* of this base rate, not its mere occurrence.
- **The effects are moderate**: d = 0.36 selective exposure means people frequently do read the other side [4]; expectancy effects on others' real outcomes are small and fade [8]; sealed echo chambers describe a small minority, and feed algorithms moved attitudes little in large experiments [4]. Reserve the "bubble" diagnosis for environments actually engineered to be closed. And since expectations mostly track evidence [8], "their bias held me back" can itself harden into an unfalsifiable frame.
- **This file is a weapon in the wrong hands**: "you're just confirmation-biased" is a symmetric accusation that settles nothing, and a target who has documented a pattern of mistreatment is not thereby biased ([[epistemic-guardrails]]). The suspicious reader runs the same machinery — someone hunting manipulation will find confirming evidence in ordinary behavior; the discriminating-test discipline applies to *your own* hypothesis about being manipulated.
- **Corrections work; wariness can be rational**: never counsel silence on backfire grounds [17] — but discounting a retraction is sometimes Bayesian-sound: retractions can be wrong or coerced, and *withdrawn is not disproven* (recantation under pressure is common where [[coercive-control]] operates — handle with care in both directions) [14].
- **Placebo cuts both ways**: feeling better after an unsupported ritual does not mean you were manipulated — attention, ritual, and hope have real value [13]; the manipulation threshold is deceptive causal claims plus payment plus displaced treatment [12][20].
- **The evidence base is WEIRD-heavy**: nearly everything cited above was measured in Western, educated, industrialized samples, and cross-cultural generality is largely untested — dialectical-thinking research suggests belief revision under contradiction itself varies across cultures, so export these effect sizes beyond WEIRD populations with caution [21].
- **Seams**: this file owns *favoring belief-consistent evidence*; [[pattern-illusions]] owns *seeing structure in randomness* — a person can exhibit neither, either, or both; [[availability-salience]] owns what comes to mind first; [[framing-anchoring]] owns reference-point placement; [[memory-fallibility]] owns the rewriting of the record itself.
