# Hope & Greed Exploitation

> Fear makes people comply; hope makes them *participate*. Dangle a disproportionate reward — wealth, love, the life that should have been — and targets will push past their own doubts, fund their own exploitation, and defend the scam against rescuers. Investment fraud, not tech-support fraud, is the costliest crime category on the internet.

## The lever

Hot states hijack the controls: **visceral influences** — craving, desire, anticipation — crowd out deliberative weighing of consequences in ways people neither predict nor remember accurately [1]. Scam research formalizes the result as **phantom fixation**: attention locks onto a vivid disproportionate reward (the inheritance, the jackpot, the soulmate, the 10× coin) and the trivial-seeming costs en route stop registering — the UK OFT study found some victims half-knowingly treated scams as gambles worth taking [2]. The lever is self-reinforcing: **near-misses** — engineered almost-wins — are rated *less* pleasant yet increase the urge to continue, recruiting the same reward circuitry as wins [3]. Hope, dosed intermittently, becomes the engine of [[intermittent-reinforcement]].

## Evidence base

- Loewenstein (1996): visceral states systematically override deliberation; people in cold states cannot forecast their hot-state behavior [1].
- Fischer, Lea & Evans (2009), OFT *Psychology of Scams*: disproportionate-reward fixation, visceral triggers, and scarcity cues as the recurring scam-persuasion elements across mass-market fraud [2].
- Clark et al. (2009, *Neuron*): near-misses increase desire to play and activate win-related striatal/insula circuitry, strongest under perceived personal control; Reid (1986) documented that slots and lotteries are *contrived* to overproduce near-misses [3].
- **Scale**: FBI IC3 2024 — investment fraud was the costliest category at **$6.57B**; crypto-related losses $9.3B (crypto-*investment* subset $5.8B); FinCEN's 2023 pig-butchering alert codified the "fatten then butcher" relationship-investment playbook with 15 red flags [4].
- **MLM economics**: the FTC's 2024 staff analysis of 70 income disclosure statements found most participants made $1,000/year or less (in ≥17 MLMs a majority earned *nothing*), with disclosures typically omitting expenses; the widely-quoted "~99% lose money" figure is Jon Taylor's after-expenses advocacy analysis — directionally supported, not an FTC finding [5].
- **The smart-investor paradox**: the NASD/FINRA-lineage victim research found investment-fraud victims scored *higher* on financial literacy than non-victims — confidence and self-rated savvy are risk factors, not armor [6] (full treatment in [[why-smart-people-fall]], [[optimism-overconfidence]]).

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **a vivid phantom, a plausible path, and escalating fees on the path.**

- **Advance-fee structures** — the inheritance/lottery/contract that needs "one small fee" repeatedly: the phantom justifies each next payment ([[con-anatomy]]'s "convincer" and "send"; [[commitment-consistency]] compounds each fee into reason for the next).
- **Pig butchering** — romance/friendship grooming fused to a fake trading platform: small verified "withdrawals" early (the convincer), then escalating deposits, then the freeze ([[romance-scam-arc]], FinCEN red flags [4]).
- **Ponzi mechanics** — early payouts manufacture proof; redemption theater and exclusivity ("closed fund, but for you…") keep money in ([[ponzi-psychology]], [[affinity-fraud]], [[social-proof]]).
- **MLM income theater** — lifestyle posts, stage walks, "be your own boss": the phantom is identity-shaped (freedom, mattering), the costs arrive as inventory and lost friendships ([[mlm-recruitment-scripts]], [[mlm-lifecycle]]) [5].
- **Engineered near-misses** — slots and loot-box economies by design [3]; "you almost qualified — one more purchase" tiers; the recruiter's "you were our second choice, stay close" ([[breadcrumbing]] is the relational version).
- **Hope in love** — "the one" dangled: [[future-faking]] (vivid promised futures as payment-now leverage) and [[love-bombing]] run on romantic phantom fixation; recovery scams re-run the phantom on fraud victims ("we can get your money back — small retainer") [2][4].
- **Get-saved offers** — prosperity teachings, miracle cures, "manifestation" courses: hope harvested where institutions have failed people ([[cults-high-control]], [[affinity-fraud]] adjacency).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Selling hope honestly is most of legitimate commerce and all of mentorship: real opportunities, disclosed odds, proportionate claims. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation shows **disproportion** (returns/outcomes far beyond market reality), **fee-gated phantoms** (the reward always one payment away), **engineered almost-wins**, and **hostility to diversification, delay, or due diligence** ([[genuine-opportunity]] tolerates all three).

## Recognition

- **Returns/outcomes out of proportion** to anything verifiable — "guaranteed" 10%/month, "risk-free", soulmate-in-a-week. Disproportion *is* the tell, not a detail [2].
- **The reward retreats as you advance** — each fee/deposit/favor reveals one more gate ([[moving-goalposts]] with a jackpot painted on it).
- **Pressure against verification and diversification**: "don't tell your bank what it's for", "wire it all to capture the window", "outsiders won't understand the opportunity."
- **Almost-there messaging** as a system: near-misses, "you barely missed the bonus", second-chance offers [3].
- **Felt-sense indicators:** future-life daydreams crowding out present arithmetic; irritation at doubters (including your own doubt — you've begun defending the phantom); checking the platform/person compulsively for the next dose of almost.

## Resistance

1. **Make "too good to be true" operational** ([[detection-heuristics]]): benchmark every claimed return against boring reality (market index, base-rate of jackpot love); >40% of adults in FINRA's study couldn't flag a "guaranteed 110%/year" pitch as fraudulent — train the reflex deliberately [6].
2. **Cold-state decisions only** [1]: never commit money/promises while the daydream is hot; impose a 72-hour rule and re-read the offer when the phantom has faded ([[verification-rituals]]).
3. **Pay-to-receive is the stop sign**: legitimate winnings, inheritances, jobs, and lovers do not require escalating fees to release them. The second fee request ends the conversation.
4. **Test with withdrawal/diligence**: real platforms tolerate small withdrawals, audits, and "my accountant will look at this"; phantoms punish them ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s response-to-resistance test) [4].
5. **Diversification as policy**: no single opportunity gets money you can't lose — stated upfront, it also functions as a manipulator filter (they push back; fiduciaries don't).
6. **Audit the near-miss**: ask "who controls how close I almost came?" If the answer is the house, the almost was manufactured [3].

## Caveats

- **Hope is not the enemy** — ambition, courtship, entrepreneurship, and faith all run on it, and most opportunity-sellers are merely optimistic, not predatory ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; [[genuine-opportunity]]).
- **Real asymmetric wins exist** (early-stage equity, genuine windfalls); the diagnostics are verification-tolerance and fee-direction, not upside size alone.
- **Victim-blaming inversion guard**: phantom fixation is a human universal under the right targeting — fraud victims include the financially literate [6]; "greedy victims deserved it" is both false and exactly the shame that suppresses reporting ([[shame-leverage]]).
- **The "99% lose money" MLM figure is advocacy analysis** — directionally consistent with FTC's conservative findings but not an official statistic; this KB cites both with their grades [5].
- **Boundary with [[scarcity-urgency]]:** scarcity compresses *when* you decide; hope-greed corrupts *what* you're weighing. Scams run them stacked.
