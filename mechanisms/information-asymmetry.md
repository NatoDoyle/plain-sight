# Information Asymmetry

> Whoever knows what you can't verify can charge you for it, steer you with it, or starve you of it. Economics gave the problem a Nobel; abusers, employers, and high-control groups run it as standing policy.

## The lever

When one side knows more and the other side can't check, exploitation becomes structurally available. Akerlof's "lemons" analysis showed asymmetric quality information doesn't just enable individual rip-offs — it degrades whole markets [1]. The sharpest case is **credence goods** — services where you can't verify what you needed *or what you got* (medicine, repairs, law, IT): theory predicts and field experiments confirm overtreatment, undertreatment, and overcharging; in the classic taxi field experiment, passengers who merely *signaled unfamiliarity* were taken on significantly longer detours [2]. The deeper layer is **epistemic dependence**: modern life makes reliance on others' knowledge rational and unavoidable [3] — so the lever can't be removed, only managed. Interpersonally, the same structure appears as **information control**: Lifton's first criterion of thought reform is milieu control — control of communication and information [4]; economic abusers hide financial information as a measured, validated abuse dimension [5]; organizations do it as "knowledge hiding" (evasive hiding, playing dumb, rationalized hiding) [6].

## Evidence base

- Akerlof (1970), *QJE*: adverse selection from quality asymmetry [1].
- Dulleck & Kerschbamer (2006), *JEL*: credence-goods theory; Balafoutas et al. (2013), *Review of Economic Studies*: Athens taxi detours — ignorance signals raised fraud (~15-point rise in manipulated bills for tariff-ignorance) [2].
- Hardwig (1985): epistemic dependence as rational necessity [3].
- Lifton (1961) milieu control; Hassan's BITE Information-control component (withholding/distorting information, restricting outside sources, compartmentalized doctrine) [4].
- Adams et al. (2008), Scale of Economic Abuse, *Violence Against Women* 14(5): hiding financial information and blocking account access as validated economic-control items [5].
- Connelly et al. (2012), *JOB*: knowledge hiding in organizations [6]. ("Mushroom management" is folklore — kept, graded folk [6].)

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **engineered ignorance — yours — priced, steered, or enforced.**

- **Credence-goods predation** — unnecessary repairs, procedures, add-ons sold to customers who can't audit need or delivery [2] ([[sales]], [[four-square-payment-packing]]; the mechanic/dentist/consultant problem; [[fear-exploitation]]'s diagnoser-rescuer fusion compounds it).
- **Financial information control in relationships** — hidden accounts, intercepted mail, "don't worry about the money": measured abuse, not mere secrecy [5] ([[financial-abuse]], [[coercive-control]]).
- **Milieu control in groups** — banned media, insider-only doctrine, members reporting on members [4] ([[cults-high-control]], [[isolation-tactics]] applied to information rather than people).
- **Workplace gatekeeping** — mushroom management, knowledge hiding, salary opacity, "need to know" deployed as rank [6] ([[workplace-bosses]], [[credit-theft-visibility]]; pay secrecy norms persisting despite legal protection — see [[authority]]'s NLRA note).
- **Selective disclosure in deals** — technically-true pitches built on what's omitted ([[lying-typologies]]'s omission and paltering live on this lever; undisclosed conflicts of interest; the fine print as burial ground [[cognitive-overload-confusion]]).
- **Dependence farming** — keeping the target ignorant *by design*: passwords never shared, processes never explained, "I'll handle it" for years — so leaving means losing operational capacity ([[escalation-entrapment]], [[weaponized-incompetence]] inverted: engineered *competence hoarding*).
- **Asymmetry plays in negotiation** — exploiting what only you know the other side doesn't ([[bad-faith-negotiation]]; the legitimate/illegitimate line is exactly the deception threshold in [[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s legal table).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Expertise *is* information asymmetry — doctors, lawyers, and mechanics necessarily know more, and confidentiality, privacy, and surprise parties are benign concealment ([[legitimate-expertise]], [[benign-concealment]]). The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **profits from your ignorance rather than curing it**, **resists verification** (second opinions, documentation, transparency requests), and **engineers the asymmetry** (hiding what you'd need, punishing learning).

## Recognition

- **Your questions change the temperature** — requests for documentation, itemization, or a second opinion produce irritation, delay, or "don't you trust me?" ([[trust-mechanics]]).
- **Information arrives only on a need-to-comply basis** — you learn things exactly when knowing serves them, never before decisions.
- **Learning is discouraged**: jokes or anger when you read the contract, google the symptom, ask a colleague what they earn, or talk to ex-members [4].
- Chronic, *engineered* dependence: systems only they can run, accounts only they can see, "it's too complicated for you."
- **Felt-sense indicators:** deciding important things while feeling like you're missing the page everyone else has; embarrassment at how little you know about your own money/job/group; relief when they "handle it" — shading into unease that you couldn't handle it alone anymore.

## Resistance

1. **Buy the audit** ([[verification-rituals]]): second opinions, independent inspections, vehicle-history reports — credence-goods fraud collapses where verification is cheap and *expected* [2]; normalize "I always get a second quote" as policy, not accusation.
2. **Signal knowledge, not ignorance** [2]: the taxi finding generalizes — basic preparation ("I've read the tariff", "I've seen the comps") measurably reduces exploitation attempts.
3. **Right-to-know hygiene in relationships**: both partners can name the accounts, the balances, the passwords that matter; resistance to financial transparency is a flagged abuse dimension, not a quirk [5] ([[documentation-practices]]).
4. **Reverse the burden**: in any consequential deal, what they *won't* show you is the disclosure ([[detection-heuristics]]; "what would I learn from the version of this document you don't want me to see?").
5. **Keep outside information channels alive** — the group/partner/employer who mediates *all* your information has acquired the lever whole [4] ([[isolation-tactics]] counter; talk to leavers, read the critics, know your market rate).
6. **Calibrated epistemic dependence** [3]: you must rely on experts — so choose ones embedded in verification structures (licensure, review, second-opinion norms) rather than ones who *are* the structure.

## Caveats

- **Asymmetry is the human condition**, not an accusation: every expert, parent, and institution knows things you don't, and nearly all of them aren't exploiting it ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; the diagnostics are profit-from-ignorance + verification hostility + engineering, in conjunction).
- **Privacy isn't gatekeeping**: people legitimately keep salaries, histories, and feelings private ([[benign-concealment]]); the tactic-grade requires *your* decisions being steered by *their* curation.
- **"I'll handle it" is usually kindness** — division of labor is how households and teams work; the flag is dependence that deepens over time against your wishes, plus punishment for learning.
- **Don't weaponize transparency demands**: insisting on access to a partner's every account and message is itself a control pattern ([[coercive-control]] runs both directions; transparency norms should be symmetric).
- **Boundary with [[cognitive-overload-confusion]]:** overload defeats scrutiny by *flooding* the channel; this lever defeats it by *starving* the channel. The fine-print trick uses both. [[lying-typologies]] (omission, paltering) are the utterance-level tactics built here.
