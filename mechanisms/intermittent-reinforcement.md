# Intermittent Reinforcement

> The cruelest finding in behavioral science: unpredictable reward binds harder than reliable reward. The slot machine, the hot-and-cold partner, and the infinite feed run on the same schedule — and the burst of effort when the reward stops is part of the trap's design.

## The lever

Two distinct, canonical findings get fused under this name, and the KB keeps them precise. **Rate:** variable-ratio schedules — reward after an unpredictable number of responses — produce the highest, steadiest response rates of any schedule (Ferster & Skinner's systematic mapping) [1]. **Persistence:** the *partial reinforcement extinction effect* (PREE, originating with Humphreys 1939) — behavior reinforced only sometimes survives far longer after rewards stop entirely than behavior reinforced every time [1]. Translation: unpredictability makes you respond *more*, and inconsistency makes you persist *longer* once there's nothing left. A third element matters defensively: the **extinction burst** — when reinforcement is withdrawn, responding often intensifies before it fades (documented in roughly a quarter of clinical datasets — common, not universal) [2]. Whoever controls an unpredictable reward supply gets maximal effort, maximal persistence, and a built-in punishment-spike for anyone who tries to quit them.

## Evidence base

- Ferster & Skinner (1957), *Schedules of Reinforcement*: VR schedules beat interval schedules on response rate at matched reinforcement [1]; Humphreys (1939): the PREE in classical conditioning [1].
- Lerman & Iwata (1995): extinction bursts in 24% of 113 applied datasets (36% under extinction alone) — the "worse before better" pattern is real and *not* inevitable [2].
- **Gambling engineering**: Schüll's ethnography of machine gambling — algorithms, ergonomics, and player tracking engineered for "time on device" and the "machine zone" trance [3]; structural-characteristics research (Griffiths 1993) [3]; the near-miss findings live in [[hope-greed]].
- **Loot boxes**: psychologically akin to gambling on structural analysis [4]; loot-box spending correlates with problem-gambling severity in large samples (n=7,422; replicated) [4].
- **Apps and feeds**: variable reward is an explicit design component of the industry's own playbook (Eyal's Hook Model: trigger → action → *variable reward* → investment) [5], with a critical ethics literature treating addictive design as exploitative [5].
- **Relationships**: traumatic bonding theory — Dutton & Painter's two structural factors for paradoxical attachment to an abuser are **power imbalance** and **intermittency of good/bad treatment**; their post-separation study found both predicted attachment strength, trauma symptoms, and lowered self-esteem [6] ([[trauma-bonding]] carries the full treatment and its evidence debates).

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **an unpredictable supply of something needed, with the schedule in the exploiter's hands.**

- **The hot-and-cold partner** — affection, rage, and withdrawal on no learnable schedule: the target works ever harder for the good days and can't extinguish on the bad ones [6] ([[trauma-bonding]], [[abuse-cycle]]; the reconciliation phase is the jackpot that re-arms the schedule).
- **Engineered gambling** — slots, and their digital descendants: loot boxes, gacha mechanics, mystery rewards [3][4] ([[dark-patterns-social-urgency]] adjacent).
- **Attention economies** — pull-to-refresh, notification batching, algorithmic feeds: maybe-something-good on every check [5] ([[digital-platforms]], [[parasocial-influencer-tactics]]).
- **Crumb economies in courtship** — [[breadcrumbing]]: minimal, irregular attention calibrated to keep a target orbiting (preliminary academic correlates: lower life satisfaction, more loneliness and helplessness [7]); the deliberate version of what flaky people do by accident.
- **Workplace carrot-scheduling** — praise, opportunities, and "almost-promotions" dispensed unpredictably ([[workplace-bosses]], [[moving-goalposts]]: the goalpost move *resets the schedule*); commission structures engineered as near-miss machines ([[mlm-lifecycle]]'s rank systems).
- **Hoovering and the extinction burst** — when a target finally withdraws (gray rock, no contact), the manipulator's *intensified campaign* — gifts, declarations, crises, threats — is the interpersonal extinction burst [2] ([[hoovering]]; expecting it is half of surviving it: see Resistance).
- **Cult cycles** — favor and disfavor from leadership on unreadable schedules; status granted, stripped, restored ([[cults-high-control]], [[conditional-love]] at group scale).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Variability isn't sinister — life, love, and honest games are intrinsically variable, and surprise is part of joy (the unplanned gift, the spontaneous trip). The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation means the **schedule is controlled and calibrated** by a party who benefits from your persistence (engagement metrics, compliance, devotion), **the "rewards" are recycled basics** (ordinary decency repackaged as jackpot after engineered drought), and **your attempts to stabilize the schedule are punished** ("needy", "controlling") — because predictability would break the lever.

## Recognition

- **You can't predict which version of them you'll get** — and you've started working *harder* because of it, not less.
- **Ordinary decency lands like euphoria** — a calm evening or basic kindness feels like a jackpot; that contrast is the drought talking, not the gift.
- **Requests for consistency get punished or pathologized** — asking "can we just be steady?" triggers anger or withdrawal; stabilizing the schedule is treated as an attack (it is — on the lever; [[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s response-to-no).
- **Checking behavior**: compulsively monitoring the phone/feed/person for the next maybe ([[breadcrumbing]], app patterns alike).
- **Felt-sense indicators:** white-knuckle hope; exhaustion plus inability to stop; describing the relationship in averages ("they're wonderful 20% of the time") while living for the spikes; post-quit cravings strongest right after — and *their* sudden intensification right after you pull away.

## Resistance

1. **Name the schedule** ([[detection-heuristics]]): chart it if needed — dates, good days, bad days, what preceded each. Variable-ratio patterns visible on paper lose their mystique; the chart also defeats the averaging your memory does ([[documentation-practices]]).
2. **Price the relationship at its base rate, not its peaks**: decide based on the modal day. The jackpot days are the *mechanism*, not the relationship.
3. **Expect the extinction burst** [2]: when you withdraw, escalation often comes — more charm, more crisis, more pressure ([[hoovering]]). Pre-deciding "escalation confirms the pattern, it doesn't reopen the question" converts the burst from re-hook to final evidence ([[no-contact-exit-planning]]; if escalation includes threats, see safety resources in `README.md`).
4. **Stabilize or leave** — the direct test: request consistency plainly, once, and watch ([[boundary-scripts]]). Partners stabilize or honestly say why they can't; schedule-controllers punish the request.
5. **For the digital version**: kill variable triggers (notifications off, scheduled checking, grayscale), which attacks the schedule rather than your willpower [5].
6. **Refill elsewhere while deciding** — variable reward binds hardest on a depleted target; steady sources of connection and reward ([[attachment-needs]]'s diversification rule) lower the jackpot's leverage.

## Safety notes

If the hot-and-cold pattern includes violence, threats, strangulation, surveillance, or escalating control, the severity override in [[epistemic-guardrails]] governs: this is [[coercive-control]] territory, and **separation is the documented highest-risk window** — do not improvise the exit protocol in Resistance §3–4 alone. Plan with professionals: **US:** National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 / thehotline.org · **UK:** 0808 2000 247 · elsewhere, local DV services (standing list in `README.md`); the dedicated protocol is [[dv-safety-planning]]. Expecting an extinction burst is *not* the same as being safe during one.

## Caveats

- **Inconsistent ≠ manipulative.** Depression, bipolar mood cycling, ADHD, overwork, and avoidant attachment all produce hot-and-cold patterns *without* a controlled schedule or harvested benefit ([[neurodivergent-communication]], [[self-protective-distance]]). The diagnostics: who controls the variability, who benefits from your persistence, and what happens when you ask for steadiness — in conjunction, over time ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).
- **New relationships are legitimately variable** — early-stage uncertainty is not a schedule; the file targets *established patterns* where consistency is possible and punished.
- **The extinction burst is common, not universal** [2] — its absence proves nothing, and "it'll get worse before better" should not be weaponized to predict doom for every exit.
- **Variable reward in design is a spectrum** — surprise-and-delight features differ from engineered compulsion loops; the loot-box correlations [4] are correlational (direction debated), flagged as such.
- **Boundary with [[trauma-bonding]]:** this file is the *schedule* (the behavioral engine); trauma-bonding is the resulting *attachment dynamic* under power asymmetry, with its own evidence base and debates. [[hope-greed]] owns the near-miss findings; [[abuse-cycle]] owns the cyclical clinical model.
