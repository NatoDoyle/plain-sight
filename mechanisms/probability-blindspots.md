# Representativeness & Probability Blindspots

> Probability is not something the mind perceives; it computes a stand-in — resemblance, story quality, outcome vividness, the nominal number, the visible sample — and feels the result as likelihood. Every one of those stand-ins can be manufactured, which is why so many scams are, at bottom, arithmetic performed on you: a numerator they chose, a denominator you never see, in a format you can't check.

## The lever

**Resemblance replaces arithmetic.** The **representativeness heuristic**: probabilities are evaluated "by the degree to which A is representative of B, that is, by the degree to which A resembles B" [1] — similarity to a prototype gets computed where a posterior probability was the question, and priors have no term in the similarity computation. Its signature failure is the **base rate fallacy** (base rate neglect): in the lawyer–engineer problem, judgments from a personality sketch were essentially identical whether the stated pool was 70 engineers/30 lawyers or the reverse, and even a deliberately worthless sketch caused base rates to be discarded [2]; in the taxicab problem the Bayesian posterior is 41% and the modal answer is 80% — witness reliability substituted for the whole computation [2]. Two calibrations keep this honest: Koehler's critique — base rates *are* used when made causally relevant or learned from experience; this is a tendency, not blindness [3] — and Gigerenzer's format result: natural frequencies ("out of 1,000 people…") lift correct Bayesian answers roughly from 16% to 46% in the original studies and from ~4% to ~24% pooled meta-analytically (226 performance estimates, 35 papers) — facilitation that is real, large, and still leaves most people wrong [4]. This KB's worked over-detection treatment — why a "strong sign" of manipulation still leaves ~88% innocence — lives in [[epistemic-guardrails]]; this file defers to it. **Insensitivity to sample size**: samples are expected to resemble their populations regardless of n — on the hospital problem, 56% answered "about the same" and only ~22% correctly chose the small hospital [1], and research psychologists themselves plan underpowered studies ("belief in the law of small numbers") [5]; Sedlmeier & Gigerenzer's restriction: intuitions are decent on frequency-distribution tasks and fail specifically on sampling-distribution tasks — the deficit is real but narrower than "no sample-size intuition" [5]. The **conjunction fallacy**: judging P(A∧B) > P(A) — ~85% ranked "Linda is a bank teller and is active in the feminist movement" above "Linda is a bank teller" [6]. Every added detail can only lower a scenario's probability while raising its felt plausibility — the scam-narrative engine. The Hertwig–Gigerenzer conversational-pragmatics critique is flagged and real ("probable" read cooperatively; frequency formats shrink the error) [7], but the effect survives real-money bets, transparent set relations, and the clarified "whether or not" wording (57% still erred) — "none dissipated the effect" is the review verdict [6] — while incentives plus group consultation do cut it substantially, which is itself a defense [7]. The **subadditivity effect** (brief): support theory — subjective probability attaches to *descriptions* of events, not events; "death from natural causes" is judged 58% packed but its named parts sum to 73% unpacked, with the boundary that unpacking *atypical* components can reverse the effect [8]. (The representativeness umbrella traditionally also covers streak intuitions — gambler's fallacy, hot hand; those are pattern-*perception* errors and live in [[pattern-illusions]].)

**Emotion and modality flatten the curve.** **Neglect of probability** — graded honestly in three layers. Established: prospect theory's weighting function — small *described* probabilities are overweighted and midrange differences underweighted [9] — the engine of both lottery tickets and insurance; plus the description–experience gap: rare events are overweighted when described and *under*weighted when merely lived through, which predicts both jackpot marketing and "nothing bad has happened yet" complacency [9]. Supported as a field pattern: Sunstein's probability neglect — under strong emotion, people respond to the badness of the outcome image rather than its likelihood; terrorism works by "producing public fear that might greatly exceed the discounted harm" [10]. Contested: the celebrated lab demonstration (Rottenstreich & Hsee 2001 — a median $7 to avoid a 1% chance of electric shock vs $10 for a 99% chance) **failed its Many Labs 2 replication**: original d = 0.74, replication d = −0.08 (95% CI [−0.13, −0.03], N = 7,218), pointing in the *opposite* direction [11]. The fear-and-jackpot analysis below hangs on the surviving legs, not on that study. The **appeal to probability fallacy** (informal logic — no evidence grade applies): treating the possible as certain, ◇P therefore P; its tell is a modal verb that hardens from "could" to "will" between hook and close. **Murphy's Law** (evidence: folk): an engineering maxim from the Edwards AFB rocket-sled tests (~1949) repurposed as folk fatalism — it feels true observer-side because memorable failures are retained while uneventful successes never register, and the law of truly large numbers guarantees rare mishaps *somewhere* [20]; as defensive-design doctrine it is rational, and "everything always goes wrong" deployed as doom-narration is a frame to notice, not a statistic.

**Money's units go uncomputed.** **Money illusion** (established): "a tendency to think in terms of nominal rather than real monetary values" [12] — a 7% nominal pay cut at zero inflation is judged unfair by most, while a 5% raise under 12% inflation is judged acceptable: identical −7% real outcomes drawing opposite verdicts [12]; Fehr & Tyran showed even minimal individual-level illusion generates considerable, asymmetric aggregate nominal inertia (sticky downward, quick upward) [12] — with the note that the aggregate interpretation has a live minority dispute (Petersen & Winn; Fehr & Tyran's reply) [12]. **Mental accounting** (established): "the set of cognitive operations used by individuals and households to organize, evaluate, and keep track of financial activities" [13] — money booked into non-fungible accounts, each with its own budget and rules, confirmed at field scale (marginal propensity to spend SNAP benefits on food ≈ 0.5–0.6 vs ≈ 0.1 for cash) [13]. Hedonic editing — segregate gains, integrate losses — is the grammar of bundling and "free bonus" framing; the **house-money effect** specifically is graded down inline (evidence: supported — framing-fragile: the same prior gain produces risk-seeking in a "lottery" frame and the opposite in a "portfolio" frame, with mixed field evidence) [13]; pennies-a-day temporal reframing makes $475/year feel like $1.30 [13]; and partitioned pricing — the academic ancestor of drip pricing — is [[framing-anchoring]]'s territory, cross-linked rather than duplicated. The **denomination effect** (evidence: supported — the original paper's "three replications" are internal, and no independent registered replication or meta-analysis existed as of the 2026 sweep): people spend small denominations far more readily than an equivalent large one (63% of students given four quarters spent vs 26% given a dollar bill) — the large bill functions as a self-control brake, "less replaceable" than its parts [14]. The exploitation reading — casino chips, gift cards, and in-game currencies strip that brake by pre-breaking value into abstract units — is an inference from the mechanism plus the payment-decoupling literature (credit-vs-cash willingness gaps), and this file labels it as such [14].

**The sample was chosen for you.** **Survivorship bias** (established — a selection effect whose mathematics are certain; what varies empirically is the failure to correct for it): conclusions drawn from whatever survived a filter, while the filtered-out cases generate no observations at all. Wald's bomber-armor memoranda are the canonical logic — estimating vulnerability from the survivors' *unhit* regions, because planes struck there never came home (the after-dinner version with the stunned generals is a later dramatization; the method is the real lesson) [15]. Quantified: excluding dead funds inflates reported mutual-fund performance ≈ 0.9 percentage points/year, and "70% of extant funds could truthfully claim to have performance in the first quartile of their peers if the peer group includes funds that have closed" [15]; Denrell's formal result — where failure predicts disappearance, learning from success stories is *negatively* informative [15]. **Zero-sum bias** (evidence: supported — and two distinct constructs, kept distinct): Meegan's lab misjudgment — "intuitively judging a situation to be zero-sum when it is actually non-zero-sum": students predicted lower grades for the next student after seeing many high grades awarded *under explicitly absolute grading*, a unidirectional bias reduced by restating the policy [16]; and Belief in a Zero-Sum Game — "one person's gain is possible only at the expense of other persons" — a measurable worldview across 37 nations, stronger in lower-GDP and lower-SES contexts, i.e. partly *learned realism* from genuinely scarce environments rather than pure error [16]; both US political camps construe life zero-sum, about different hierarchies [16]. The **identifiable victim effect** (evidence: contested): the famous story is much weaker than its fame. The classic demonstrations are real as sympathy demos — more given to one named "Rokia" than to statistics, and adding the statistics *reduced* giving; the singularity effect (one identified child outpulls eight); even bare determinateness ("victim #4 has been selected") moved donations, while vividness per se showed no significant effect [17]. But the meta-analysis puts identifiability's overall effect at r ≈ .05 across 41 studies with the best-powered studies ≈ 0 [18], and post-meta preregistered tests run null: no overall IVE in Moche & Västfjäll (2021); no support in Maier, Wong & Feldman's far replication of the Rokia studies (ηp² = .000; hypothetical-donations caveat), whose robust-Bayesian reanalysis of the Lee–Feeley meta-analysis also indicates publication bias; and no meaningful effect on COVID health compliance [18]. What remains is small and conditional — larger for a child, photographed, blameless, poverty-framed — a moderator list that doubles as the scam-persona checklist. [[availability-salience]] already carries the r = .05 calibration; charity-fraud and pity-play exploitation runs on fabricated vividness and urgency far more than on any robust identifiability switch.

## Evidence base

The file grade reflects the core Kahneman–Tversky program; members carry their own grades above. Replication status, honestly:

- **Survived cleanly:** the natural-frequency facilitation itself (meta-analytic, 35 papers) [4]; sample-size insensitivity (decades, no replication controversy; scope-restricted to sampling-distribution tasks) [5]; the conjunction fallacy (persists under real bets, clarified wording, and transparent sets) [6]; subadditivity (large support-theory literature, experts included) [8]; prospect-theory probability weighting [9]; money illusion (survey, experimental, and neural convergence) [12]; mental accounting's core non-fungibility (Nobel-cited lab program plus SNAP field data) [13]; survivorship bias (structural — nothing to replicate) [15].
- **Corrected or downgraded by later work:** "people ignore base rates" as a universal deficit → a format- and relevance-dependent tendency [3][4]; the Rottenstreich–Hsee affect-amplification result → failed Many Labs 2 outright (d = 0.74 → −0.08, N = 7,218) [11]; the house-money effect → framing-fragile, mixed in markets [13]; the theatrical Wald anecdote → stylized history (keep the method, drop the theater) [15]; the identifiable victim effect → contested (r ≈ .05, best-powered ≈ 0, post-meta preregistered nulls, and a robust-Bayesian publication-bias signal in the meta-analytic base itself) [18].
- **Limited breadth, graded down inline:** denomination effect — internal replications only; the chips/gift-card/microtransaction extension is mechanism-extrapolation, not a tested result [14]; zero-sum bias — one lab paradigm plus a belief-scale program that measures a different construct [16].
- **Not empirical constructs:** appeal to probability (informal logic; nothing to replicate) and Murphy's Law (folk; observer-side selective memory wearing an adage).
- **Calibration:** the critiques share one through-line — these errors are strongly *format-dependent*: they shrink (though rarely vanish) under natural frequencies, transparent set relations, incentives, and consultation [4][6][7]. That is both the honest limit on "humans are innumerate" and the entire defensive playbook below. The person is a moderator too: numeracy predicts risk comprehension and resistance to framing and denominator tricks across tasks like these [22], and it is measurable (the Berlin Numeracy Test) and trainable — a defensively actionable lever, not a fixed trait [22]. And nearly every lab member here was measured in WEIRD samples; the 37-nation BZSG program is the exception [16].

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **the operator picks the numerator you see, hides the denominator, and chooses the format the numbers arrive in.**

- **Engineered resemblance** — impersonation fraud maximizes prototype-match: spoofed caller ID showing the agency's real number, badge numbers, case numbers, official seals — so "looks exactly like the SSA/my bank" replaces the prior that unsolicited contact demanding payment is almost always fraud ([[scams-fraud]]); the fake bank email looks more bank-like than the bank's. [[cold-reading]] runs the same lever inward: high-base-rate guesses ("an older man with a J name…") feel diagnostic because the sitter never computes the chance hit rate.
- **Detail-stacked narratives** (conjunction) — pig-butchering backstories, psychic prophecies, conspiracy arcs, and scenario-laden investment pitches ("a crash triggered by a China property collapse" outsells "a crash"): each added specific raises felt plausibility while multiplying improbability ([[ponzi-psychology]]). "Too detailed to be made up" is the fallacy stated as a compliment.
- **Tiny-n proof** (sample size) — testimonial ratios with no n ("9 of 10 users"), five-star pages with eleven reviews, "clinically shown" pilot studies, and cherry-picked short track records; the pig-butchering "test" — a $200 gain on a $1,000 trial withdrawal — reads as proof precisely because nothing internally flags how little n = 1 means ([[hope-greed]] supplies the wanting; this file supplies the innumeracy).
- **Jackpot vividness, odds invisibility** (probability neglect) — lottery marketing foregrounds winner imagery and never the published odds — for Powerball, 1 in ~292.2 million ("somebody has to win — could be you") [20][21]; sweepstakes fraud improves on it by making the prize vivid and the probability literally zero; loot boxes hide near-zero drop rates behind showcase animations — after China mandated odds disclosure in 2017, a compliance study found only ~5.5% of top-grossing games disclosing in the most prominent format, on the purchase page itself [20]. The same flatness runs downhill in fear pitches: narrate the catastrophe — the intruder, the crash, the audit — and never state its frequency ([[fear-exploitation]] owns the hot threat-and-rescuer structure).
- **Peril itemization** (subadditivity) — the insurance or security pitch that unpacks "something bad" into ten named dooms, inflating total felt risk above any honest aggregate — people paid more for flight insurance covering "terrorist acts" than for coverage of death from *any* cause [8]. The reverse move packs what you'd object to into one bland bucket: "miscellaneous fees," "other risks."
- **Nominal theater** (money illusion) — a "5% raise" during 8% inflation sold as generosity; "we've never cut anyone's pay" over an unindexed salary; shrinkflation holding the sticker while the unit shrinks; "was $649, now $399" theater where neither number was ever a real transaction price; long-horizon investment pitches quoted in nominal returns [12].
- **Account relabeling** (mental accounting) — casino "free play," match-deposit bonuses, and "bonus bets" relabel money as not-yours so it is wagered harder (widely reported: Ohio and Massachusetts regulators barred "risk-free bet" advertising language as deceptive in 2023 — press coverage, no docket cite on file); refund and overpayment scams manufacture "found money" that feels separate from savings right up until you wire it back; tax-refund-season pitches target the "free money" account; "$1/day" reframes shrink subscriptions below deliberation threshold [13]. Drip-priced partitions are covered in [[framing-anchoring]].
- **Token conversion** (denomination) — chips, gift cards, and in-game currencies strip money of its felt units: value pre-broken, decoupled from payment pain, denominated so bundle sizes never divide into item prices (the 950-gem skin against 1,000-gem packs strands a remainder that forces the next purchase) [14]. Gift cards double as the untraceable payment rail of choice across scam categories — a request to pay anything in gift cards is close to dispositive.
- **Fixed-pie propaganda** (zero-sum) — "every job they take is a job you lose": immigration, welfare, and replacement rhetoric supplies the zero-sum frame and lets the bias fill in the never-specified mechanism ([[propaganda-devices]]); the lump-of-labor fallacy is the economists' name for the flagship case. The commercial version aims the same frame at a rival's gains ([[fud-competitor-tactics]]), and recruitment funnels manufacture zero-sum over unlimited digital goods ("territories are being claimed").
- **Survivor theater** — the MLM opportunity meeting *is* the filter: everyone who quit isn't in the room. "I did it, so can you," with the denominator hidden — MonaVie's own 2007 income disclosure showed fewer than 1% qualified for commissions; in the 2008 Amway UK case roughly 90 of 33,000 participants earned enough to cover business costs; FTC v. Herbalife ended in $200M redress and mandated restructuring [19] ([[mlm-recruitment-scripts]]). Finfluencers post wins and delete losses ([[parasocial-influencer-tactics]]); the classic tipster funnel manufactures a perfect prediction streak by sending half the marks each side and pruning; fund marketing runs the same arithmetic at institutional scale [15]. The visible winners are the marketing; the graveyard is the data.
- **The manufactured victim** (identifiable victim) — charity fraud leads with one fabricated child or veteran: the Cancer Fund of America network raised $187M on "direct financial aid" claims with 1–2.5% reaching any beneficiary [19]; GoFundMe hoaxes script the single photogenic case; romance and grandparent scams run the effect in reverse — the *fraudster* presents as the one vivid victim (stranded, sick child, arrested grandson) ([[pity-plays]]). Calibration: with the honest identifiability effect near zero [18], the fraud lift comes from fabrication, urgency, and trust mechanics; the named face is garnish.

## Ethical use vs exploitation

These heuristics are mostly the mind working: resemblance is diagnostic in stable environments, fast-and-frugal heuristics can match or beat complex models where information is costly [3][4], and honest communicators pull the same levers *for* their audience — a doctor quoting a rare catastrophic risk with the rate attached, natural-frequency risk communication, insurance against genuinely ruinous events, a charity telling one true story next to real program numbers. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **withholds the denominator** (winners shown, entrants hidden), **picks the format that disables computation** (tokens without exchange rates, nominal figures resisting real-terms translation, perils itemized or packed to order), **fabricates the numerator** (the invented victim, the doctored track record), or **punishes the question**. The disclosure test: an honest communicator survives "how many, out of how many — and what happened to the rest?"

## Recognition

- **You can picture the outcome but not the odds** — catching yourself planning the jackpot or rehearsing the disaster; simulation vividness doing duty as likelihood.
- **Typical phrases, hope side:** "Someone has to win." "You can't win if you don't play." "It's due." "Look at all these winners." "I did it, so can you." "Everyone who bought this is smiling."
- **Typical phrases, money side:** "Would you rather pay 3 easy payments of $33?" "That's just 2 coffees a week." "You're playing with the house's money." "It's your bonus — treat yourself." "Biggest jackpot in history." "A 5% raise" (CPI: 8%).
- **Typical phrases, fear and fixed-pie side:** "It only takes one intruder." "Can you afford to risk your family?" "Every job they take is a job you lose." "There's only so much to go around."
- **Detail feels like truth** — a pitch that grows *more* convincing as it grows more specific; "it all adds up"; the story's coherence experienced as its probability.
- **The token latency** — you cannot say, within a couple of seconds, what the thing costs in your home currency: gems, chips, credits, "bonus funds." That latency is the exploit working.
- **Winners visible, graveyard invisible** — inspiration from a stage full of successes with no sensation that anything is missing; absence doesn't feel like anything, which is why this one needs an external prompt.
- **Felt-sense indicators:** the "it just fits" click that arrives before any thought of how common the category is; a handful of outcomes feeling like a verdict; someone else's win registering as your loss with no mechanism you could name; one face with a name outweighing a thousand-person statistic — and the statistic, when added, *cooling* your sympathy.

## Resistance

1. **Ask for the denominator — then the graveyard** ([[verification-rituals]]): "how many, out of how many?" and "of everyone who started, what happened to the rest?" For MLMs, pull the income disclosure statement and read the *median* (usually ≈ $0), not the mean; for funds, demand dead-fund-inclusive performance; for testimonial pages, assume the graveyard is 10–100× the podium. Refusal or irritation at the question is itself the disclosure ([[detection-heuristics]]).
2. **Convert to natural frequencies** — restate any probability claim as "out of 1,000 people like this, how many…": the best-replicated debiasing move in this literature [4], and the arithmetic [[epistemic-guardrails]] already runs on over-detection. Apply it symmetrically — to the pitch you want to refuse and the accusation you're tempted to believe.
3. **Translate every price into one real unit** — annual, post-tax, inflation-adjusted, home-currency: write the cash price next to every token price before buying; multiply the daily reframe back out ($1.30/day = $475/yr); state raises, "deals," and returns in real terms and watch who resists the translation.
4. **Undo the partition** — repack itemized perils ("all causes combined, what's the annual probability — and is it already covered?"); fold "bonus" money into the one ledger the moment it is spendable ("would I bet this if it were cash from my checking account?"); treat the chips-conversion, not the later wager, as the spending decision.
5. **Strip the story to its weakest claim** — price the sparest version of the pitch alone; remind yourself each added detail must *lower* the probability of the whole package; recruit a second head and restate in frequencies — consultation and stakes measurably cut conjunction errors [7].
6. **Pin the modality** — "what probability, by when, in writing — and what do you owe me if not?" A pitch that slid from "could" to "will" has already answered.
7. **Ask what the base rate is** — before resemblance carries a verdict in either direction: for the caller who looks exactly like the fraud department, and for the person who "fits all the signs" ([[epistemic-guardrails]] carries the worked math and its limits).

## Caveats

- **Probability simplification is usually adaptive.** Fast-and-frugal heuristics exist because they work: in stable environments resemblance *is* diagnostic, and simple rules can match or beat complex models [3][4]. Expert intuition is real where valid cues meet rapid feedback (firefighting, chess) and unreal where they don't (stock-picking, long-range forecasting) [3] — grade the intuition by its training environment, not its confidence.
- **Weighting catastrophes heavily can be precaution, not bias.** Insuring against rare, ruinous, irreversible outcomes is rational even at negative expected value; the bias lives in rare *trivial* outcomes (gadget warranties) and in any pitch that punishes the frequency question.
- **Mental accounting is often a functional self-control device.** Envelope budgeting, sinking funds, "don't touch savings," keeping cash in one large bill — the same psychology deployed *for* the agent, and Thaler frames it exactly that way [13][14]. The bias isn't having accounts; it's letting a counterparty write the labels.
- **Zero-sum framing is correct for genuinely fixed pies.** One house in a negotiation, one promotion slot, an auction: locally zero-sum, and reading them that way is calibration. BZSG itself tracks environments where resources genuinely were scarce — a harsher world's lesson misapplied, not stupidity [16]. And distributive concern ("who captures the surplus?") is a legitimate question even inside positive-sum games.
- **Vivid-victim storytelling is how honest charities communicate too.** Communication requires cases, and philosophers genuinely dispute whether statistical lives should always outweigh identified ones. The markers are fabrication, unverifiability, and the story deployed to *block* the denominator question — never vividness itself ([[pity-plays]] carries the tactic-side treatment).
- **Small samples are sometimes all there is.** Updating on n = 3 is rational Bayesianism; the error is certainty. "Suggestive, not settled" is what calibration sounds like, and someone reasoning that way is not biased.
- **Base-rate reasoning is itself weaponizable.** "Statistically, it's probably innocent" is true of a single ambiguous cue and false as an answer to a documented pattern: the base rate sets the prior; documented specifics are the evidence that moves it. Anyone citing this file's math to wave away your timeline is doing rhetoric, not statistics — [[epistemic-guardrails]] holds both halves of that discipline.
- **Seams:** this file owns errors of probability and number *calculation* — priors, denominators, sample sizes, conjunctions, nominal-vs-real, selection filters. [[pattern-illusions]] (the declared distinguished-from) owns pattern *perception* in randomness — streaks, clusters, and the gambler's-fallacy/hot-hand family, including the Miller–Sanjurjo correction that partly rehabilitated the hot hand. [[availability-salience]] owns what comes to mind first; [[framing-anchoring]] owns reference-point placement and drip pricing; [[hope-greed]] and [[fear-exploitation]] own the hot channels these cold errors feed; pennies-a-day sits on the seam with [[time-distortions]], and the risk-feeling family (normalcy, zero-risk, ostrich) belongs to [[risk-misperception]].
