# Reciprocity

> Unrequested favors create debts — and a manufactured debt can be called in for far more than the favor cost. The lever is the discomfort of owing.

## The lever

Humans run a ledger. Gouldner argued the norm of reciprocity — help those who help you; don't injure them — is near-universal across cultures [1]; Mauss's classic anthropology found gift economies built on three binding obligations: to give, to receive, and to repay [2]. The plausible evolutionary substrate is reciprocal altruism: cooperation with non-kin is only stable if debts are tracked and welchers sanctioned [3]. The subjective experience of the ledger is *indebtedness* — a low-grade discomfort that motivates repayment even toward people we don't like, even for "gifts" we never asked for. That last clause is the vulnerability: **the norm fires on receipt, not on request**.

## Evidence base

- **Regan (1971), the Coke study.** A confederate's unsolicited soda roughly doubled how many raffle tickets participants later bought — and in the gift condition, how much participants *liked* the confederate stopped predicting purchases. Obligation overrode affinity [4].
- **Kunz & Wolcott (1976).** Christmas cards mailed to complete strangers drew replies from a substantial share (~20%) — reciprocation without relationship [5].
- **Reciprocal concessions / door-in-the-face.** Refusing a large request makes a smaller follow-up feel like a concession owed: Cialdini's juvenile-center study tripled compliance with a zoo-trip request (17% → 50%) by preceding it with an outrageous ask [6]. Meta-analytically the door-in-the-face effect is real but modest, strongest when the same requester makes both asks for a prosocial cause [7].
- **Small gifts move professionals.** Among 279,669 US physicians, receipt of even a *single* industry-sponsored meal (mean value under $20) was associated with significantly higher prescribing of the promoted brand-name drug [8] — converging with the broader conflict-of-interest literature [9]. Diners tipped more when given a mint with the bill, more still when the mint-giving was personalized [10].
- **Robustness.** Reciprocity is among the most consistently supported influence principles across lab, field, and economic-games literatures [3][11].

## How it's weaponized

The exploit pattern is always: *give something small, unrequested, and visible — then ask.*

- **Commercial openers** — free samples, free trials, free audits, free lunch investment seminars; the timeshare "free weekend" ([[timeshare-playbook]]); the "gift" that opens a [[sales]] pitch. The Hare Krishna flower-then-donation method is the canonical case study [11].
- **Concession theater** — [[door-in-the-face]] sequencing and staged "let me talk to my manager" discounts ([[bad-faith-negotiation]], [[four-square-payment-packing]]): a fake retreat that obligates a real yes.
- **Favor-banking** — bosses and colleagues who perform unrequested favors and later invoke the ledger ("after all I've done for you" — [[guilt-tripping]], [[emotional-blackmail]]); the workplace version often pairs with [[we-are-family-rhetoric]].
- **Grooming and capture** — gifts are a documented stage of grooming sequences ([[grooming-sequence]]); cults and high-control groups open with free dinners, free personality tests, free community ([[cult-conversion-funnel]], [[love-bombing]]); MLMs gift "mentorship" that becomes loyalty debt ([[mlm-recruitment-scripts]]).
- **Fraud** — pig-butchering scams pay out small early "returns" to create both proof and obligation ([[romance-scam-arc]], [[ponzi-psychology]]); loan sharks and traffickers begin with rescue-flavored generosity that converts to unpayable debt ([[escalation-entrapment]]).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Reciprocity is the backbone of cooperation — favors, hospitality, and mutual aid are how relationships and societies function. The line (per [[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation is marked by **calibration to an ask** (gift→request within a short window), **disproportion** (small gift, large ask), **unsolicited delivery engineered to be hard to refuse**, and **ledger speech or resentment** when repayment isn't in the currency the giver wanted.

## Recognition

- A gift or favor you didn't ask for, followed quickly by a request — especially from someone with something to sell or recruit.
- Felt obligation disproportionate to what you received; difficulty saying no *because* you accepted something.
- Public gifting (witnesses raise the social cost of non-repayment).
- Ledger language: "after everything I've done", itemized favors, generosity that gets *invoked*.
- Your refusal of the gift being refused ("I insist") — genuine givers accept "no thanks"; tacticians need the debt planted.

## Resistance

1. **Redefine, don't refuse.** Cialdini's rule: accept genuine gifts gladly, but the moment a favor reveals itself as a compliance device, mentally redefine it as a *tactic* — and the norm releases you: tactics deserve no repayment [11].
2. **Decouple in time.** "Thank you — I never make decisions the same day as a gift or favor." Obligation pressure decays; the engineered ask usually can't wait.
3. **Repay in kind, not in compliance.** A soda-sized debt is repaid with a soda, not a purchase. Cap repayment at the favor's actual size.
4. **Policy shields** ([[boundary-scripts]]): institutional gift rules exist because the effect works on experts who deny it [8][9] — adopt personal ones ("I don't accept gifts from vendors").
5. **Audit the sequence** ([[detection-heuristics]]): unsolicited + calibrated + disproportionate + witnessed = treat as an opener, not kindness.

## Caveats

- **Most generosity is genuine.** The base rate of kindness vastly exceeds the base rate of gift-as-tactic; applying ledger-suspicion to friends and family corrodes exactly the relationships reciprocity exists to sustain ([[genuine-generosity]]: no calibration, no invoice, survives non-repayment).
- **Cultural gift norms vary enormously** ([[cultural-difference]]): refusal can insult; obligation cycles (e.g., gift seasons, hospitality codes) are community fabric, not traps. Judge by local norms both parties share.
- **Indebtedness is healthy in relationships** — mutual, roughly balanced, uninvoiced. The defense posture is for *asymmetric, invoiced, ask-adjacent* giving.
- Single instances prove little ([[epistemic-guardrails]]); the diagnostic is the gift→ask *pattern* across occasions.
