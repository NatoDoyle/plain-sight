# Stereotyping & Essentialism

> Sorting people into kinds is not optional and not a defect — it is how inference works at all, and it runs as a cognitive discount whether or not you approve of it. The exploit is never the sorting. The three moves bolted on afterwards are **inflation** of the difference, its relocation from circumstance into **nature**, and the declaration that it **cannot change**; given a category and an essence claim, the audience's own machinery builds the rest of the case — including the part where the individual in front of them stops counting as an individual.

## The lever

**Stereotyping.** A stereotype is a cognitive structure linking a social category to attributes and applied to a person by virtue of membership. Keep three layers apart, because manipulation lives in the slippage between them: a **stereotype** is a belief, **prejudice** is an attitude, discrimination is a behavior [1]. Allport's founding synthesis already treated categorization as ordinary cognitive economy that prejudice rides on rather than an aberration [1] — and Macrae, Milne & Bodenhausen turned that into a measurement. Participants forming impressions of targets while simultaneously monitoring a prose passage did *better* on the passage when a category label was available: the stereotype is a **resource-preserving device**, and *subliminal* activation bought the same discount as conscious activation [2]. That one finding supplies this file's exploit condition wholesale: anything that raises cognitive load — time pressure, fear, fatigue, information volume — should raise reliance on category-based inference, which is why the pitch lands when you are overloaded ([[cognitive-overload-confusion]], [[manufactured-urgency]]). How a group–trait link gets *built* out of statistically empty evidence is a different mechanism and lives in [[pattern-illusions]] (illusory correlation); this file is about what happens to inference once the category exists.

**Stereotype content is structured, and the structure is about the perceiver's position, not the group.** Fiske's **stereotype content model** puts stereotypes on two dimensions — warmth and competence — with *status predicting perceived competence and competition predicting low warmth*, and four emotions (pity, envy, admiration, contempt) sorting the quadrants; many stereotypes are mixed rather than uniformly hostile, "contrary to antipathy models" [3]. Read defensively this is a map of what a campaign will say about a target *before* it says it, and it says nothing whatever about what any group is like: when a campaign frames a group as a *competitor*, warmth is stripped (the competent-but-cold quadrant — envy, resentment, the "they are organized against us" template); when it frames a group as *low status*, competence is stripped (the warm-but-incompetent quadrant — pity, protection framing, agency removed "for their own good"). A stereotype that sounds like a compliment is still a stereotype. Grade the model `supported`, not settled: three of its four predictions replicated across ten non-US nations, while Koch et al.'s data-driven work — which declined to hand participants the two dimensions — found agency and beliefs emerging spontaneously instead, with communion appearing only as a derived quality [3].

**The accuracy debate, stated straight, because a KB that hides it is lying to its reader.** Jussim, Crawford & Rubinstein write that "stereotype accuracy is one of the largest and most replicable findings in social psychology" [4]. That is a real sentence in a peer-reviewed journal, and Jussim's fuller statement ran in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* — a venue that publishes signed critical commentary alongside the target article, which is the honest place to send anyone who wants the argument rather than the slogan [4]. What is actually measured is **aggregate correspondence**: how well *pooled beliefs about a group's average* track a *measured group statistic* on some specific criterion. Note what is not in that definition: no individual person, and no criterion the perceiver chose after the fact.

The limits come from inside the same literature, and the best single study is one Jussim co-authored, because it carries its own correction. Chan et al. had 3,323 raters in 26 countries estimate age differences in personality traits. The **consensual** stereotype — the pooled estimate — correlated with observed differences at a median r ≈ .75 across six countries; that number is a correlation between a *pooled* judgment and a *group average*, and it says nothing about any individual member of the group and nothing about any individual rater. Any **one** rater's accuracy averaged r = .34, and "not one of the participants was as accurate as the consensual stereotype profile." And magnitudes were inflated even where directions were right: a perceived difference of d = .90 against an actual d = .54, with perception-to-self-report dispersion ratios averaging 1.66 across facets, 1.34 after removing a possible contrast artifact [5]. Wühr et al. found the same shape in a different domain — "accurate in direction, but inaccurate in size," overestimating the real difference in 10 of 17 categories [5]. Three further limits are literature, not editorializing. Whole classes of stereotype are simply wrong: national-character ratings across 49 cultures were reliable and *did not converge* with assessed traits — "unfounded stereotypes," on a paper Jussim later co-signed [6] — and partisan-composition estimates in Ahler & Sood's US samples were off by factors of roughly five and nineteen on the two items they tested, with out-party error the larger [6]. Bordalo et al.'s model explains how a "kernel of truth" and a gross distortion coexist: stereotypes overweight whatever is most *distinctive* of the target group relative to a **chosen reference group**, so they are context-dependent and most distorted precisely when the groups are similar — meaning whoever picks the comparison class picks the stereotype [7]. Dixon adds that where a belief has shaped the opportunity structure being measured, "accuracy" is partly circular [7]; and Williams, Sng & Neuberg found race-based inference *disappearing* when participants were given information about the target's ecology — the category had been standing in for a variable [7].

**Aggregate accuracy licenses nothing about an individual.** That is the part that matters defensively and it has to be said plainly. Distributions overlap; a true mean difference is compatible with this specific person sitting anywhere in the range; and where individuating information about the person exists, it is the individuating information that should dominate — Jussim's own abstract lists "whether stereotypic (vs. individuating) information can be used rationally in person perception" as an open *controversy*, not a permission slip [4]. A base rate about a group is not evidence about the person in front of you. The manipulation use of this literature is exactly that leap performed out loud: a group statistic produced as though it settled an individual case, or as though an empirical claim about a distribution were a normative claim about treatment. Both steps are unlicensed by anything in the accuracy corpus. And the reciprocal test is cheap: the person quoting group statistics at you almost never accepts the same method applied to a group they belong to.

**Implicit associations**, **implicit stereotypes**, **stereotypical bias** — split the grade, and this file's honesty case runs opposite to the one above. The **phenomenon** — automatic, unintended activation of category-linked content — is `supported` and does not depend on any one instrument. Devine's classic dissociation is the anchor: high- and low-prejudice people are "equally knowledgeable of the cultural stereotype," and when conscious monitoring is precluded, both produce stereotype-congruent readings of ambiguous behavior [8]. Correll's shoot/don't-shoot studies are the keystone: bias in the task tracked *perceived level of the cultural stereotype and contact*, **not** personal racial prejudice, and appeared at equivalent magnitude in Black and White community participants [8]. Knowing a culture's associations is near-universal; endorsing them is not — which is exactly what makes this a mechanism file rather than a file about bad people. (The shooter literature is messier than its reputation: reaction-time effects, error effects, nulls and reversals all appear, and the meta-analysis found only very small correlations between cultural stereotypes, personal beliefs, and shooter bias [8].)

The **measure** — the IAT, used to score a person — is `contested`, and the numbers are not close. Oswald et al.'s meta-analysis: "IATs were poor predictors of every criterion category other than brain activity, and the IATs performed no better than simple explicit measures" [9]. The dueling estimates are best quoted from the IAT camp's own reply, which prints both: r̄ = .236 for their meta-analysis against r̄ = .148 for Oswald's, with the difference "due mostly to their use of different policies for including effect sizes" [9] — roughly 5.6% and 2% of variance. Temporal stability is the killer for individual use: implicit measures averaged r = .54 across occasions against r = .75 for explicit ones, *despite comparable internal consistency* — so this is genuine instability in what is being picked up, not task noise [10]; child samples run lower still (.34–.48) [10]; and roughly 70% of the variance in a person's score is not shared with that same person's score on another day. Then Forscher et al.'s network meta-analysis — 492 studies, 87,418 participants — found implicit measures can be moved but weakly (|ds| < .30), that procedures "generally produced trivial changes in behavior," and, decisively, that "changes in implicit measures did not mediate changes in explicit measures or behavior"; nine interventions in a separate program all reduced implicit preferences immediately and *none* survived a delay of hours to days [11]. Note the author lists: Nosek and Devine are on that meta-analysis. This is the field correcting itself, not an outside attack. The IAT's own principal architect concedes in a flagship review that "the theory needed to confidently guide those applications remains insufficiently developed" [12]. The synthesis this file adopts is Payne's: most systematic variance in implicit measures is *situational*, so the instrument behaves as **a poor thermometer for a person and a decent barometer for a place** — county-level aggregates track historical and present structural inequality even as individual scores wobble [12].

**An individual IAT score must not be used to diagnose a person** — not by an accuser, not by a trainer, not by the person themselves. That rule is both the science and the defense against the measure being weaponized in either direction: a score with ≈.54 stability that does not mediate behavior change cannot carry a verdict about a person, in either direction, no matter who is holding it up [9][10][11].

**Prejudice** is the attitude layer — the evaluative, affective one — and the SCM already shows it is not simple antipathy: pity and envy are prejudices too, with different downstream harms [1][3]. Its best-evidenced counter is intergroup **contact**. Pettigrew & Tropp's meta-analysis of 713 independent samples from 515 studies found contact typically reduces prejudice, tested for and rejected selection and publication bias in *their* corpus, found effects generalizing beyond the immediate partner to the out-group as a whole, and — correcting the textbook telling — found Allport's optimal conditions to be "an interrelated bundle rather than … independent factors" that produce *greater* reduction when present but are **not essential** [13]. Mediation runs mainly through anxiety reduction and empathy rather than knowledge, which is why arguing people out of prejudice with facts underperforms [13]. The honest shrinkage: restricted to random assignment *with* delayed outcomes, Paluck, Green & Green found only 27 treatment arms, an overall estimate of 0.39 SD that falls to **0.20 once disability studies are excluded** and 0.25 (95% CI [0.08, 0.42]) for ethnic, racial, religious and immigrant targets, with a file-drawer signature (smaller-SE studies reporting weaker effects) and the coverage gap stated bluntly: "no studies of ethnic or racial contact include participants over 25 years of age" [14]. The wider prejudice-reduction field carries "troubling indications of publication bias," with 76% of 418 experiments testing light-touch interventions of unclear durability [14]. And contact is not a universal solvent: negative contact predicts increased prejudice more strongly than positive contact predicts reduction [14]; a randomized Iraqi soccer intervention improved behavior toward teammates but did not generalize to other settings or shift attitudes [14]; a Nigerian field experiment moved *behavior* without moving attitudes and found the segregated condition made participants worse [14]. The durable outlier is non-contact: ten-minute perspective-taking conversations at the door reduced transphobia for at least three months and survived exposure to counterarguments [15]. Defensively, the important fact is structural — contact and outside information are the first things a capture environment removes ([[isolation-tactics]], [[information-asymmetry]], [[cults-high-control]]).

**Essentialism** is the most manipulation-relevant member of the family, and the one this file exists to name. *Psychological essentialism* is the intuition that a category's members share a deep, hidden, identity-conferring nature that causes their observable features and cannot be changed by circumstance — Medin & Ortony's **essence placeholder**: people reason *as if* an essence exists without being able to say what it is, and the placeholder does the causal work regardless of its content; Gelman documents that the reasoning appears early and untaught [16]. Applied to social categories it delivers naturalness, immutability, discreteness, inductive potential ("knowing they're X tells you a lot"), and inherent cause ("they're like that because of what they *are*") [16]. Two honest qualifications first. Essentialism is **not one belief**: Haslam, Rothschild & Ernst found two independent dimensions — natural-kind-ness and entitativity/reification — and concluded that "essentialism is not a unitary syndrome of social beliefs, and is not monolithically associated with devaluation and prejudice" [17]; their follow-up test of Allport's claim "received partial support but had to be qualified in important respects," associating essence beliefs strongly with anti-gay attitudes but only weakly with sexism and racism, finding no cognitive style consistent across stigmatized categories, and finding some *anti*-essentialist beliefs associated with prejudice too [17]. So do not write "essentialist beliefs cause prejudice." But the *experimental* evidence is stronger than the correlational trait evidence, and that asymmetry is precisely what a manipulation KB cares about: an operator does not need to find essentialists, they can **manufacture essentialist framing in an ordinary audience**. Making essentialist information salient raised prejudice and in-group bias, *particularly among people already holding chronic essentialist beliefs* — that study selects on the trait, so it does not by itself carry the ordinary-audience claim [19]. The ordinary-audience claim rests on the other two, both run in unselected samples: framing group membership as biologically determined increased acceptance of racial inequities and cooled interest in cross-group interaction, "generally independent of racial prejudice" [19]; and inducing essentialist beliefs raised endorsement of social hierarchies, which mediated the effect on attitudes — in Black *and* White participants alike [19]. A cross-lagged panel study adds non-experimental evidence for the harmful direction: essentialism predicted later willingness to act against an out-group, with no significant effect in the reverse direction for the hostile case (the pro-social case ran the other way, behavior shaping essentialism) [19]. Temporal precedence in a panel is not an experiment, and the file does not upgrade it to one. Essentialism is also *demand-driven*: entitativity beliefs mediate the path from dispositional need for closure to prejudice, which means essences become more attractive under exactly the threat, uncertainty and time pressure that campaigns manufacture ([[certainty-needs]], [[fear-exploitation]]) [19]. The delivery mechanism is grammatical and demonstrated: hearing **generic** language about a *novel* social category ("Zarpies are afraid of ladybugs") — as against the same content scoped to an individual ("this zarpie is") — installed essentialist beliefs in four-year-olds and adults, and inducing essentialist belief in parents made them produce more generics when talking to their children. A closed loop, running on grammar [18]. A story emphasizing ethnic essence raised inter-ethnic bias in six-year-olds [18]; and essentialist reasoning about locally salient categories is selectively learned and *attenuated among children in integrated schools* [18] — the machinery is aimed by the environment, which is why segregated institutions function as an input rather than only an output. The chain the file names, with at least one experiment behind every arrow: **generic language → essence → immutability ("they can't change") → entitativity ("they act as one") → moral exclusion → dehumanization → licence.** At the end of it, blatant dehumanization is measurable and consequential: it predicts consequential attitudes *above and beyond prejudice*, is reliable over time, "spikes immediately after incidents of real intergroup violence," and predicts support for torture and retaliatory violence [20]. Carry the dispute with it: Over's seven challenges argue that out-group members are not actually treated like nonhuman entities and that the causal link from perceived sub-humanity to harm is doubtful, with a published reply defending conditional versions [20]. The KB's posture is the same one it takes on illusory correlation: the vermin/disease/infestation register is a reliable propaganda signature and a genuine escalation marker, and its recognition value does not depend on winning the mechanism argument.

**Out-group homogeneity bias** and the **cross-race effect** are resolution deficits, not attitudes, and they are the file's cleanest evidence that this machinery is not made of hostility. Park & Rothbart demonstrated homogeneity perception symmetrically — each side saw the other as more uniform, sorority members rated their own group as more internally dissimilar than outside judges did — and, critically, the effect appeared across items varying in *desirability*, so it is not derogation; the mechanism showed up in memory, with people retaining finer-grained subordinate attributes of in-group members [21]. Then the meta-analytic correction that kills the lazy version: "strong support for the effect is obtained across all measures in natural-group settings, [but] no consistent effect is observed in minimal-group settings" [21]. Arbitrary labels are enough to produce in-group *favoritism* (owned by [[unity-ingroup]]); they are **not** enough to produce "they're all alike." Homogeneity perception is built from real differential exposure and encoding, which is why segregation and incident-only media diets worsen it and individuated exposure repairs it — and why these two mechanisms genuinely dissociate and deserve separate files. It also reverses: minority-group members under threat show *in-group* homogeneity, "we are all one" as the mirror image [21]. The perceptual floor is early: fMRI adaptation shows out-group homogeneity effects emerging in visual cortex tuning for faces, not only at the judgment stage [21]. Which brings the **cross-race effect** (**other-race effect**, own-race bias). Meissner & Brigham's meta-analysis — 39 articles, 91 independent samples, nearly 5,000 participants — found a **mirror effect**: own-race faces produced both a higher proportion of hits *and* a lower proportion of false alarms, with significant own-race bias in discrimination accuracy and in response criterion [22]. The double error is what makes it forensically dangerous: other-race faces are simultaneously harder to recognize correctly and easier to falsely "recognize." Two facts must travel with it. It is **not** an attitude: the meta-analysis found no evidence that racial attitudes drive it, and contact moderates it only weakly (r ≈ −.13 in the original, −.15 in a 2022 update with twenty more years of data, strongest for childhood contact and when contact is experimentally manipulated) — so "I have lots of friends from that group" is not the immunity people assume [22]. And it is learned: newborns show no own-race preference while three-month-olds do, making it a product of early exposure rather than an innate mechanism [22]. Applied, this is the clearest case in the whole file of category-level perception producing confident, sincere, wrong testimony about a specific person with the state's power behind it — the Innocence Project's DNA-exoneration record puts eyewitness misidentification in the large majority of its cases, and cross-racial identification is a listed estimator variable in the National Research Council's assessment, which recommended blind lineup administration, contemporaneous confidence statements and jury instructions [23]. New Jersey's *Cromedy* and *Henderson* line is the standard example of courts operationalizing it (US/NJ) [23].

## Evidence base

The frontmatter grade reflects the established core — that category membership drives inference, the out-group homogeneity effect in natural groups, and the cross-race effect. Members carry their own grades:

- **Category-based inference as cognitive economy — `established`.** Dual-task demonstration of the load discount, including under subliminal activation [2]; converging with the automatic-activation dissociation [8].
- **Stereotype content model — `supported`.** Nine samples in the original, three of four predictions replicated across ten non-US nations, and a live challenge that the two dimensions may be an artifact of handing participants two dimensions [3]. The exploit-relevant reading survives either map: campaigns move a target along a *status* axis and a *threat* axis, and the emotion that follows determines whether the audience is recruited to neglect, resentment, or elimination.
- **Stereotype accuracy — real, and `contested` in interpretation.** Consensual estimates track measured statistics; personal accuracy is far weaker (r ≈ .34, with no individual matching the consensus); magnitudes are inflated by a third to two-thirds; national-character and partisan stereotypes fail outright; and the criterion may be partly a downstream measurement of the discrimination [4][5][6][7]. Grade the *findings* supported and the *inference people draw from them* contested — and never print an accuracy correlation without its individuating-information limit in the same breath.
- **Automatic association (the phenomenon) — `supported`.** Not carried by the IAT: Devine's equal-knowledge dissociation and Correll's knowledge-not-prejudice moderator do the work [8]. The shooter-task literature itself is mixed, with nulls and reversals and very small correlations to cultural stereotypes and personal beliefs [8].
- **The IAT as an individual-level measure — `contested`, and this file will not launder it.** Predictive r̄ between .148 and .236 depending on inclusion policy; no better than explicit measures on Oswald's criteria; test–retest ≈ .54 (children .34–.48) despite fine internal consistency; movement in the measure does not transfer to behavior and does not mediate behavior change; interventions decay within days; the construct itself has at least five unresolved definitional disputes [9][10][11]. `taxonomy/bias-codex-index.md` flags "IAT-based implicit association claims" as an entry this KB does not launder; this file is where that promise is paid.
- **Implicit-bias *training* as a counter — weak to `contested`.** Diversity-training meta-analysis finds the largest effects on reactions and cognitive learning, smaller ones on behavioral and attitudinal learning, with attitudinal effects decaying; a preregistered field experiment with an active placebo control concluded that one-off trainings "are unlikely to be stand-alone solutions" [11]. It is not listed among this file's counters, deliberately.
- **Contact as a counter — `supported`, and thinner than its reputation.** 713 samples in the correlational meta, robust to that meta's own bias tests, generalizing, with Allport's conditions as enhancers rather than gates [13]; but 27 randomized-with-delay arms, 0.39 SD dropping to 0.20 without disability studies, 0.25 for ethnic/racial/religious/immigrant targets, zero randomized studies of adult racial prejudice, a file-drawer signature, an asymmetric negative-contact effect, and generalization failures in the two best field experiments [14]. The widely quoted mean effect size from the 2006 meta could not be verified against the primary by any sweep and is **not reproduced here**.
- **Essentialism — `supported`, with the correlational and experimental halves graded separately.** As a stable trait it is multidimensional and only patchily linked to prejudice, target-specific, and not a consistent cognitive style [17]. As an *induced state* it reliably raises intergroup bias, hierarchy endorsement and acceptance of inequity, in adults and in children, in members of advantaged and disadvantaged groups alike, with cross-lagged evidence for the harmful direction [19]. Generic language is a demonstrated causal delivery route [18].
- **Blatant dehumanization — `supported`; the *causal* dehumanization hypothesis — `contested`.** Measurable, incremental over prejudice, temporally reliable, event-triggered, predictive of support for violence [20]; and challenged as an account of *why* harm occurs, with a published exchange in the same journal [20].
- **Out-group homogeneity — `established` for natural groups, with two boundary conditions that matter.** Absent in minimal groups (which favors information-encoding accounts over motivational ones), and reversed for minorities under threat [21].
- **Cross-race effect — `established`, the best-evidenced entry here.** Meta-analytic mirror effect, converging developmental and neural evidence, forensic corroboration, no failed-replication literature located; only the *mechanism* (perceptual expertise vs. social categorization) is disputed, and that dispute touches neither the recognition nor the counter. The frequently quoted odds ratios for hits and false alarms sit in the paper body behind a paywall; one sweep verified them through a law-review pinpoint cite and two could not, so they are **not printed here** — the qualitative mirror effect is the load-bearing claim anyway.
- **Discrimination as behavior — `established`, and independent of every contested measure above.** Meta-analysis of hiring field experiments: since 1989 white applicants received on average 36% more callbacks than African American applicants and 24% more than Latino applicants, with *no change* across 25 years [12]. This is the fact that makes "implicit bias was debunked, so discrimination isn't real" a non sequitur.
- **Not imported: stereotype threat.** The performance-decrement literature has substantial replication and publication-bias problems. Nothing in this file rests on it; the phenomenology in Recognition (representation weight, self-monitoring) is reported as felt experience, not as a performance claim.

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **the operator supplies the category and the essence claim, and the target's own inference machinery supplies the verdict** — then the group statistic, the homogeneity, and the sincerity of the resulting perception get presented back as evidence.

- **Scapegoating and dehumanization campaigns.** **MOVE:** a group is assigned a fixed hostile nature; individual acts are recoded as expressions of that nature; the frame flips to self-defense, which converts aggression into protection ([[propaganda-devices]], [[disinformation-playbooks]], [[propaganda-politics]]). **TELLS:** generic bare plurals replace named actors ("they are…", never "some of the ones I dealt with"); a vermin/disease/infestation/contamination register, which does the essentialist work silently because vermin cannot be negotiated with; **no exit** — no described behavior would move a member out of the category; both content axes moving at once, the target simultaneously incompetent-and-subhuman *and* a powerful coordinated threat, a contradiction the campaign tolerates because the two claims do different jobs [3]; and timing — dehumanizing content surges immediately after a violent incident, which is also when your own certainty is least trustworthy [20]. **COUNTER:** the individuation test (can the speaker describe a member as an ordinary individual? can they state what evidence would falsify the claim?); the substitution test (swap in a group you belong to and see whether the sentence becomes recognizable as hate); treat the metaphor register as the leading indicator, since dehumanizing vocabulary reliably precedes dehumanizing policy; and impose a deliberate delay on your own conclusions during a post-atrocity window. When those escalation signs are present and aimed at a group you belong to, treat it as a physical-safety and record-keeping matter as well as an epistemic one — keep a dated record of incidents and know your local reporting routes ([[documentation-practices]]). The record that this escalates is judicial, not theoretical: the ICTR's Media Case convicted radio and newspaper principals of direct and public incitement to commit genocide (trial judgement 2003; appeals judgement 28 Nov 2007, which left Barayagwiza at 32 years, Nahimana at 30 and Ngeze at 35 — the trial sentences those figures were reduced *from* are not evidenced here, the appeals judgement being a scanned document with no retrievable text layer, so this file states the appellate sentences and not the reduction) [20].
- **Radicalization funnels.** **MOVE:** graduated commitment inside an information-sealed environment; categories introduced as descriptive, then essentialized, then moralized, then framed as existential ([[radicalization-pipeline]], [[cult-conversion-funnel]]). **TELLS:** every explanation reduces to one group's essence; the in-group is granted full internal variety while the out-group is uniform; disagreement is re-attributed to the dissenter's category rather than answered; a "forbidden truth" available only inside; the metaphor register escalating over weeks. **COUNTER:** keep outside contact and outside information sources deliberately alive — isolation is not incidental to these dynamics, it is the removal of the one intervention with meta-analytic support [13][14] ([[isolation-tactics]]); and watch for the exact sentence where a *descriptive* claim becomes an *essential* one. That transition is the hinge and it is visible in the text.
- **Accuracy-laundering.** **MOVE:** a group statistic is produced as though it settled an individual case, or as though a fact about a distribution were a warrant for treatment. **TELLS:** "it's just statistics"; "I'm only being realistic"; "you have to admit there's a reason for the stereotype" — followed by a conclusion about a *person*; the comparison class never stated; magnitude never stated; the same speaker refusing the method when it is aimed at a group they belong to. **COUNTER:** ask *compared to what?* — the reference group is the free parameter that sets the whole result [7]; ask *how big, and how much overlap?* — direction is usually right and size usually inflated [5]; ask *what is the category standing in for?* — supplying the confounded variable can make the inference vanish [7]; and refuse the leap from aggregate to individual out loud, since that is the step doing all the work.
- **Homogenizing the out-group.** **MOVE:** "they all think alike / they're all in on it," converting a diverse population into a single coordinated agent, which is the grammatical precondition for collective blame and for most conspiracy narratives. **TELLS:** the asymmetry test — the speaker grants their own side full internal variety and treats the other as interchangeable; one member's act narrated as the group's behavior, then as the group's nature. **COUNTER:** name the asymmetry; ask for three ways members of the accused group differ from one another; note that this bias is largely an *information* deficit [21], so the fix is exposure to individuals, not argument.
- **Bias accusations as a lever — both directions, and the file must carry both.** **MOVE A:** an unfalsifiable charge — "the test says you're biased; your denial is the bias" — built on a measure with ≈.54 stability that its own field's meta-analysis says does not predict behavior [9][10][11]; structurally identical to any "resistance proves the diagnosis" move ([[therapy-speak-weaponization]], [[darvo]]). **MOVE B:** the critique as a licence — "implicit bias was debunked, therefore discrimination isn't real" — a non sequitur, since the critiques indict an instrument and an intervention, not the audit-study and administrative record [12]. **MOVE C:** compliance theatre — mandatory training marketed on a literature that says the training does not change behavior, functioning as liability documentation ([[institutions]], [[organizational-defenses]]). **TELLS:** a claim that cannot be failed and cannot be passed; a score treated as a verdict about a person; substance never engaged. **COUNTER:** keep the three layers apart — belief, attitude, behavior — and insist on the one that is measurable; a specific behavioral complaint with a record beats a psychometric accusation in either direction ([[documentation-practices]]).
- **Affinity fraud's out-group-facing half.** [[unity-ingroup]] owns the in-group trust exploit and the SEC record; this file contributes only what that file does not: the pitch's use of stereotype *content* about the mark's own community ("our people understand X, unlike them") to buy credibility, and the under-reporting dynamic where victims stay silent to avoid confirming an out-group stereotype about their community — which is why these frauds run long ([[affinity-fraud]], [[shame-leverage]]).
- **Cross-race misidentification in wrongful convictions — structural, usually with no manipulator at all.** **MOVE:** a system that treats confident identification as strong evidence, operating on a perceptual mechanism with a known race-linked error asymmetry, while suggestive procedure inflates the confidence. **TELLS:** a cross-racial identification; a showup or a lineup where the suspect stands out; an administrator who knows who the suspect is; witness confidence *rising* between first identification and trial; no contemporaneous record of confidence at the first identification. **COUNTER:** blind administration, contemporaneous confidence statements, cross-racial jury instructions and the NRC best-practice set (US) [23]; and for an individual witness, knowing that your own cross-race identification is less reliable than it feels, and saying so on the record rather than being talked up to certainty.
- **Transmission to children.** **MOVE:** the essence claim arrives in ordinary adult-to-child speech rather than as anything recognizable as a campaign — generic language about a category installs essentialist belief in four-year-olds and adults about groups they have never heard of, and induced essentialism in parents produces more generics in return, so the belief propagates through family and classroom talk without anyone intending one [18]. **TELLS:** generics about a group in adult-to-child speech ("they are…", "that's what they do") where the same speaker would use scoped description about anyone else; "that's just how they are" offered as the answer to a child's question about a person or an event; no exit clause — nothing anyone in the category could do or become would move them out of it. **COUNTER:** restate the claim in scoped form in front of the child ("some of the people at that shop", "the ones I worked with") — the scoped form does not install the belief and the generic does, which makes this the cheapest correction available [18]; name the generic aloud, so a child hears that the grammar itself was doing the work; and treat the structural counter as the real one, since essentialist reasoning about locally salient categories is measurably *attenuated among children in integrated schools* [18] — which makes a segregating institution an input whether or not it means to be.

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Category-based reasoning is not merely tolerable, it is often correct and sometimes obligatory: epidemiology, actuarial risk, screening programs, and safety statistics are explicitly about *rates in populations* and are stated as such. Role inference is ordinary social life — asking someone a question because of their job, their department, or where they are standing. And a group-level finding honestly reported is knowledge, not prejudice. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **converts a rate into a nature** (a claim about a distribution restated as a claim about a hidden essence), **applies the aggregate to a person while individuating information is available and being ignored**, **hides the reference class** that generated the "statistic" in the first place, **forecloses exit** (no evidence and no behavior could move anyone out of the category), or **moves from an empirical claim to an entitlement to act**. The disclosure test is unusually clean here: an honest source can state its comparison group, state the magnitude and the overlap, say what would change its mind about a specific person, and does not lose the argument when you ask it to restate the generic in scoped form — "some", "the ones I've met", "in that particular workplace". The person laundering prejudice through statistics will not do any of those four things, and will change the subject when asked.

## Recognition

Category inference is universal; a single marker means very little. The diagnostic is the **conjunction** — generics *plus* immutability *plus* entitativity *plus* individuating information failing to land *plus* hostile affect. Anything less is ordinary human sloppiness.

**On the receiving end.**

- You are answered as a **representative** rather than as yourself — "what do you people think about…", "how do [group] feel about this", "you'd know about that".
- **Your individuating information stops landing.** You supply a specific fact about yourself; the reply re-anchors to the category. Two or three rounds of that is the diagnostic zone, and it is far more informative than any single remark.
- **Compliment-shaped exclusion** — "you're not like the others", "you're one of the good ones". Warmth granted by exception, which preserves the generalization about everyone else instead of updating it.
- **Pre-loaded roles** — being greeted as the assistant, the junior, the help, the interpreter, the one who will handle the emotional work.
- **The immutability move** — your disagreement, or your having changed, treated as impossible or as further proof: "that's just how they are", "she'll go back to it", "it's in the blood".
- **Asymmetric attribution** — your failures explained by your nature, your successes by circumstance or by the category. [[attribution-errors]] owns this pattern and its evidence.
- **The grammar tell, and it is the highest-value one:** talk about your group is *generic* — bare plurals and timeless present — never scoped. "They are…" and "the ones I've dealt with…" are a real epistemic difference, not a politeness one, and the generic form is a demonstrated causal route into essentialist belief in listeners [18].
- **Typical phrases:** "No offence, but you know how they are." · "I'm not saying all of them, but…" (followed immediately by a generic) · "You're different." · "They all stick together." · "They don't value [x] the way we do." · "It's in their culture." (culture used as a soft essence — inherited, causal, immutable) · "It's just statistics." · "You have to admit there's a reason for the stereotype."
- **Felt-sense indicators:** a specific fatigue — arguing from behind, having to establish your individuality before an ordinary conversation can start; the flinch before speaking, pre-editing against how it will be read as evidence about everyone like you; the sense that your ordinary mistakes go into a ledger; being *read before you speak*, the reaction arriving ahead of the information; relief in in-group settings disproportionate to how much you actually like the people there.
- **Escalation signs:** descriptions shift from behavior to nature ("they did X" → "they *are* X"); vermin, disease, contamination, infestation, flood or swarm metaphors appear; the group is described as coordinated and hidden; remedies are declared impossible ("they can't be reformed", "it can't be integrated") — immutability is the precondition for arguing that only removal works; children are being addressed; and a triggering incident is being used as a launch window [20].

**Catching your own category inference.** Nobody opts out of categorizing. The question is what happens in the two seconds afterwards, and these are prompts for a check, not evidence of a defect.

- **The certainty tell.** You feel you *know* something about a person you have not observed. The feeling of knowing arrived before any evidence; that is the category doing the work.
- **The load tell.** You are tired, rushed, frightened or drowning in information, and your judgments have gone crisp and confident. That crispness *is* the resource discount [2]. High confidence under high load is the signature.
- **The exception tell.** "She's not like most of them." You have just preserved the generalization rather than updating it.
- **The asymmetric-detail tell.** You can name three ways people in your own group differ from one another and none for the other group [21].
- **The proxy tell.** Ask what the category is standing in for — where a situational or environmental variable is confounded with it, supplying that variable can dissolve the inference entirely [7].
- **The grammar tell, in your own mouth.** Count your own generics.
- **The magnitude check.** Even granting a real difference: how big, how much overlap, and where in that distribution is *this specific person*? [5]

## Resistance

1. **Seek individuating information and let it dominate** ([[manipulation-audit]]). This is the one counter on which the accuracy literature and its critics agree, and the numbers back it: any single person's group-level stereotype is a weak instrument (mean accuracy r ≈ .34, and not one of the 3,323 raters matched the pooled estimate) [5], while the person in front of you is directly observable. Where individuating information exists and is being set aside for a category prior, that is the move to name.
2. **Ask "compared to what?"** The reference class is the free parameter: stereotypes overweight whatever distinguishes the target group from a *chosen* comparison, so changing the comparison changes the "statistic" [7]. Anyone who will not state their comparison group has not made a claim you can evaluate.
3. **Correct composition with real numbers.** Misperceptions of who is actually in a group run wild — factors of five and nineteen on the two items tested — and supplying the actual composition made partisans see the other side as less extreme and feel less socially distant [6]. This is a cheap, tested intervention and one of the few in this literature with a clean result.
4. **Watch the grammar — yours and theirs.** Generic statements install essentialist belief; scoped statements do not [18]. Restating a claim in scoped form ("some of the ones I met", "in that workplace") is the fastest available de-essentializing move, and it costs nothing.
5. **Refuse the aggregate→individual and the empirical→normative leaps out loud.** Both are unlicensed by anything in the accuracy corpus [4][5], and naming them is more effective than disputing the statistic, which is often the trap.
6. **Prebunk the campaign shape** ([[inoculation-prebunking]]): the generic plural, the essence claim, the no-exit clause, the metaphor register, the post-atrocity launch window. Knowing the moves in advance is the cheapest defense the KB has, and the dehumanization surge is *event-triggered*, which makes it predictable [20].
7. **Keep contact and outside information alive — with the caveats.** Contact is the best-evidenced prejudice counter available, and it is what capture environments remove first [13][14] ([[isolation-tactics]]). But it is not "just meet people": negative contact predicts increased prejudice more strongly than positive contact predicts reduction, and generalization beyond the specific setting is unreliable [14]. Sustained, equal-status, cooperative, institutionally-supported contact is the version with evidence; the individually actionable version is usually *breaking your own information isolation* rather than a contact program.
8. **Perspective-taking conversation beats argument.** Contact works mainly through anxiety reduction and empathy, not through knowledge transfer [13], and the most durable single result in this literature is a ten-minute non-judgmental exchange about the other person's own experience, which held for three months and survived counterarguments [15].
9. **Procedural fixes where perception is the problem.** For identification specifically: blind lineup administration, a contemporaneous confidence statement at first identification, and jury instruction on cross-racial identification (US) [23]. Procedure beats good intentions because the effect is perceptual and does not respond to attitude.
10. **What does not work, stated plainly:** taking an IAT to find out about yourself (retest ≈ .54, and moving the score does not move behavior) [10][11]; one-off implicit-bias training [11]; assuming friendships with members of a group inoculate your face memory (contact moderates the cross-race effect at r ≈ −.15) [22]; and arguing someone out of a category judgment with data alone [13].

## Caveats

- ***Ordinary-categorization* is not prejudice, and this is the file's first and largest fence.** Sorting the world into kinds is how inference works; a KB that treats every category-based expectation as bias would fire constantly and be ignored. The pejorative sense requires something *added*: magnitude inflation, essence attribution, immunity to individuating evidence, or hostile affect. Noticing a category is not the offense.
- **Noticing a real group-level statistic is not a stereotype in the pejorative sense — and is also not evidence about an individual.** Both halves. A rate is a claim about a distribution; "they're like that by nature" is a claim about a hidden cause. Aggregate accuracy is real [4][5]; overlap is nearly total for most socially salient comparisons; and where the belief has shaped the outcome being measured, the "accuracy" is partly circular [7]. Report the finding, refuse the leap, and do not let either move be smuggled past you.
- **An IAT score must not be used to diagnose an individual** — including *against* one, and including by the person themselves. Retest ≈ .54 with fine internal consistency, predictive r̄ between .148 and .236, no incremental value over explicit measures on Oswald's criteria, and no mediation from score change to behavior change [9][10][11]. Read the instrument the way Payne's synthesis suggests: informative about places and populations, not about persons [12].
- **And the critique is not a licence either.** "Implicit bias was debunked, so discrimination isn't real" does not follow: the callback gap in audit studies is behavioral, large, and unchanged across 25 years, and owes nothing to any contested lab measure [12].
- **Accusations of bias are themselves weaponizable.** "You're only saying that because of X" ends a substantive argument by relocating it to the speaker's category, and "your denial proves the bias" is an unfalsifiable claim wearing a psychometric costume — the severity rules in [[epistemic-guardrails]] govern, and the KB's standing pattern applies: a claim that cannot be failed cannot be passed. Adjacent misuses to recognize: bias claims deployed as a shield against specific, checkable feedback ([[tone-policing]], [[bad-faith-argumentation]]); as social punishment where a single ambiguous incident carries the whole weight; and the [[darvo]] variant in which an accused party reframes accountability as bias against *them*. Say this symmetrically: reflexive "concept creep" charges are equally used to dismiss genuine reports, and the microaggression construct in particular is a live scholarly dispute the KB does not adjudicate.
- **The cross-race effect is not a moral failing and says nothing about the perceiver's attitudes.** It is a perceptual-expertise and memory effect, present in three-month-olds and absent in newborns, weakly moderated by contact, and explicitly *not* driven by racial attitudes in the meta-analysis [22]. Someone confusing you with another person of your group is far more likely to be running an encoding limitation than expressing hostility. Naming it as racism will usually be both wrong and counterproductive; naming it as a documented perceptual effect — and, in a courtroom, as grounds for a jury instruction — is right and useful.
- **Out-group homogeneity is largely an exposure artifact, is absent in minimal groups, and reverses for minorities under threat** [21]. Someone who knows few members of a group and generalizes sloppily may be under-informed rather than hostile; and "they're stereotyping us because we're a group" is not automatically diagnostic.
- **Essentialism is not one thing and is not uniformly prejudice-linked.** Two independent dimensions, target-specific associations, no consistent cognitive style across stigmatized categories, and some *anti*-essentialist beliefs also tracking prejudice [17]. In some contexts, immutability beliefs about a stigmatized trait have been argued to *reduce* blame; the per-dimension directions in the strongest study of this were not verifiable for this file and are not asserted. Do not treat every essentialist-sounding sentence as a red flag.
- **Bias is not bigotry, and this file will not be used to sort people into the good and the bad.** The keystone finding is that task bias tracked cultural stereotype *knowledge* and contact, not personal prejudice, and appeared at equal magnitude in members of the targeted group [8]. Naming a pattern in someone's speech is not evidence about their character — which is also why moral condemnation is a poor intervention here and structural or procedural change is a better one.
- **Innocent look-alikes for "I'm being stereotyped":** ordinary role inference; legitimate actuarial, epidemiological or screening reasoning that is explicitly about rates and explicitly not about anyone's nature; being disliked for a specific reason you would rather not hear (category attribution is an available and self-protective explanation for negative feedback); a single clumsy remark, since people misspeak; and the cross-race effect above. Single instances are genuinely ambiguous and this file says so rather than resolving them for you.
- **Never a taxonomy.** This file describes the machinery of category inference and contains no claims about what any real group is like. Every content-level example here is either a fictional category ("zarpies"), a structural description of a content quadrant with no group named, or historical propaganda described *as propaganda*. A passage that could be quoted approvingly by someone building a prejudice campaign would be a defect in this file, not a finding.
- **Base rates for the KB's own detection.** Category-based inference is universal and mostly benign, so one marker means almost nothing. The signal is the conjunction, and this file's Recognition section should be read as a prompt for a second look, not a verdict ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).
- **Seams.** [[pattern-illusions]] owns stereotype *formation* from statistically empty evidence (illusory correlation, Hamilton & Gifford, and the mechanism dispute) — this file owns what the category does to inference once it exists. [[unity-ingroup]] owns the "us" half: in-group favoritism, minimal groups, identity fusion, forced teaming, affinity fraud's trust exploit; the clean empirical hinge is that minimal groups reliably produce favoritism but **not** out-group homogeneity [21], so the two mechanisms genuinely dissociate. [[attribution-errors]] owns the intergroup attribution asymmetry (and grades the "ultimate attribution error" honestly). [[cognitive-overload-confusion]] owns the load conditions this file's discount runs on; [[certainty-needs]] owns the closure motive that makes essences attractive. [[fluency-familiarity]] routes familiarity's effects on *category* judgments here. Codex routing for the eight rows in this cluster: [[bias-codex-index]].
