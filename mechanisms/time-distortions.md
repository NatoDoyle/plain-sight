# Time & Future Distortions

> You cannot stand at another point in time. You can only imagine one from here — and the imagining runs on your current state, your current mood, and a story about how this case will go, none of which will still be true when the future arrives. The exploitable consequence is not that you value the present. It is that **whoever chooses the moment you decide from has already chosen most of the decision**, and the cost of that choice lands somewhere you cannot see from where you are standing. None of this is a claim that people in hardship choose worse — the 61-country income null and Mann's 60% are in Caveats, and being squeezed is not the same as being fooled.

## The lever

**One failure, several registers.** Everything in this file is a version of the same problem — simulating yourself or the world at a different time: a future self in a different state (**projection bias**, **impact bias**), a future project at a different scale (**planning fallacy**, **present bias**), or a past era through a warmed lens (**declinism**). That unification is this file's framing, not a finding; the members were studied by separate literatures with separate methods and they grade separately, from a 12-million-person *Nature* result down to one entry with no located research base at all. Read the per-entry grades in Evidence base before using any of it, and note the direction of the honest correction: **three of these entries are materially weaker than their bias-codex billing**, and the file says so rather than smoothing it.

**Hyperbolic discounting / present bias — the family's strongest member, and a model dispute worth reporting honestly.** The regularity: the implicit discount rate falls with delay, so preferences reverse as a delayed reward comes close — the thing you sincerely planned to skip becomes the thing you take, at the moment it is available. Ainslie's matching-law work supplied the psychology and Laibson's **quasi-hyperbolic** (β–δ) model — weight the present at 1 and every later period at βδ^t — supplied the formalization economics actually uses [1]. Two things must be said with it. First, the field's own founding review is a skeptical document, not a coronation: Frederick, Loewenstein & O'Donoghue catalogue the "'anomalies' … inconsistent with [the discounted-utility model's] theoretical predictions," stress "the **spectacular variation in implicit discount rates across studies**," and argue time preference is a bundle of impulsivity, anticipation and visceral influence rather than one parameter [1]. Second, the *magnitude* depends enormously on what is being deferred. The best meta-analysis puts β for **money** at **0.938 [0.905, 0.972]** after correcting for selective reporting — roughly a 6% premium on now, a real but small deviation — against **0.750 [0.643, 0.857]** for nonmonetary rewards, with no evidence of selective reporting there, and an ordering of real consumption > real effort > money [2]; an independent meta over 220 estimates from 28 articles using a different elicitation protocol reaches the same two conclusions, real but heterogeneous and inflated by "modest selective reporting in the direction of over-reporting present bias" [2].

That split is not a technicality, it is the whole defensive point. Money is fungible and arbitrageable, so a lab choice between money now and more money later is partly a *financial* judgment: convex-time-budget designs found "reasonable levels of both discounting and curvature and, surprisingly, **dynamically consistent time preferences**," the same authors showed apparent present bias may reflect "a direct preference for certainty" (now is certain, later carries counterparty risk), and the reconciliation study confirms "very limited time inconsistency in monetary choices" while finding "considerably more present bias in **effort**" — which also predicted demand for binding commitment devices [3]. The current authoritative review, co-authored by the man who wrote the β–δ model, concedes that money-earlier-or-later tasks reflect factors "distinct from underlying time preferences" [3]. Model form is unsettled too: heuristic models comparing absolute and percentage differences out-predicted both exponential and hyperbolic discounting out of sample [4], and a 2024 experiment found most participants preferring to complete tasks *immediately even at greater effort* — precrastination, the opposite sign — with "quasi-hyperbolic discounting, anticipatory utility, fixed costs, decision costs, and cost-of-keeping-track … all unable to provide a reasonable account" [4]. So: write "people frequently over-weight an immediate benefit against a diffuse later cost, and predictably regret it"; do **not** write "people discount hyperbolically." The functional form is a modelling choice. What travels is the anomaly itself — tested in 61 countries with 13,629 participants using local currencies and value standards, choice anomalies came back "highly consistent" [5].

**Planning fallacy — solid core, domain-conditional edges, and one actionable counter.** Named by Kahneman & Tversky, whose corrective procedure (take the outside view) is older than most of the evidence for the bias [8]. Buehler, Griffin & Ross's verified findings are the load-bearing ones and they are structural rather than numerical: "(a) People underestimate their own but not others' completion times, (b) people focus on plan-based scenarios rather than on relevant past experiences … (c) people's attributions diminish the relevance of past experiences" — plus two results that are counters in disguise, the bias "**eliminated** for Ss instructed to connect relevant past experiences with their predictions" and observers *over*estimating others' completion times [8]. The frequently-quoted numbers from that paper's thesis study are not printed here; no sweep read them in the primary [8]. Now the corrections. There is **no meta-analytic point estimate** for the planning fallacy; the nearest synthesis is a *Psychological Bulletin* review that qualifies the canonical story hard: "underestimation was more frequently reported than overestimation in studies from the **engineering and management** literature. However, this was not the case in studies from the **psychology** literature," the recurrent small-tasks-overestimated/large-tasks-underestimated rule may be "a **statistical artifact** caused by random error," and "many studies fail to distinguish" hours-of-work predictions from delivery-date predictions [9]. The actionable defense is **reference-class forecasting** — forecast from the distribution of outcomes of comparable past actions instead of from the story about this one [10]. Its record is good but not unanimous: an evaluation on real project data found "RCF indeed performs best, for both cost and time forecasting" against Monte Carlo and earned-value methods, while a longitudinal case study of a DKK 23.6bn megaproject reports the contrary, that RCF "does not lead to more accurate cost estimates" (n = 1, but a real published dissent) [10].

The manipulation-relevant fact about this entry is that it is frequently *not a bias at all*. Across 258 transportation projects worth US$90 billion, cost estimates used to decide whether projects should be built were "highly and systematically misleading," and "underestimation **cannot be explained by error** and is best explained by strategic misrepresentation, that is, lying" [10]. Reference-class forecasting is prescribed against both, precisely because it never consults the promoter's story [10]. For this KB the consequence is sharp and runs against the charitable instinct: when an estimate is wrong in a direction that reliably benefits the estimator, **"planning fallacy" is the generous reading and should not be the default one** — and **this file's reading** of the domain split in [9] (the fields with money and approval on the line are the ones that skew) points the same way. That reading is an inference drawn here; Halkjelsvik & Jørgensen report the split and draw no such conclusion from it [9].

**Impact bias — the durability half, and a three-round fight the KB must not report as two.** In forecasting emotion, people are said to overestimate both the **intensity** and the **duration** of their reaction; **durability bias** names the duration half, and its proposed mechanism is *immune neglect* — forecasters omit their own rationalization, reframing and meaning-making, which will blunt the blow [11]. Then the dispute. Levine et al. argued a procedural artifact: participants "**accurately predicted the intensity of their feelings about events**" and overestimated only when asked to predict feelings *in general* and later report feelings without reference to the focal event — and "clarifying the meaning of the forecasting question significantly reduced overestimation" [12]. Wilson & Gilbert replied that the challenge rests on four specific errors (omitted contradicting studies, faulty classification, collapsed conditions, miscoding) and that "when these errors are corrected, their claim is clearly not supported" [12]. Levine et al.'s same-issue rejoinder — the round the KB previously did not carry — reports that the pattern survives the recommended recoding, claims new analyses **ruling out focalism** as the alternative explanation, and titles itself "the impact bias is both dead and alive" [12]. [[framing-anchoring]] owns focalism and the focusing illusion and already cites this exchange; its hedge is inherited here verbatim in spirit — *the core is safe, precise magnitudes are not* — with round three added rather than a second version of the argument started. Levine et al.'s **register distinction** — forecasts about how you will feel *about a specific event* are reasonably accurate, while forecasts about your *general emotional state at a future time* are overestimated — is the part this file relies on, and **Wilson & Gilbert do not concede it**: their reply holds that once the coding errors are corrected the challenge "is clearly not supported" [12]. Treat it as the contested reading, not a settled convergence. It is used here because it is exactly the register a promised or threatened future is sold in — with the hedge travelling attached to it.

**Projection bias — the best field evidence in the file, and the only member that survived the skeptic sweep without a mark.** The formal claim: people "exaggerate the degree to which their future tastes will resemble their current tastes," understanding qualitatively that tastes change but underestimating the magnitude — with the authors themselves naming mandatory **cooling-off periods** as a welfare-improving remedy [13]. The evidence is revealed-preference data at scale rather than scenario studies: catalog orders of cold-weather items are over-influenced by the weather on the order date, such that a 30°F colder order-date temperature raises the return probability by **3.95 percent** in the authors' own units (percent vs percentage points is not disambiguated in the abstract); and across more than 40 million vehicle transactions, the choice to buy a convertible or a four-wheel-drive is "highly dependent on the weather at the time of purchase in a way that is inconsistent with classical utility theory," with the authors explicitly unable to fully exclude salience as a rival channel [13]. Magnitudes are modest shifts in choice and return rates, not wholesale self-misprediction. [[emotional-flooding]] owns the **hot–cold empathy gap** — the in-the-moment failure to simulate the other state — and this file owns only the forecasting register: *the hot–cold gap is failure to simulate; projection bias is building a plan, or buying a thing, as if the current state were permanent.* Loewenstein's own illustration is the tactic stated by a ten-year-old on a hike: *"you should have asked me when we were resting back on that rock"* [13].

**The weak members, graded down rather than dressed up.** Two entries arrive with more billing than evidence, and they fail in opposite directions — one has no construct behind it, the other has a very strong descriptive result and a weak causal one.

- **Pessimism bias** is not a distinct construct. Its nearest empirical anchor, depressive realism, meta-analyses to a "small overall depressive realism effect (Cohen's *d* = −.07)" in which **both** groups remain positively biased (dysphoric *d* = .14, nondysphoric *d* = .29) and the "realism" concentrates in studies lacking an objective standard of reality and using self-report symptom measures [14]; a well-powered preregistered replication "found no evidence that depressive symptoms relate to illusory control or to overconfidence" [14]. [[optimism-overconfidence]] owns optimism bias, which is the documented general tendency; treat pessimism as a mood-, domain- and target-conditional moderator on it, note that the mirror construct has its own artifact critique [14], and carry the manipulation-relevant version, which is not a standing bias at all but an **installed forecast** about one specific thing: the target's life without the manipulator. **Grade: `contested`** — treat it as a moderator, not a mechanism.
- **Declinism** has a very strong descriptive result and a weak causal one. Across archival and original data (n = 12,492,983), people in at least 60 nations believe morality is declining, have believed it for at least 70 years, and attribute it both to individuals aging and to successive generations — while "people's reports of the morality of their contemporaries have **not** declined over time, suggesting that the perception of moral decline is an illusion," produced by biased exposure to information plus biased memory for information, and **attenuated, eliminated or reversed** when respondents are asked about people they know well or people who lived before they were born [15]. A related result finds the denigration is trait-specific and tracks the rater's own standing ("we view kids these days unfavorably, especially on traits at which we excel, partly because we have a biased view of the past") [15], and decline rhetoric is a documented component of populist attitudes [16] — but the experimental test failed: nostalgia correlated with populist attitudes across three preregistered studies while "a manipulation of nostalgia could not establish a causal effect" [16]. So decline framing is best described as **a framing that finds an audience, not a technique that manufactures one**. [[memory-self-editing]] owns rosy retrospection and fading affect and already ceded declinism here; this file owns only the projection of that warmth onto *eras*, and that extension is an inference, not a demonstration. **Grade: `established` for the descriptive illusion, `supported` for the mechanism, `contested` for the causal-persuasion claim.**

**And the members that carry almost nothing.** Four entries route here from the codex; between them they support no inference about an individual.

- **Time-saving bias** is real and narrow: drivers "underestimate the time saved when increasing from a low speed and overestimate the time saved when increasing from a relatively high speed," because time saved is a difference of reciprocals rather than a linear function of speed added; it survives outside hypothetical scenarios, including a driving-simulator study where "the perception of the time elapsed while driving does not eliminate the time-saving bias," and it has a small debiasing programme attached [17]. It is also a two-group literature (an exact-phrase index query returns twelve hits) with no independent large-scale replication, no effect size worth quoting, and — importantly — **no documented commercial or interpersonal exploitation**; the applied work is road-safety debiasing, i.e. protective in intent [17]. **Grade: `supported`, thin and single-cluster, with no exploitation literature and no quotable effect size.**
- **Well-traveled road effect** has **no located research base at all**: exact-phrase and construct searches across Crossref, OpenAlex and Europe PMC returned no primary study, no review and no originating author, and the entry appears to circulate by inheritance from bias-list compilations [18]. It is printed here only so the codex index can explain its absence; the real adjacent literature is retrospective duration estimation, which belongs to [[memory-retrieval-quirks]] and [[fluency-familiarity]]. **Grade: none — a verified null. Not graded, not used, and nothing in this file certifies it.**
- **Appeal to novelty** (*argumentum ad novitatem*) is an informal-logic label with **no psychological research base** under that name, and the skeptical point cuts against the entry's own premise: there is no general "newer is better" tendency in humans, and the better-evidenced general effects (mere exposure, neophobia) run the other way [19]. It is a rhetorical move that works in specific commercial contexts, not a bias people carry. **Grade: `n/a` (informal logic, not a measured effect).**
- **Pro-innovation bias** is a category error as usually listed: Rogers coined it as a bias in **diffusion research itself** — the assumption that an innovation should be adopted by everyone, rapidly, without modification or rejection, so that non-adoption is treated as a defect rather than a judgment — and the strongest empirical work on it is a selection problem in scholarship, showing that studying only widely-adopted policies leads researchers to "overestimate the impact of geographic diffusion pressures and policy attributes, and to underestimate the importance of professional associations" [19]. There is no lab paradigm and no effect size; the useful companion finding is that non-adoption has its own structure as **innovation resistance** rather than being an absence of adoption [19]. **Grade: `supported` as a critique of research and institutional decision-making; not established as an individual cognitive bias.**

## Evidence base

- **Frontmatter mismatch, stated rather than hidden — and it runs downward, not upward.** The file carries **`supported`**, which is the honest ceiling. The lead member and first alias (hyperbolic discounting / present bias) is `supported` as a regularity and `contested` as a model; the unification offered in The lever is this file's framing rather than a finding, so there is no established core construct for a higher grade to attach to; and `evidence:` is machine-readable, so an alias hit on "well-traveled road effect" — which has **no research base at all** — must not return a file stamped `established`. The exception runs the other way and is graded inline: declinism's *descriptive* result is `established` [15], as is the cross-national generality of intertemporal-choice anomalies [5], but those are exceptions within the file, not the basis for the file's grade. One entry is `contested`, one is `n/a`, and one is a verified null. Never quote this file's frontmatter as a grade for a specific entry; use the bullets below.
- **Present bias — `supported` as a behavioral regularity; `contested` as a model, and the standard money-based lab measure is substantially discredited.** Two independent meta-analyses with different protocols agree it is real, heterogeneous, and inflated by selective reporting, with β ≈ 0.94 for money against ≈ 0.75 for nonmonetary rewards [2]; the money paradigm fails under better elicitation and may be measuring certainty preference [3]; heuristic models out-predict discounting models [4]; and there is an opposite-signed phenomenon (precrastination) the standard story ignores [4]. The anomalies replicate across 61 countries [5]. Do not print a β figure as *the* present-bias number, and do not say the target's discount rate "is hyperbolic."
- **Planning fallacy — `supported`, with domain fences.** The core asymmetries (own vs others; scenario thinking; past experience discounted; observers overestimate) are from the seminal paper's own abstract [8]. But direction of error is domain-conditional, the small/large pattern may be a regression artifact, and no pooled effect size exists [9]. In commercial and political settings **strategic misrepresentation is a live competing explanation**, and the systematic one-directionality is its signature [10].
- **Impact bias — `supported`, magnitude actively disputed across three published rounds** [11][12]. The duration (durability) half fares better than the intensity half, and the intensity half is the part attacked as a question-interpretation artifact [12]. Consistency requirement: [[framing-anchoring]] carries the same exchange for focalism; the two files must not drift, and this file adds the 2013 rejoinder rather than restating the argument.
- **Projection bias — `supported`, and it survived the skeptic sweep intact** — no failed replication and no artifact critique located. Large-N field evidence [13]; honest limits are that the designs are quasi-experimental, salience is not fully excluded by the authors themselves, magnitudes are modest, and lab versions overlap heavily with the hot–cold empathy gap owned by [[emotional-flooding]] [13].
- **Pessimism bias — `contested`, and best treated as a moderator rather than a mechanism.** Depressive realism is small, method-dependent, and leaves both groups positively biased [14]; the "sadder but wiser" finding failed a preregistered replication [14]. The KB carries the *induced* version (a forecast installed about the target's alternatives), which is a targeted-expectation manipulation, not a standing bias.
- **Declinism — `established` for the descriptive claim, `supported` for the mechanism, `contested` for the causal-persuasion claim.** The scale and cross-national reach of the illusion finding are unusual for a codex entry [15]; the biased-exposure-plus-biased-memory mechanism is the authors' own account and its two moderators are falsification conditions [15]; the nostalgia–populism *association* is preregistered and robust while the *manipulation* failed [16]. The label "declinism" itself has almost no dedicated primary literature — the entry is assembled from adjacent findings, and this file says so.
- **Time-saving bias — `supported`, thin and single-cluster.** Replicated beyond scenarios into a simulator, with a debiasing line, but concentrated in two research groups, small citation footprint, no effect size, and **no documented exploitation** [17]. Its everyday extensions (speed upsells, "10× faster" pitches) are this KB's inference, labeled as such.
- **Well-traveled road effect — no research base located** [18]. Not graded, not used. **Appeal to novelty — `n/a` (informal logic)** [19]. **Pro-innovation bias — `supported` as a critique of research and institutional decision-making; not established as an individual cognitive bias** [19].
- **Applied bridges, labeled.** The consumer-credit material below is regulator-documented in its own right [6][7]; the *attribution* of those designs specifically to present bias is the FCA's own behavioural unit's language, not this file's extrapolation [7] — but the interpersonal applications (state-timed asks in relationships, durability claims at the exit door, decline framing inside families and groups) are **mechanism-level extrapolations with no direct study**, and the weight for them sits with the tactic files.

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **the benefit is placed where you are standing and the cost is placed where you cannot see** — later, in a state you are not currently in, or in an era nobody can check.

- **Deferred-cost consumer credit, and the tells a regulator has already written down.** The FCA's behavioural unit found buy-now-pay-later journeys "play into consumers' behavioural biases," naming "**present bias** when presenting only the benefits of deferred payments before a sale and not making the potential implications as clear," and concluded these biases "may – **by design** – make it more likely that consumers make impulsive decisions that are not in their best interest" [7]. Recognition, not technique: the total is hard to find and the instalment impossible to miss ("pay £150 now or £50 for 3 months and it sounds better at the checkout," in a user's own words); a credit product is styled as a payment method so that people classify it with debit cards rather than borrowing; and the counterparty's real customer is the merchant, paid for conversion — "anecdotally we have been told some BNPL providers are telling retailers they can increase their sales by up to 30%," with US regulators quoting lenders' own merchant-facing claims of 41–85% higher average order values [7]. The clean personal tell that the interest-free frame has stopped being true: paying a 0% instalment with a 20% credit card, a pattern most prevalent among younger consumers and the most deprived geographies [7] — a distribution that tracks who has no slack, not who has no sense. **None of this bullet is a claim that people who use these products choose worse: across 61 countries lower-income individuals were not significantly more present-biased [5], and about 60% of payday borrowers accurately predict their own repayment [6]. Being squeezed is not the same as being fooled.** Counter: state the all-in total in one sentence, out loud, before anything else; say "this is a loan"; check the exit path before the price. Structural counters exist and are worth asking for — Sweden's rule that credit options cannot be presented before debit options online [7]. [[probability-blindspots]] owns the arithmetic half (pennies-a-day, the total nobody computes) and [[four-square-payment-packing]] owns the instalment-repackaging close; this file owns only the *temporal* half.
- **Short-term credit, without the condescension.** Administrative data: over 80% of payday loans are rolled over or followed by another loan within 14 days; half of all loans sit in a sequence at least ten loans long; and for more than 80% of multi-loan sequences the last loan is the same size or larger than earlier ones — the escalation tell, from the regulator's own numbers [6]. And the finding that keeps this honest: about 60% of borrowers accurately predict how long repayment will take, which "directly contradicts the oft-stated view that substantially all extended use of payday loans is the product of lender misrepresentation or borrower self-deception" [6]. Both are true. Most people know the shape of the deal; the product is structured so that the minority who mispredict carry it. **Being squeezed is not the same as being fooled**, and a file that confuses them will lose the reader who has actually taken one.
- **The act-now close.** [[scarcity-urgency]] and [[manufactured-urgency]] own the deadline itself — the countdown, the expiring discount, the FOMO — and this file owns only why a deadline is so effective on a *deferred-cost* offer: it keeps the decision inside the window where the benefit is present and the cost is not. The tell that separates them is ownership of the clock: a real constraint has a reason outside the seller's control, a manufactured one has only a timer, and both are checkable ([[dark-patterns-social-urgency]] documents timers that reset on reload).
- **Timelines quoted as forecasts when they function as bids.** The renovation, the agency estimate, the treatment plan, the "you'll be earning within 90 days." Tells, in recognition voice: the number comes from a *story about this case* and never from a *count of similar cases*; asking "what did the last ten of these actually take, and what was the spread?" produces discomfort, deflection or reasons this one is different; every surprise costs you and none saves you; and contingency is treated as pessimism or disloyalty. Counter: ask for the reference class rather than the plan [10] — a counterparty who cannot or will not produce base rates has told you what kind of number it is — and contract against delivered milestones rather than against a schedule.
- **Future-faking, which is this mechanism in interpersonal form.** [[future-faking]] owns the tactic; what this file supplies is why it is paid for in advance. A vivid imagined future is exactly the register in which forecasts are least accurate — general emotional state at a future time, not feelings about a specific event, a register distinction Wilson & Gilbert dispute rather than concede, so treat it as the contested reading [12] — and the promise arrives with a *scene* and no *schedule*. Tells: the pitch has a first day and no second month; "give me six months and I'll have changed" is offered where a checkable present-tense change is available and cheaper; the timeline moves each time it is reached. Counter: the reference class for a personal promise is the **previous promises**, written down with their dates ([[documentation-practices]]).
- **Asks timed to a state that will not last.** Commercially this is documented at scale — cold-weather goods bought on cold days and returned later, convertibles on sunny days [13]. Interpersonally the recognizable shape is a commitment whose *duration* far exceeds the *state* it was obtained in: the agreement taken during a reconciliation high, the enrolment during a grief spike, the signature at the emotional peak of an event, the contact schedule agreed while depleted because you cannot summon the version of you who will mind. The tells are that the counterparty chose or controls the timing, that "you feel it now, don't you?" is offered as evidence about the future, and — the sharpest one — that a cooling-off interval is resisted, since a cooling-off period is the **source literature's own prescribed remedy** [13]. Counter: re-decide on an ordinary Tuesday, and treat inability to tolerate a 48-hour gap as information about the offer.
- **Durability claims in both directions.** "You'll never feel this good again" sells the upside ([[timeshare-playbook]], [[hope-greed]]); "you'll never get over this," "no one else would put up with you," "you'd never manage on your own" sells the downside and is standard in controlling relationships ([[isolation-tactics]], [[coercive-control]], [[financial-abuse]]). Both are forecasts of your emotional future delivered with certainty by someone with a stake in the answer, and both omit the ordinary adaptation clause. The evidence-backed reply is that people adapt and that forecasts of general future feeling are systematically overstated [11][12] — but see **Safety notes** below, which govern this bullet: **this is never to be used to argue down a fear about danger**, and the right reference class is people who have actually left ([[no-contact-exit-planning]], [[dv-safety-planning]]).
- **Decline framing, political and domestic.** The public version pairs an idealized, uninspectable past with a named culprit and a restorer ([[propaganda-devices]], [[radicalization-pipeline]], [[cult-conversion-funnel]]: "the world out there has rotted; in here it is safe"). The domestic version points the same move inward — "you used to be so much better before you started listening to them," "this family used to be close" — where the better earlier version being invoked is reliably the more compliant one. Tells: the good era recedes when you ask for a year; no metric is offered and offered metrics are dismissed; the decline is *total*, with nothing acknowledged as improved. Counters come from the finding itself: ask about **people you actually know**, which attenuates or reverses the perception [15], and ask for the year and the number. Calibration the file must keep: the causal-persuasion claim failed its experimental test [16], so decline rhetoric finds an audience rather than manufacturing one — and some decline is real (see Caveats).
- **Hype-cycle selling.** Pro-innovation framing makes adoption inevitable and non-adoption a defect in you; appeal to novelty supplies the chronology in place of an argument. The cleanest tell in the family is the **category-to-instance switch**: you ask about the specific token, product, fund or vendor and the answer returns to the technology class. Others: every case study is a success and there is no failure list — an *absence*, not a refusal; critics are characterized (luddite, incumbent, "not technical") rather than answered; the timeline is imminent and keeps moving; the champion's stake is undisclosed until asked; and "the only risk is not being in," a sentence that is always false. Counters: separate the category bet from the instrument out loud and see whether the conversation survives; ask for the **rejection case** — who is this wrong for? — which is the question Rogers said the literature stopped asking [19]; ask for the failure base rate, since the bias is a survivorship problem before it is anything else [19]. Shame at non-adoption routes to [[shame-leverage]]; note that [[fud-competitor-tactics]] is the mirror move, manufacturing doubt about the alternative rather than inevitability about the offer, and that both are answered the same way — by asking what checkable claim is actually being made.
- **Speed and time-saved pitches — flagged as the KB's own inference.** No documented exploitation of the time-saving bias was located [17]. The defensible use is self-defense arithmetic: any pitch whose value is "this saves you time" deserves the calculation, because intuition about time saved is wrong in a known direction and is most confident where it is most wrong — and because a speed ratio quoted where the bottleneck is elsewhere ("10× faster") invites you to compute a saving the task structure will not deliver.

## Ethical use vs exploitation

The same levers carry ordinary honest life. Credit exists to smooth genuinely lumpy expenses against reliable future income, and instalments are how most people buy a boiler. Deadlines are frequently real (*genuine-deadline*), and urgency is then information. Estimates from honest people are optimistic as a matter of course, not as a tell. Enthusiasm is a precondition for building anything, and sincere over-optimism is far more common than fraud. Anticipated feeling is how motivation works at all. Commitment devices — the standing order, the pre-booked class, the app you deleted — exist *because* projection bias is normal and people know it about themselves, and someone helping you commit in advance may be doing you a service. Nostalgia is usually grief, and grief for a genuinely lost world is not a cognitive error.

The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]) is procedural and unusually easy to test here: exploitation **chooses your moment** (the ask timed to a state the counterparty selected, with a cold-state review refused), **hides the total** (the instalment shown, the all-in number unavailable, the category of the product misstated), **substitutes a story for a base rate** (a timeline with no reference class, a forecast of your feelings with no distribution behind it, a golden age with no year), or **defines the evidence set by outcome** (only successes shown, no failure list, non-adoption pathologized). The disclosure test: an honest counterparty can say the total in one sentence, produce what happened to the last ten comparable cases, name who this is wrong for, and grant you 48 hours without the offer changing character. None of that costs an honest seller anything.

## Recognition

These are prompts for suspicion, not diagnostics. Every tendency in this file is close to universal, so **detection has to run on the engineering — who chose the timing, who benefits from the forecast, whether the base rate is available — never on the presence of impatience, optimism or nostalgia.**

- **"Future me will deal with it."** A conscious split in which the future self is imagined as a better-organized stranger with more money, more discipline and more time. Its partner: the cost feels abstract while the benefit feels textured — you can picture the sofa in the room and cannot picture month 14 of the payment plan.
- **The deal changes character overnight**, without any new information arriving. Also: it felt different at 11pm than it did at 9am, and you talked yourself out of noticing.
- **Relief at signing that is disproportionate to what was gained** — the relief is about ending the deliberation, not about the terms.
- **"Yes, the last three took six months, but this one is different because…"** — and the "because" is a feature of *this* case rather than a reason the reference class does not apply. Related: being asked for best/expected/worst case and finding all three clustered together.
- **A vivid image of the arrival and none of the following Tuesday.** The ring, the keys, the badge, the group welcoming you — with no second month in the picture, and no date attached to the feeling.
- **"I don't know why I agreed to that,"** said sincerely and repeatedly about the same *kind* of agreement — the signature of decisions harvested in one state and enforced in another.
- **Grief presented as analysis.** A real sense of loss borrowing the grammar of a factual claim about the world, with the speaker positioned as the route back.
- **Standing on a platform watching a train leave.** The felt sense of hype: social, not analytical. The emotion is FOMO; the argument is a chronology.
- **Typical phrases (seller and operator side):** "Nothing to pay today." · "Four interest-free payments." · "First month free." · "Take it home today, sort the paperwork later." · "This price is only good while you're on the call." · "Just give me a rough date, I won't hold you to it." · "How long *would* it take if everything went right?" · "You said two weeks." · "Imagine how you'll feel when…" · "You'll regret this for the rest of your life." · "You'll never find anyone who understands you like I do." · "Let's agree the principle now and sort the details later." · "You said you were fine with it." · "Things were better before." · "Nobody has any respect any more." · "They don't build them like they used to." · "You're going to get left behind." · "The only risk is not being in." · "Still using that?"
- **Structural tells, which are the actual diagnostics.** Can you state the all-in total in one sentence? Is the deadline attached to anything outside the seller's control? Was this estimate built from a count of past cases or a story about this one? Does the forecast about your future happen to require you to stay? Did you choose this moment, or did they? Is there a list of the ones that failed? Does the good era have a year and a number attached? Is a 48-hour gap tolerable to the other side — unless the constraint is real, dated and independently checkable (*genuine-deadline*)?
- **Escalation signs:** each instalment or renewal the same size or larger than the last [6]; the timeline reset a third time with a new reason; cooling-off periods argued against on principle; forecasts about your life delivered with rising specificity and no supporting facts; the "before" era invoked more often and dated less precisely.

## Resistance

1. **Insert a state change between the decision and the commitment.** This is the counter the source literature itself prescribes [13], and it is the one move that works across every entry in the file: decide it again on an ordinary Tuesday, hungry decisions re-made full, resolute decisions re-made tired. Two fences. The pause must be **yours** — regulators' mandated cooling-off periods did not change 14-day payday renewal rates, because a pause that expires back into the same offer is theatre [6]. And a counterparty who cannot tolerate the gap has told you the offer depends on your current state — unless the constraint is real, dated and independently checkable (*genuine-deadline*).
2. **Convert to the total, in currency, across the realistic number of periods** — including the renewal you will probably make. This is the best-evidenced counter here: reinforcing the adding-up dollar cost of rollovers reduced subsequent payday borrowing by 11% over four months, with the authors themselves "agnostic as to the overall sufficiency of better disclosure" [6]. Real, and small. Write the number down; APR alone is disclosed correctly and uselessly.
3. **Name the product out loud.** "This is a loan." A large share of BNPL users had not made that classification at all, associating the product with payment technologies rather than credit [7], and making the classification is most of the defense.
4. **Ask for the reference class, not the plan** [10]. "Of the last ten of these, what actually happened — the distribution, not the best one?" Use it on timelines, on investments, on "who should *not* buy this," and on your own projects.
5. **Borrow the observer's eye.** Observers overestimate others' completion times while the person doing the work underestimates [8] — so ask someone with no stake to predict your timeline and take their number, and be aware that you are the observer when it is someone else's project.
6. **Force the past into the room, and unpack the task.** Explicitly connecting relevant past experience to a prediction *eliminated* the bias in the original study [8], and breaking a task into its sub-steps reduces underestimation [8]. Before agreeing to a date, write down what the last comparable thing actually cost you.
7. **Precommit at the account level, not the moment level.** Present bias in effort predicts genuine demand for binding commitment [3] — which is empirical warrant for spending caps, removed saved payment methods, card blocks and standing appointments agreed while cold. You are not weak for needing them; the demand for them is itself a documented, rational response.
8. **Answer durability claims with base rates, not argument.** "How long did I actually feel that way last time?" and, where the forecast is about leaving, a reference class of people who left rather than a story from the person you would be leaving [11][12]. Route to services, not to debate ([[no-contact-exit-planning]], [[cult-exit-support]]).
9. **Against decline framing, ask for the year and the metric — then use the finding's own moderator.** Set aside "people today" and name the people in your own life who have behaved badly this year and the ones who have behaved well; the illusion is attenuated, eliminated or reversed exactly there [15].
10. **Against inevitability framing, split the category from the instrument and ask for the rejection case and the failure list** [19]. "I might believe the technology and still think this specific deal is bad" is a sentence worth saying out loud to watch what happens to the conversation.
11. **Write the state down at the time** ([[documentation-practices]], [[manipulation-audit]]). The reason a contemporaneous note beats memory here is that both ends are corrupted — the forecast by your current state, the retrospect by fading affect ([[memory-self-editing]]). A dated line about what you wanted and why converts a feeling into a record you can check against the next offer.
12. **What does not work, plainly:** knowing about these effects; legislated pauses that expire into the same choice [6]; APR-style disclosure on its own [6]; being generally skeptical; and arguing anyone — including yourself — out of a fear about physical danger by citing affective forecasting.

## Safety notes

**Forecasts about physical safety are out of scope for everything in this file.** Nothing in the adaptation or affective-forecasting evidence licenses anyone — a counterparty, a helper, an AI, or the reader — to discount their own fear of a dangerous person. The durability findings concern general emotional state after ordinary life events [11][12]; they say nothing about the risk of being harmed. **"Your fear about leaving is just impact bias" is a forbidden inference here**, and post-separation danger is real ([[dv-safety-planning]]).

The lift-out risk is specific and worth naming. The durability-claims material above sits next to the DV register — "you'll never get over this," "no one else would put up with you," "you'd never manage on your own" — and those lines are standard in controlling relationships ([[coercive-control]], [[isolation-tactics]], [[financial-abuse]]). Where they appear alongside monitoring, financial control, threats or isolation, the response is planning with people who do this professionally, not a debate about forecasting accuracy: [[dv-safety-planning]] carries the protocol and [[no-contact-exit-planning]] the exit sequencing, and both carry the standing resource list. **US:** National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 / thehotline.org · **UK:** 0808 2000 247 · standing list in `README.md`.

Where a forecast concerns physical safety, legal exposure, immigration status or money that will actually run out, the answer is services and specifics, not a psychology citation. The right reference class for "could I manage on my own?" is people who have actually left — not the person you would be leaving.

## Caveats

- **Impatience is often rational, and this is the file's most important fence.** If your income is volatile, your housing insecure, your health poor or the promiser unreliable, a smaller-sooner reward genuinely has higher expected value than a larger-later one, because the later one may not arrive. That is correct discounting of a real environment, not a defect in the person — and the same logic is why "now is certain, later carries counterparty risk" survives as a live explanation of measured present bias in the lab [3]. This matters because targets are routinely told their urgency is irrational when it is not: someone who needs the money this week, or who wants a decision from a partner now rather than "when things settle down," may be reading their situation accurately. The anti-condescension evidence is direct: across 61 countries, low-income individuals were **not** significantly more present-biased — the variance sat in inequality and financial circumstances, not in character [5]; measured present bias moved before payday while decision quality, risk-taking and cognitive performance did **not** [5]; and about 60% of payday borrowers accurately predict their own repayment [6].
- ***genuine-deadline*: real inelastic constraints exist, and then urgency is information.** Statutory dates, auction closes, flight prices, finite stock, an actual medical window, a supplier's quarter-end. The distinguishing test is never the presence of a deadline but whether it survives an independent check and whether the reason lies outside the seller's control. A reader who leaves this file unable to act quickly on a real constraint has been made worse off, not safer.
- **Some things genuinely were better before, and some innovations genuinely are better.** The documented illusion is about *diffuse moral decline*, not about all negative trend claims [15]; specific, dated, measured claims — a town that lost its industry, an institution whose standards fell, a service that got worse — can simply be true, and the finding explicitly does not license dismissing older people's testimony (what has not declined is reports of *contemporaries*' morality). Symmetrically, recency is often a decent prior: security patches, safety standards and medical guidelines usually do improve, early adopters are sometimes just right, and appeal to *tradition* is the equal and opposite error. Caution has its own failure mode — missed treatments, missed protections — and a KB that teaches reflexive suspicion of the new produces its own casualties. The diagnostic is never "is it new?" but whether the champion accepts the burden of evidence and can describe what would count as failure.
- **Planning-fallacy correction is itself weaponizable.** "Your timeline is just optimism," "you always underestimate," "be realistic" can be used to dismiss a competent, base-rate-grounded estimate, to force a padded number that then becomes the schedule, or to install a general doubt about the target's judgment — the same move as [[tone-policing]] in quantitative dress. Two facts protect against it: in the psychology literature underestimation is **not** the reliable pattern, short tasks are often over-estimated, and the small-over/large-under rule may be a statistical artifact [9]; and the correction has a checkable form — an estimator who can produce the reference class has answered the objection, and someone who keeps asserting the bias after seeing the distribution is not correcting a forecast.
- **Strategic misrepresentation and bias must not be collapsed in either direction.** Calling deliberate low-balling a "planning fallacy" launders it [10]; calling an honest optimistic estimate a lie is a serious accusation to make on weak evidence. Domestically, chronic lateness in a partner is far more likely to be ordinary planning optimism than contempt, and treating it as contempt does real damage. The discriminator is direction and beneficiary: errors that are systematically one-directional *and* reliably benefit the estimator warrant scrutiny; scattered errors do not.
- **The near-universality problem.** Present bias, optimistic planning, projection, affective misforecasting and the sense of decline are held by essentially everyone who has ever been measured, across decades and continents [5][15]. **None of them is diagnostic of anything about a specific person or situation**, and none of them is evidence that anyone pulled a lever. Innocent look-alikes are everywhere: preference change is not manipulation ("I agreed then and I don't want to now" is legitimate on its own terms); familiar-route preference usually reflects real local knowledge rather than an "effect"; and BNPL, credit and instalments have entirely legitimate uses.
- **Safety fence on affective forecasting — see Safety notes above, which govern.** This file must never be used to tell someone their fear about leaving, their grief, or their assessment of danger is "just a bias."
- **Neurodivergence is not a finding here, and this vocabulary is used as a weapon.** Nothing in this file may be used to code fast decisions, optimistic timelines, impulsive-looking choices or a poor sense of elapsed time as evidence about a *person* — they are near-universal tendencies (see the near-universality problem above), and the observable behaviours are also ordinary features of ADHD and other executive-function differences. The force of this doubles where such a label is already in play, whether diagnosed, self-identified or merely alleged: "you're just impulsive," "you have no concept of time," "you never plan" is itself a dismissal move ([[tone-policing]]) — it converts a disagreement about a decision into a standing claim about the other person's judgment, which is exactly the effect [[gaslighting]] aims at. Two consequences. A person with an executive-function difference is not thereby more manipulable, and this file offers no evidence that they are; and if the timeline is the dispute, the discriminator is the same as everywhere else here — can the estimator produce the reference class? — never a trait attribution. The ADHD/delay-discounting literature is real and was **not swept for this file**; it is logged as an unswept adjacent area in the bibliography rather than summarized, because an unsourced effect asserted about a clinical population is precisely what the prime directives forbid.
- **Depression is not a marker, in either direction.** This file does not license treating a person's negative forecasts as evidence of manipulation, nor telling a depressed person their pessimism is "actually more accurate" — the meta-analysis says close to the opposite, with both groups positively biased [14]. Defensive pessimism is also a functional strategy for some people, not a symptom. And accurate negative forecasts exist: "leaving will be financially hard for two years" may simply be correct, which is why the tell for *installed* pessimism is that the bleakness is specific to the option that would reduce the other person's control.
- **Two members carry nothing and one is miscategorized.** The well-traveled road effect has no located research base [18]; appeal to novelty is a logic label, not a measured effect, and the general psychology runs the other way [19]; pro-innovation bias is a critique of research practice that hardened into a codex row [19]. They are listed here for routing completeness, and a defense agent should not build an inference on any of them.
- **Literature limits, stated once.** The money-based lab measure of present bias is substantially discredited as a measure of time preference [3]; there is no meta-analytic point estimate for the planning fallacy and its direction is domain-conditional [9]; the impact-bias magnitude is under an unresolved three-round dispute [12]; projection-bias field designs cannot fully exclude salience [13]; declinism's political-persuasion claim failed its experimental test [16]; time-saving bias is a two-group literature with no documented exploitation [17]. Nothing here supports "this person is under the influence of a time bias" as a conclusion about an individual ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).
- **Seams.** [[emotional-flooding]] owns the hot–cold empathy gap; this file owns only its forecasting register. [[memory-self-editing]] owns rosy retrospection and fading affect and has already ceded declinism here; this file owns the projection of warmth onto *eras*. [[optimism-overconfidence]] owns optimism bias and overconfidence; pessimism is a moderator on it and lives there in substance. [[scarcity-urgency]] and [[manufactured-urgency]] own deadlines, countdowns and FOMO; this file owns discounting the future, not compressing the present. [[framing-anchoring]] owns focalism, the focusing illusion, drip pricing and the Levine/Wilson-&-Gilbert citation, which is kept identical here. [[probability-blindspots]] keeps the arithmetic half of pennies-a-day; this file takes the temporal half. [[future-faking]] owns the tactic; this file is the *why* underneath it. [[commitment-consistency]] and [[escalation-entrapment]] own sunk cost and effort justification, which is what happens *after* an over-optimistic commitment. Risk-*feeling* entries (normalcy, ostrich, zero-risk) belong to [[risk-misperception]] and choice-simplification entries to [[choice-simplification]].
