# Trust Mechanics

> Trust is willingness to be vulnerable — and it's granted on *cues*, not audits: warmth read in milliseconds, competence inferred from confidence, integrity assumed from symbols. Every cue is fakeable, which is why the con is named after the thing it steals: confidence.

## The lever

The standard model says we judge trustworthiness on three factors — **ability, benevolence, integrity** [1] — but the judging is fast and shallow. Warmth and competence are the two universal dimensions of social perception, with **warmth judged first** and weighted most [2]; face-based trustworthiness impressions form within **100 milliseconds** [3] — and here is the crucial defensive fact: those impressions are *consensual but largely invalid* — people agree on who looks trustworthy while the judgments barely predict actual trustworthiness [3]. Economic trust games confirm humans extend real resources to strangers on thin evidence [4]. Modern life adds **swift trust**: temporary teams and transactions import trust from roles and conventions upfront, verifying later (or never) [6] — a convention every pretexter exploits. One famous biological shortcut deserves its flag: the **oxytocin-spray-increases-trust** finding failed repeated replication; treat all "trust hormone" claims as hype [5].

## Evidence base

- Mayer, Davis & Schoorman (1995): the ABI trustworthiness model; trust as willingness to be vulnerable [1].
- Fiske, Cuddy & Glick (2007): warmth/competence; warmth primacy [2].
- Willis & Todorov (2006): 100-ms judgments [3]; Todorov et al. (2015, *Annual Review of Psychology*): the validity of face-based attributions "has been greatly overstated" [3].
- Berg, Dickhaut & McCabe (1995): the trust game [4].
- Nave, Camerer & McCullough (2015): six failed replications of intranasal-oxytocin→trust [5] — weak-construct registry material.
- Meyerson, Weick & Kramer (1996): swift trust in temporary groups [6].
- Kim et al. (2004), trust repair: **apology** repairs competence violations better; **denial** works better for *alleged* (unproven) integrity violations — with proven guilt, apology wins even there [7]. Defensive relevance: manipulators know denial protects integrity reputations, which is part of why flat denial ([[darvo]]) is the default response to accusation.
- The term itself: William Thompson, the original "confidence man" (NY Herald, 1849), simply asked strangers, "Have you confidence in me to trust me with your watch till tomorrow?" — trust extracted by invoking trust [8].

## How it's weaponized

The recurring exploit: **counterfeit the cue that grants the vulnerability.**

- **Warmth counterfeits** — charm, mirroring, instant intimacy ([[charm-offensive]], [[liking-similarity]], [[love-bombing]]): the first-judged, heaviest-weighted cue is also the cheapest to fake [2].
- **Competence counterfeits** — confident jargon, props, credentials, borrowed institutions ([[authority]] is the role-signal shortcut, and the Dr. Fox conditions live there).
- **Integrity counterfeits** — conspicuous piety, moral talk, community standing: the affinity fraudster's uniform ([[affinity-fraud]], [[unity-ingroup]]); visible scrupulosity in small things buying license in large ones (the con's "convincer" logic — [[con-anatomy]]).
- **Swift-trust exploitation** — pretexting rides role conventions: the "IT guy", the "auditor", the "new contractor" are trusted *as roles* before any verification [6] ([[phishing-pretexting]]; BEC fraud is swift trust at wire-transfer scale).
- **Trust-by-installments** — small reliabilities accumulated deliberately (repay the small loan, keep the small secret, deliver the small win) to bank trust for one large extraction ([[con-anatomy]]'s convincer; [[boundary-testing]] run in reverse).
- **Repair-norm exploitation** — fluent apologies that reset the ledger without behavior change ([[non-apology-patterns]]); flat denial deployed because it works on unproven accusations [7] ([[darvo]], [[history-rewriting]]).
- **Trust-language invocation** — "don't you trust me?" as a compliance lever: framing verification as insult, exactly Thompson's move [8] (the demand to be trusted *instead of* verified is self-incriminating; [[manipulation-vs-influence]]).

## Ethical use vs exploitation

Trust is civilization's working capital — extending and honoring it is how everything functions, and most trust is well-placed. The line ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]): exploitation **manufactures cues instead of substance**, **front-loads trust claims before history exists**, **punishes or shortcuts verification** ("don't you trust me?"), and **spends banked trust on extraction**.

## Recognition

- **Trust demanded rather than earned** — offense at verification, "after all this time you're checking on me?", speed-runs to "you can trust me."
- **Cue–substance gaps**: heavy warmth/credential signaling with thin verifiable history; the resume that glitters but no reference answers.
- **Installment patterns**: a run of small, conspicuous reliabilities followed by an outsized ask (the small-favors ledger built *toward* something).
- **Role-pressure**: "I'm from [institution], so skip the process" — swift trust being invoked to bypass the verification the role normally carries [6].
- **Felt-sense indicators:** "I feel like I've known them forever" (you haven't); guilt at your own caution; noticing you trust them more than the evidence you could list.

## Resistance

1. **Trust the process, not the face** [3]: your 100-ms read is real and *largely invalid* — let verification carry the weight your gut wants to carry ([[verification-rituals]]: callbacks, references, second channels).
2. **Scale trust to verifiable history, not to cues** — extend small, revocable trust early; raise it on *outcomes*, not warmth; keep large vulnerabilities (money, credentials, secrets) gated behind independent verification regardless of how the relationship feels.
3. **"Trust me" triggers verification** ([[detection-heuristics]]): the request to skip checking is the single best reason to check — honest counterparties *welcome* audit (the recurring lesson across [[authority]], [[fear-exploitation]], and here).
4. **Separate apology fluency from repair** [7]: after violations, weight changed behavior over eloquence; an apology that resets trust without cost is a tool, not a repair ([[non-apology-patterns]]).
5. **Audit the installment ledger**: when someone's small reliabilities feel *pointed* — visible, counted, referenced — price the coming ask before it arrives.
6. **Institutional version** ([[organizational-defenses]]): dual control and out-of-band confirmation make swift-trust exploitation structurally impossible — no role, however convincing, moves money alone.

## Caveats

- **Default trust is correct policy** — most people honor it, high-trust societies outperform, and chronic distrust costs more than occasional betrayal ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; the goal is *calibrated* trust: cheap to extend, expensive to scale, revocable on evidence).
- **Slow trust-builders aren't suspects**: building trust deliberately is also what good colleagues and partners do — the tell is cue-substance divergence plus verification hostility plus the outsized ask, in conjunction.
- **Asking to be trusted is human** — insecure people say "don't you trust me?" without agenda; once is a feeling, a *pattern replacing verification* is a strategy.
- **The face-judgment caveat cuts both ways**: untrustworthy-looking people are no likelier to betray you [3] — this file forbids physiognomy in both directions.
- **Boundary with [[authority]]:** declared there (role-signals as a trust *shortcut*); with [[liking-similarity]]: liking is the warmth cue's engine, this file is the full vulnerability decision it feeds.
