- **Less-is-better seam, stated once and not blurred.** `mechanisms/framing-anchoring.md` already owns distinction bias, the joint/separate machinery, decoys and tier grids, with Hsee & Zhang (2004), Hsee et al. (1999) and Hsee & Zhang (2010) as its source [9], and already carries the correct fence ("joint evaluation is often *good for you*: when differences are real and consequential — interest rates, safety ratings — comparing hard is the defense, not the trap"). Per the single-declaration rule no reciprocal edge is declared; the seam is prose-only from this file's side.
- **Unit bias came out of the sweep stronger than the steer assumed, and the replication question the steer asked has a clean answer.** Independent 2025 confirmation in a different country, food and lab (Kontor et al., *Heliyon*: larger unit sizes significantly increase consumption **regardless of packaging**), plus **Cochrane-level support for the applied effect** (Hollands et al. 2015, full abstract retrieved by DOI, with SMDs, CIs and a population kcal estimate). But the **effect and the mechanism are graded separately and this is the file's main epistemic move on this row**: the portion/package/tableware size effect is `established`; unit bias as the *explanation* is `supported` and mechanistically contested — Kerameas et al. (2015) put the dispute in their title ("Unit bias or segmentation effect?"), Oldham-Cooper et al. (2017) find multiple smaller units raising *expected satiety* in a direction a simple unit-as-norm account does not predict, and Zuraikat et al. (2018) find take-away containers substantially attenuating the effect. The **non-food extension** (subscription tiers, token/credit bundles, minimum orders, billing increments) is flagged in-file as **this KB's analogy, not a measured finding**, with the decoy/compromise half routed to `framing-anchoring` and `thats-not-all-decoys` so the file does not claim a novel mechanism where an owned one suffices.
- **Information bias: two corrections, and the second one is the reason the row is dangerous.** First, the **terminological collision** — in epidemiology "information bias" means measurement/misclassification error, a completely different construct that dominates any medical-corpus retrieval. Three sweeps flagged it; the file states it in the entry's first sentence. (The academic sweep suggested a disambiguating alias such as *value-of-information neglect*; the frontmatter alias set was fixed by the steer, so the disambiguation is carried in prose instead — worth revisiting.) Second, and adopted as a mandatory Caveat: **the label is weaponizable against someone doing due diligence.** This KB's own standing advice is to verify, document, check independently and sleep on it; a target being pressured to decide fast will be told their desire to check is a cognitive bias. The construct is narrow — information that **cannot change the action** — and the file fences it hard, including the recognition sweep's point that wanting to understand something already decided (notably one's own experience of harm) is normal and often necessary, and the skeptic sweep's point that anxiety-driven checking belongs to the reassurance-seeking literature with a different mechanism and a different counter. Both real-world applications the codex invites (diagnostic-test overuse; "just one more check" as stalling) are printed as **over-attributions and extrapolations**, labelled in-line.
- **The spine is evaluability, not "simplification."** The academic sweep's recommendation, adopted: Hsee's evaluability hypothesis is the one properly-sourced organizing construct in the cluster — it explains less-is-better directly, explains the bike-shed phenomenon better than Parkinson does (trivial items are *easy to evaluate*, so everyone has an opinion), and connects to unit bias (the unit is the easy-to-evaluate quantity). The bike-shed application of it is labelled inference. The file's one-sentence spine — **who chose the mode you are evaluating in** — is the exploitation sweep's formulation, and it does not require any of the folk entries to be true.
