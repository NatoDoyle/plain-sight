## Sources cited in dynamics/coercive-control.md

1. Stark, E. (2007). *Coercive Control: How Men Entrap Women in Personal Life.* Oxford University Press — abuse as an ongoing pattern of domination, not discrete incidents; the "liberty crime"; isolation/surveillance/micro-regulation/deprivation/intimidation. (UK edition subtitled *The Entrapment of Women in Personal Life*.) Supported; law-shaping.
2. Johnson, M. P. (2008). *A Typology of Domestic Violence: Intimate Terrorism, Violent Resistance, and Situational Couple Violence.* Northeastern University Press — the intimate-terrorism vs situational-couple-violence distinction. See [[epistemic-guardrails]].
3. Domestic Abuse Intervention Programs (Duluth, MN) — the Power and Control Wheel (hub: power & control; spokes: coercion/threats, intimidation, emotional abuse, isolation, minimizing/denying/blaming, using children, economic abuse, male privilege). Practitioner model; original is explicitly gendered, gender-neutral adaptations exist.
4. Intimate-partner-homicide literature (e.g., J. C. Campbell, Danger Assessment): coercive control and the separation period are major lethality risk factors; risk spikes when the abuser perceives loss of control. See [[dv-safety-planning]]. (US/general; not legal advice.)
5. Legal: Serious Crime Act 2015 s.76 (E&W) + Domestic Abuse Act 2021 s.68; Domestic Abuse (Scotland) Act 2018; Domestic Violence Act 2018 s.39 (Ireland); Crimes Act 1900 (NSW) s.54D; California Family Code §6320; "Jennifer's Law" (Connecticut). Date- and jurisdiction-specific; verify before any legal-facing use.
