## Sources cited in tactics/cold-reading.md

1. Forer, B. R. (1949). "The Fallacy of Personal Validation: A Classroom Demonstration of Gullibility." *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology* 44(1), 118–123 — generic sketch rated 4.26/5 for personal accuracy (N=39). Replications: Manning (1968); Dickson & Kelly (1985, review). The term "Barnum effect" is attributed to Paul Meehl (1956). "Subjective validation": Marks, D. & Kammann, R. (1980), *The Psychology of the Psychic*. Established (psychology).
2. Hyman, R. (1977). "'Cold Reading': How to Convince Strangers That You Know All About Them." *The Zetetic* (now *Skeptical Inquirer*) 1(2), 18–37 — foundational skeptical analysis by a psychologist and former mentalist. Practitioner/skeptic (authoritative).
3. Rowland, I. *The Full Facts Book of Cold Reading* — catalogue of named elements (rainbow ruse, Barnum statement, fuzzy fact, fishing, etc.); plus standard terms "stock spiel," "shotgunning," and the hot/warm/cold distinction. Practitioner.
4. Predatory psychic-fraud cases (court-grounded): *United States v. Rose Marks* et al. (S.D. Fla., 2013–14; "Operation Crystal Ball") — ~$17.8M in curse/"return-the-money" schemes; *People v. Sylvia Mitchell* (NY, grand larceny) — ~$128K. Legal line: "intent to defraud" (claiming a real supernatural power to take money) distinguishes fraud from protected belief/entertainment. Established (court records); larger press-cited single-victim figures are anecdotal.
