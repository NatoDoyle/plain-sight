## Sources cited in mechanisms/confirmation-bias.md

Numbering matches the file's `## Sources` list.

1. Nickerson, R. S. (1998). "Confirmation Bias: A Ubiquitous Phenomenon in Many Guises." *Review of General Psychology* 2(2), 175–220 — the standard umbrella review; operative definition quoted; restricts the term to *unwitting* selectivity. Established. + Stanovich, West & Toplak (2013), *Current Directions* 22(4) — myside bias uncorrelated with intelligence.
2. Wason (1960), *QJEP* 12(3) — the 2-4-6 task; Klayman & Ha (1987), *Psychological Review* 94(2) — the positive-test-strategy reinterpretation (usually adaptive; fails under specific structures). Established; the KB leads with the corrected reading.
3. Lord, Ross & Lepper (1979), *JPSP* 37(11) — biased assimilation (robust) vs attitude polarization (self-report only; does not survive direct measurement: Kuhn & Lao 1996, *Psych Science* 7(2); Guess & Coppock 2020, *BJPS* 50(4), three large experiments, no backlash). Lord, Lepper & Preston (1984), *JPSP* 47(6) — consider-the-opposite debiasing, the family's best-evidenced counter.
4. Hart et al. (2009), *Psychological Bulletin* 135(4) — selective-exposure meta-analysis, d = 0.36 (verified verbatim); defense vs accuracy motivation moderator structure. Echo-chamber calibration: Arguedas et al. (2022), Reuters Institute review (partisan-only diets ≈ 5%); Guess et al. (2023), *Science* 381; Nyhan et al. (2023), *Nature* 620 — 2020-election feed experiments, no measurable attitude change.
5. Baron, Beattie & Hershey (1988), *OBHDP* 42(1) — congruence bias origin (diagnostic-reasoning program, not merely a Wason variant); bias shrinks when alternatives are made explicit. Supported; thin independent literature under this name.
6. Hastorf & Cantril (1954), *J Abnormal & Social Psych* 49(1) — "They Saw a Game" (~9.8 vs ~4.3 infractions counted; single case study, minimal statistics); Kahan et al. (2012), *Stanford Law Review* 64 — "They Saw a Protest," the experimental conceptual replication. Construct graded supported.
7. Evans, Barston & Pollard (1983), *Memory & Cognition* 11(3) — belief bias (invalid-believable acceptance ~70% vs invalid-unbelievable ~10%; exact cell figures vary by materials); Dube, Rotello & Heit (2010), *Psychological Review* 117(3) — response-bias reinterpretation (with the Klauer & Kellen 2011 exchange); Trippas et al. (2018), *PB&R* 25(6) — Bayesian ROC meta-analysis, largely supporting the criterion-shift account.
8. Expectancy cluster: Pfungst (1911), *Clever Hans* — cleanest single demonstration of unconscious cueing; Rosenthal & Fode (1963), *Behavioral Science* 8(3) — iconic but contested (Barber & Silver 1968, *Psych Bulletin Monograph* 70(6, Pt.2): analytic-flexibility critique + failed replications); Rosenthal & Jacobson (1968), *Pygmalion in the Classroom* — contested from publication (Thorndike 1968; Elashoff & Snow 1971); Raudenbush (1984), *J Educational Psych* 76(1) — d ≈ 0.11, near zero after ~2 weeks teacher acquaintance (verified); Jussim & Harber (2005), *PSPR* 9(2) — "typically small… may be more likely to dissipate than accumulate"; expectations mostly accurate; larger for stigmatized students (verified verbatim). Wheeler et al. (1993), *Mental Retardation* 31(1) + ASHA position statements (1995/2018) — facilitated communication as expectancy authorship.
9. Hróbjartsson et al. (2012), *BMJ* 344:e1119 — within-trial blinded vs non-blinded assessors: pooled ROR 0.64 (36% OR exaggeration on subjective outcomes, driven by misclassification of a median ~3% of patients; verified verbatim). Between-trial nuance: Moustgaard et al. (2020), *BMJ* 368:l6802 — no average between-trial effect; do not overclaim "unblinded is always inflated."
10. Dror, Charlton & Péron (2006), *FSI* 156(1) — 4 of 5 examiners changed their own prior identifications under biasing context: three to non-match, one to inconclusive (N = 5; report as fraction with the inconclusive noted, verified — corrects an earlier misattributed "17%" figure, which belongs to the Dror & Charlton 2006 follow-up); DOJ OIG (2006), Mayfield review — "circular reasoning" finding; Murrie et al. (2013), *Psych Science* 24(10) — adversarial allegiance, d up to ≈ 0.85; Dror et al. (2015), *J Forensic Sciences* 60(4) — Linear Sequential Unmasking (the KB's civilian-defense template); NAS (2009); PCAST (2016).
11. Forer (1949), *J Abnormal & Social Psych* 44(1) — 4.26/5 mean accuracy, N = 39, replicating since; Meehl (1956), *American Psychologist* 11(6) — "Barnum effect" coinage; Marks & Kammann (1980), *The Psychology of the Psychic* — "subjective validation" coinage; Dickson & Kelly (1985), *Psychological Reports* 57(2) — moderator review (favorable + "for you" + authority = the cold reader's levers).
12. Kirsch (1985), *American Psychologist* 40(11) — response-expectancy theory (why placebo homes in this family); Hróbjartsson & Gøtzsche (2010), Cochrane CD003974 (+ *NEJM* 2001) — vs no-treatment: effects confined to patient-reported outcomes, esp. pain/nausea (SMD ≈ −0.26 patient-reported vs −0.13 observer-reported); "difficult to distinguish… from biased reporting" (verified verbatim).
13. Kaptchuk et al. (2010), *PLoS ONE* 5(12) — first open-label placebo RCT; von Wernsdorff et al. (2021), *Sci Rep* 11:3855 (author correction PMID 34433888; publication-bias signal) — SMD ≈ 0.72 from k = 11 small trials; Fendel, Tiersch, Sölder, Gaab & Schmidt (2025), "Effects of open-label placebos across populations and outcomes: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials," *Sci Rep* 15:29940, doi:10.1038/s41598-025-14895-z, PMID 40817381 — overall SMD 0.35 (k = 63, n = 4554); self-reported SMD 0.39 (k = 55) vs objective 0.09 (k = 17), difference significant (the file's key number pair; verified against the abstract 2026-08-06).
14. Johnson & Seifert (1994), *JEP:LMC* 20(6) — warehouse-fire paradigm; alternative-account corrections beat bare negation; Lewandowsky et al. (2012), *PSPI* 13(3) — practitioner review (truth-sandwich guidance); Ecker et al. (2022), *Nature Reviews Psychology* 1; Laurent et al. (2023), *Acta Psychologica* 241 — direct replication; Connor Desai et al. (2020), *Cognition* 205 — rational continued influence (distrusting retractions can be Bayesian-sound).
15. Chan et al. (2017), *Psych Science* 28(11) — k = 52, N = 6,878; misinformation d 2.41–3.08, debunking d 1.14–1.33, persistence d 0.75–1.06 (verified); persistence worse when audiences self-generated reasons; Walter & Tukachinsky (2020), *Communication Research* 47(2) — k = 32, N = 6,527; residual r = −.05, p = .045; credible-source/repetition/delay moderators (verified). The two metas measure different contrasts — the file states the qualitative synthesis, not a single number.
16. Wegner et al. (1981), *JPSP* 40(5) — incrimination through innuendo; questions and denials still transmit the association ("just asking questions" engine).
17. Nyhan & Reifler (2010), *Political Behavior* 32(2) — origin; Wood & Porter (2019), *Political Behavior* 41(1) — 5 experiments, >10,100 subjects, 52 issues, "no corrections capable of triggering backfire" (verified); Swire-Thompson et al. (2020), *JARMAC* 9(3) — backfire tracks item unreliability; Nyhan (2021), *PNAS* 118(15) — corrections "typically at least somewhat effective"; decay, not boomerang (verified). Graded contested, largely settled against.
18. Phillips & Edwards (1966), *JEP* 72(3); Edwards (1968) in Kleinmuntz — bookbag-and-poker-chips (~.70 reported vs ~.97 Bayesian); "two to five observations to do one observation's worth of work"; Griffin & Tversky (1992), *Cognitive Psychology* 24(3) — strength-vs-weight reconciliation with base-rate neglect; Benjamin (2019), *Handbook of Behavioral Economics* Vol. 2 — underinference dominant across ~500 treatments.
19. Fraud record (all verified against agency sources): FBI IC3 *2024 Internet Crime Report* — 17,910 romance/confidence complaints, $672,009,052 losses; investment fraud (incl. pig-butchering) $6.57B; FTC press releases (Feb + Nov 2002) — "Miss Cleo" (Access Resource Services): ≈$500M consumer charges forgiven + $5M paid; DOJ press release (2023) — *US v. Patrice Runner* (Maria Duval scheme), $175M+, 1.3M+ mostly elderly victims, 10-year sentence, EDNY; SEC OIG (2009) — Madoff report (Markopolos submissions ignored from 2000).
20. *FTC v. QT, Inc.*, 512 F.3d 858 (7th Cir. 2008) — Easterbrook, J. (not Posner, as sometimes reported); placebo defense to deceptive therapeutic claims squarely rejected; monetary figures reported inconsistently across summaries, so the file cites the holding only. FTC Homeopathy Enforcement Policy Statement (Nov 2016). US jurisdiction.
21. Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan (2010), *BBS* 33(2–3) — the WEIRD-sample critique; grounds the caveat that this file's evidence base is dominated by Western, educated samples. Peng & Nisbett (1999), *American Psychologist* 54(9) — dialectical thinking: responses to contradiction (compromise vs polarization) differ between Chinese and American samples, so belief-revision effect sizes may not travel. Cited only for the cross-cultural caveat; no positive claim in the file assumes universality.

## Consulted but not cited
