## Sources cited in tactics/financial-abuse.md

1. Adams, A. E., Sullivan, C. M., Bybee, D., & Greeson, M. R. (2008). "Development of the Scale of Economic Abuse." *Violence Against Women* 14(5), 563–588 (two factors: economic control + economic exploitation). Postmus, J. L., et al. (2016), "Measuring Economic Abuse… Revising the Scale of Economic Abuse" (SEA-12; adds employment sabotage). Prevalence (~94–99% co-occurrence in DV) is from clinical/advocacy samples — "the large majority," not a population rate. Supported.
2. Littwin, A. (2012). "Coerced Debt: The Role of Consumer Credit in Domestic Violence." *California Law Review* 100 — debt incurred via fraud/coercion in the victim's name; a documented barrier to leaving (a national-hotline survey found >half of callers reported coerced debt). Supported.
3. Legal/recognition (jurisdiction-labeled; not legal advice): UK Domestic Abuse Act 2021 includes statutory "economic abuse" ("substantial adverse effect" on the ability to acquire/use/maintain money or obtain goods/services; prosecuted via the coercive-control offence). US: emerging coerced-debt remedies (e.g., California SB 975, effective 2023). See [[coercive-control]].
4. Resources: Surviving Economic Abuse (UK; founded 2017, Nicola Sharp-Jeffs); NNEDV Economic Justice / Financial Abuse Toolkit (US); financial safety planning (separate account, free credit freeze under the FCRA, document, secure documents). Practitioner.
