# Bibliography log — fluency-familiarity

Sweep date 2026-08-17 (4 parallel lenses: academic/empirical, skeptic/replication, exploitation-in-the-wild, recognition/phenomenology). The session's WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200) before all four sweeps began and Semantic Scholar rate-limited (HTTP 429) early, so verification ran through direct API calls — Crossref REST, OpenAlex, Europe PMC REST (`resultType=core`, which returns full abstracts), Semantic Scholar-by-DOI, and Unpaywall — with a small number of WebFetch calls spent on tertiary summaries for two facts no API would return. `ftc.gov` returned HTTP 403 and was not retried. Synthesis 2026-08-17.

**This file is the cluster's honesty test case in a different way from `memory-retrieval-quirks`.** There, the test was refusing to manufacture exploitation narratives for lab furniture. Here the test is *magnitude and mirror*: the headline member (illusory truth) has been reproduced in the lab for nearly fifty years **and** meta-analyses at g = 0.37 corrected on a corpus that is itself thin on external replication and preregistration, and the file's own mirror claim (disfluency improves thinking) collapsed under replication. Both facts are stated prominently. Seven Cognitive Bias Codex rows route here; processing fluency itself is the organizing construct rather than a codex row, which is why the file covers eight named effects against seven index rows.

**Steer corrections adopted into the draft** (each was flagged independently by two or more sweeps):

- **Implausibility is not a firewall — the steer's open question is closed, and closed the other way.** Pennycook, Cannon & Rand (2018) reported extreme implausibility as a boundary condition; Fazio, Rand & Pennycook (2019) — overlapping authors, a self-correction worth noting as a model of evidence honesty — argued the boundary is a psychometric artifact of the rating scale; Lacassagne, Béna & Corneille (2022, *Cognition* 223:105052, preregistered, 100-point scale, five repetitions) found the effect for blatantly implausible statements. The file leads with the later result **and carries Lacassagne's own two fences**: all judgments stayed below the scale midpoint, and about half of participants showed no or a reversed effect. Net claim in the file: repetition drifts things *within* the disbelief range; the manipulation-relevant zone is the middle (unverifiable, technical, merely unfamiliar claims), not the absurd.
- **Meyer et al. (2015) is NOT a replication of Diemand-Yauman et al. (2011).** The steer conflated two disfluency literatures. Meyer targets Alter, Oppenheimer, Epley & Eyre (2007) — disfluency → analytic reasoning on CRT/math — and pooled the original with 16 replication attempts to a null. The *retention/education* claim (Diemand-Yauman) has its own separate failure record: Rummer, Schweppe & Schwede (2016) direct null; the Sans Forgetica series (Geller, Davis & Peterson 2020; Huff, Maxwell & Mitchell 2022); Taylor et al. (2018) actively harmed learning in students with intellectual disability. The file tells both strands separately and grades the reasoning strand harder than the learning strand.
- **…but the disfluency story is not a clean pile-on, and the file says so.** Xie, Zhou & Liu's (2018) null meta-analysis was itself shown by Weißgerber, Brunmair & Rummer (2021) to contain coding and effect-size errors that leave the *recall* results and moderator analysis in doubt while the *transfer* results held up — with Rummer an author on both the null replication and the critique of the null meta, which is what honest disagreement looks like. Geller et al. (2025) still report high-blur memory benefits. Grade: `contested`, trending refuted **for the font manipulation specifically**.
- **New meta-analysis, not in the steer, and it reframes the entry:** Ye et al. (2026), *Nature Communications* 17:3270 — 182 studies, 366 effect sizes, N = 31,184, **g = 0.37 [0.30, 0.44] after correcting for small-study effects**. This supersedes Dechêne et al. (2010) as the magnitude citation and forces the file to argue on two fronts at once: serious at industrial repetition volume, and *not* an explanation for why anyone holds any particular belief.
- **The countervailing correction that keeps that number honest:** Jalbert, Schwarz & Newman (2020) — the title reads "*reduces* illusory truth," not "fails to reduce" — show the effect is **over twice as large** when the standard pre-exposure warning is omitted, and that warning protection persists 3–6 days. Lab procedures systematically *underestimate* the unwarned case, and the same finding supplies the file's best-evidenced counter.
- **IKEA effect replication status is better than the steer assumed:** Pelled, Demetriades & Walter (2025), *Psychology & Marketing*, k = 55, N = 5,454, d = 0.57, robust to customization and tangibility. Still graded `supported`, not `established`: publication-bias correction was not confirmed in what the sweeps retrieved, the mechanism is contested (Sarstedt 2017 says psychological ownership; Marsh, Kanngiesser & Hood 2018 says ownership alone cannot explain it and that effort amount does not moderate), Sarstedt did **not** reproduce the completion boundary in a customer-empowerment context, Raghoebar et al. (2017) is a clean null in children's food choice, and the effect comes from a consumer-psychology genre with a poor replication base rate.
- **Mere exposure's "subliminal beats supraliminal" is contested, not settled.** Bornstein & D'Agostino (1992) found larger effects for 5-ms than 500-ms exposures and proposed attributional discounting; Newell & Shanks (2007) tested the model directly and found the opposite — a significant effect only where recognition was highest, with liking and recognition positively correlated. The file asserts only the narrow claim (exposure need not be remembered to work), explicitly refuses "subliminal advertising works," and rests the delay/source-loss point on Jacoby et al.'s (1989) verified false-fame result instead.
- **Grooming: the honest contribution is smaller than the steer implied and the file shrinks it deliberately.** No experimental literature applies mere exposure to grooming. The verified finding is a *recognition* finding and it cuts against detection: Winters & Jeglic (2017) found **no difference in likelihood ratings across grooming-stage vignettes** — naive observers cannot flag these behaviors prospectively. Combined with `dynamics/grooming-sequence.md`'s existing (correct) calibration that differentiating behaviors cluster at desensitization rather than warmth, the file states that fluency explains why a familiar adult reads as safe *to the surrounding adults* and supplies **no detection signal**, with a dedicated caveat bullet so the section cannot be read as grounds for suspecting coaches, teachers and relatives.
- **Generation effect: the memory claim and the persuasion claim are different claims.** Slamecka & Graf (1978) and Bertsch et al. (2007) are a *memory* literature (.40, 86 studies, 445 effect sizes — the most solidly verified number in the sweep). "Self-generated conclusions are believed harder" is an ecological leap the file labels as such, routing the weight to `commitment-consistency` and `dissonance-exploitation` and to the one hard finding that actually fits the funnel: Aslett et al. (2024, *Nature*) on searching driving belief in falsehoods via **data voids** — a mechanism that is neither fluency nor generation, and the file says so.
- **Not invented here graded `folk`.** The founding study (Katz & Allen 1982) is a correlational field study of communication decay with team tenure, not a demonstration that people reject ideas because they are external; the construct's own review (Antons & Piller 2015) concedes the antecedents and consequences are not clearly understood; Lichtenthaler & Ernst (2006) split it into six sub-syndromes. Manipulation relevance is stated as low, and the only defensive angle carried is the inverse — "you're just being NIH about it" as a dismissal of substantive objections, routed to `tone-policing` / `bad-faith-argumentation`.
- **Seam decisions.** `mechanisms/availability-salience.md` already declares `distinguished-from: [fluency-familiarity]`; per the single-declaration rule the reciprocal edge is **not** re-declared here and the seam is stated in prose only, from this file's side. `mechanisms/memory-fallibility.md` line 39 keeps its "repetition as installation" line (false *episodic memory* of having been told); this file takes the *truth-judgment* effect — same repetition, two downstream misattributions, stated explicitly in Caveats. `mechanisms/memory-self-editing.md` line 121 had already ceded the topic here. `tactics/thought-terminating-cliches.md` and `tactics/overton-window-shifting.md` keep their existing (accurate) mechanism prose and citations; this file becomes the mechanism home they point to, and it inherits rather than re-argues Overton's mere-exposure fence (strongest for neutral stimuli, weak-to-reversible for disliked ones).
- **Open tension flagged in the file rather than resolved:** repetition appears to move *believing* most on claims the person initially rejects (Orchinik et al. 2024 preprint; Lacassagne 2022) while moving *liking* least on stimuli the person initially dislikes (the Overton fence, Bornstein-line work). If both hold, believing and liking dissociate under repetition. No source addressing this directly was located.

Red-team review: **run 2026-08-17, verdict PASS with all mediums and lows fixed.** File status is now `reviewed`. The review's own Crossref pull closed two PENDING items (Ye et al. 2026 re-confirmed from a second index; Pelled volume/pages resolved) and opened one new spot-check (Winters & Jeglic 2017, single-sweep-verified, journal not in Europe PMC). Revisions applied: the effortful-onboarding bullet recast from operator voice into recognition voice; the "most replicated findings" superlative qualified at first use against the file's own replication caveat; a demotion lead line added under `## Recognition` and the "I've heard of him" bullet fenced against ordinary new acquaintance; the age-gradient of source-monitoring routed to `elder-targeting`; the grooming false-positive fence split out under its own retrievable heading (**No grooming detection signal.**); the `processing difficulty effect` alias bolded at first use; "the same authors" narrowed to "two of the same authors"; the frontmatter-vs-prose grading mismatch stated in Evidence base; and the 16/17 arithmetic reconciled.
