**This file is the codex-honesty test case.** The owner's decision was "all 188 entries, honestly graded," and this is the cluster where that decision is most visible: of the **19 index rows** assigned here (20 alias names — *memory inhibition* and *retrieval-induced forgetting* are one row with two names, which is why the frontmatter carries 20 aliases against a tally of nineteen), one family (order effects) has a substantial applied-exploitation literature, two (RIF, part-list cueing) support a narrow questioning-hygiene claim, one (mood-congruent recall) shapes target-side retrieval without anyone doing anything, three (spacing, testing, levels of processing) are beneficial learning findings the codex miscategorized as biases, three (absent-mindedness, tip-of-the-tongue, next-in-line) are false-positive guards, and the rest are lab furniture with no exploitation angle. The file says all of that in plain words and deliberately manufactures no exploitation narratives.

**Steer corrections adopted into the draft** (all four sweeps independently flagged the first two):

- **RIF is not a failed replication.** The steer's "notable failures — Jonker et al." is factually wrong. Murayama et al. (2014) meta-analyzes 143 papers / k = 512 / g = 0.35 [0.32, 0.38] with fail-safe N = 56,111; Jonker, Seli & MacLeod (2013) is a rival *theory* paper in *Psychological Review*, and it is **Jonker et al.'s own supporting finding** (restudy + context change → forgetting) that failed to replicate three times (Buchli 2015 QJEP; Soares, Polack & Miller 2015 JEP:LMC; Chan, Erdman & Davis 2015 JEP:LMC). File states the correction explicitly.
- **Context effect: the flagship failed.** Godden & Baddeley (1975) diver study did **not** replicate (Murre 2021 *RSOS* 8:200724; the 2022 addendum, *RSOS* 9:211924, corrected the analysis and *reinforced* the failure). The original authors themselves found "no trace of context dependency" for recognition in 1980. Smith & Vela (2001) meta-analysis keeps the broader effect "reliable" but small and abolished by overshadowing / outshining / mental reinstatement. Graded `supported`, told as a full story.
- **Sequential-evaluation results are NOT memory effects.** Bruine de Bruin (2005, 2006) explicitly rejects a recency-in-memory reading — the later-performer advantage holds under step-by-step scoring with no memory load, so it is impression-formation/standard-shifting. Kontek & Sosnowska (2026) attribute a Chopin-Competition later-position premium to delayed use of the top of the scale. Filing these under "recency" would have been an error; the file says so in-line.
- **Lineup position is a response-criterion shift, not serial position** (Meisters, Diedenhofen & Musch 2018; Mayer et al. 2024 — position moves guessing, not detection). The sequential-vs-simultaneous superiority question is a live methodological war (Steblay et al. 2011 vs. Gronlund, Wixted & Mickes 2014 on ROC vs. ratio diagnosticity); the file asserts neither side and uses Wells et al. (2020) as the counter-model instead.
- **Ballot order is not one effect.** Ho & Imai (2008) is itself a debunking paper (no detectable major-party general-election effect); Grant (2017) reports ~10 pp in down-ballot/judicial races *"except for a few high-profile, high-information races"*; MacInnis et al. (2021) reports the opposite moderator ordering in New Hampshire. The file reports the disagreement rather than picking a winner, and leans on Kim, Krosnick & Casasanto (2014) for the moderator profile that actually generalizes (low information, ambivalence, limited cognitive skill, low effort).
- **List-length:** the dispute was always confined to *recognition* memory (free-recall list-length effects are uncontested) and is a model-adjudication argument (item noise vs. context noise), not a replication-crisis story. Two sweeps disagreed on whether it is closed — the skeptic sweep found Yim, Dennis & Osth (2025, *JEP:Gen* 154:2772–2799) reporting evidence for the effect (square-root function, small relative to other interference), co-authored by Dennis himself; the recognition sweep, run the same day, did not surface it and read the dispute as open. **File reports both and grades `contested`.** Discrepancy logged below.
- **"Digital amnesia" is marketing, not science** — reportedly coined by Kaspersky Lab in 2015 for an unreviewed survey. Named as such in the file.
- **Mood-*congruent* vs mood-*dependent*.** The fragile construct is mood-*dependent* (state-dependent) retrieval — Bower published his own non-replication (Bower & Mayer 1985), with an independent failure the same year (Wetzler 1985) and only restricted-condition recovery (Eich 1995). Mood-*congruent* recall survives meta-analysis but is heavily moderated. The file keeps them separate.
- **Routing correction:** the steer's "it wasn't that bad / I always knew / the good times were amazing" phrases are `memory-self-editing` territory (fading affect bias, peak–end, hindsight, rosy retrospection, duration neglect — all assigned there in `taxonomy/bias-codex-index.md`). This file hands off rather than annexing; that is why `memory-self-editing` is the declared `distinguished-from`. Hand-off sourcing is preserved in the recognition sweep §19 for the future `memory-self-editing` topic.
- **Promoted beyond the steer:** socially-shared RIF (Cuc 2007 → Coman 2009 → Stone et al. 2013 in **intimate couples**, from merely overhearing) is the strongest *interpersonal* finding in the cluster and is now the file's second centre of gravity; retrieval-enhanced suggestibility (Chan & LaPaglia 2011) is the testing effect's KB-relevant dark side; divided attention at encoding (Craik et al. 1996/2018) supplies the real mechanism behind "absent-mindedness" as a lever and routes to `cognitive-overload-confusion`.
