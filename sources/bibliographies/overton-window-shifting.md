## Sources

1. Mackinac Center for Public Policy, "The Overton Window" — origin as Joseph P. Overton's "window of political possibilities" (mid-1990s), a think-tank explanatory/marketing device; named "Overton Window" by Joseph Lehman after Overton's 2003 death. mackinac.org.
2. "Joseph Overton" / "Overton window" — Wikipedia overviews of origin and popularization.
3. Beck, G. (2010). *The Overton Window.* Threshold Editions / Simon & Schuster — the bestselling novel that popularized the term.
4. Britannica, "Overton window" (Criticism section); "The Myth of the Overton Window," Center for New Liberalism — critiques: single-spectrum oversimplification, incoherent "middle," non-incremental real-world shifts. Establishes the model as `contested`.
5. Zajonc, R. B. (1968). "Attitudinal Effects of Mere Exposure." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 9(2, Pt.2), 1–27. `established`.
6. Freedman, J. L., & Fraser, S. C. (1966). "Compliance without pressure: the foot-in-the-door technique." *JPSP* 4(2), 195–202. `established`. See [[foot-in-the-door]].
7. Bursztyn, L., Egorov, G., & Fiorin, S. (2020). "From Extreme to Mainstream: The Erosion of Social Norms." *American Economic Review* 110(11), 3522–3548; Crandall, C. S., Miller, J. M., & White, M.
