## Sources cited in tactics/propaganda-devices.md

1. Lee, A. M., & Lee, E. B. (1939). *The Fine Art of Propaganda* — the Institute for Propaganda Analysis's seven devices (name-calling, glittering generalities, transfer, testimonial, plain folks, card stacking, bandwagon), framed around Father Coughlin's broadcasts. IPA founded 1937 (Clyde R. Miller), disbanded 1942. Historical/foundational.
2. Jowett, G. S., & O'Donnell, V. *Propaganda and Persuasion* — standard definition ("deliberate, systematic attempt to shape perceptions, manipulate cognitions, and direct behavior…"). Ellul, J. (1965). *Propaganda: The Formation of Men's Attitudes* — saturation/repetition/pre-propaganda on the already-disposed. Bernays, E. (1928). *Propaganda* — "engineering of consent." Scholarly.
3. Limited-effects tradition: Lazarsfeld, P., Berelson, B., & Gaudet, H. (1944). *The People's Choice* (two-step flow; media reinforces more than converts); Klapper, J. (1960). *The Effects of Mass Communication*. The "hypodermic needle / magic bullet" model of all-powerful direct effects is discredited. Established.
4. Kalla, J. L., & Broockman, D. E. (2018). "The Minimal Persuasive Effects of Campaign Contact in General Elections: Evidence from 49 Field Experiments." *American Political Science Review* 112(1), 148–166 — average persuasive effect ≈ zero (narrow, decaying exceptions). Mercier, H. (2020). *Not Born Yesterday* — "open vigilance," resistance to mass persuasion. Counters: McGuire's inoculation theory; van der Linden & Roozenbeek (prebunking; Roozenbeek et al., *Science Advances* 2022) — effects real but modest/decaying. Established (Kalla-Broockman); supported (inoculation).
5. Critiques of the seven devices: Sproule, J. M. (on the authorship/origins and limits of the seven); Hobbs, R., & McGee, S. (2014), *Journal of Media Literacy Education* — the devices overlap, are thinly defined, and are a pedagogical heuristic, not a validated taxonomy. Methodological caution.
