## Sources cited in dynamics/radicalization-pipeline.md

1. McCauley, C., & Moskalenko, S. (2008, "Mechanisms of Political Radicalization," *Terrorism and Political Violence*; 2017, "Two-Pyramids Model," *American Psychologist*) — the opinion vs. action pyramids and the opinion–action gap ("99% of those with radical ideas never act"). The load-bearing finding.
2. Horgan, J. — "from profiles to pathways, from roots to routes"; "attempts to profile terrorists have failed." Borum, R. (2011), *Journal of Strategic Security* (radicalization review; equifinality/multifinality). Sageman, M., *Understanding Terror Networks* (2004) / *Leaderless Jihad* (2008) — "bunch of guys"; social bonds precede ideology; no profile.
3. Moghaddam, F. (2005). "The Staircase to Terrorism: A Psychological Exploration." *American Psychologist* 60(2), 161–169 — the narrowing-staircase heuristic; prevention best at the "ground floor." A descriptive metaphor, not a predictive sequence.
4. Disengagement vs. deradicalization — Horgan and others: leaving the behavior/group (common, realistic) vs. changing the beliefs (rarer); mirrors the opinion–action distinction.
5. Over-detection / weak prediction: low-base-rate false-positive critiques of violent-radicalization risk tools (Sarma; VERA-2R/TRAP-18 validation problems); critiques of the UK *Prevent* program constructing a "suspect community"; weak/complicated mental-illness link (systematic reviews; lone-actor selection effect). Establishes the anti-profiling, anti-over-detection core. (Analytic, not legal advice.)
