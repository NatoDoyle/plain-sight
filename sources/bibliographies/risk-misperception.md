Synthesis 2026-08-17.

**This file is the knowledge base's under-detection entry, and that inverts its usual failure mode.** Everywhere else Plain Sight guards against over-detection; here the constructs describe missing danger that is real, which creates a new and specific hazard: the file becoming a tool for telling someone that a calm reading is denial. All four sweeps flagged this independently, and it is answered in three places in the draft — a lead line under `## Recognition`, a dedicated `## Safety notes` section, and a Caveats section that opens by stating the inversion explicitly and immediately re-imposes the over-detection guardrails.

**It is also the cluster's weakest set of codex rows to date, and the draft says so.** Nine codex rows, and only one member (ambiguity aversion) reaches `established`. Two are `contested` (Peltzman/risk compensation; the ostrich portfolio finding), one is `folk` (normalcy bias), one is `supported, inherited` with no research programme of its own (pseudocertainty). The frontmatter grade `supported` is a ceiling, not an average, and the draft's Evidence base says which members it rests on. The unusual feature of this topic is that **the two best-evidenced bodies of work in the file are not codex rows at all** — the warning-confirmation/milling literature and the information-avoidance umbrella — i.e. the replacements are stronger than the constructs they replace, which is the argument for `supported` rather than lower.

**Deviation from the orchestrator's frontmatter spec, flagged for a decision.** The brief specified an exact field list that did not include `safety:`. The draft nevertheless carries **`safety: dv-escalation`** plus a `## Safety notes` section, because (a) two sweeps recommended the flag by name (exploitation §1c and §8; recognition cross-cutting note 2), (b) `CLAUDE.md` prime directive 4 requires the flag and a Safety notes section on any file touching domestic violence, and the brief's own content list mandates the "doing nothing is safer" material about targets in abusive situations, and (c) the KB's red-team checklist item 6 would fail the file without it. It matches the existing pattern on `memory-fallibility`, `intermittent-reinforcement`, `memory-self-editing` and `emotional-flooding` (flag + Safety notes between Resistance and Caveats). **One-line revert if the orchestrator disagrees:** delete the frontmatter line and the `## Safety notes` section; the crisis resources also appear nowhere else in the file, so they would need re-siting into Caveats.

**Steer corrections adopted into the draft** (each was flagged independently by two or more sweeps):

- **Normalcy bias is weaker than the steer expected, and weaker than folk usage in a specific, reportable way.** The steer asked whether disaster sociology rejects the panic/denial folk model and reframes evacuation delay as information-seeking and warning confirmation. **It does, and all four sweeps converged on it.** Three separate findings stack: (1) the coining paper (Omer & Alon 1994) defines normalcy bias as a **planners'** error — underestimating the probability or extent of expected disruption — and pairs it with an "abnormalcy bias" that *underestimates victims' ability to cope*, so the popular "individuals freeze and deny" reading inverts half of its own source; (2) the literature footprint is essentially nil under the name (Crossref title search → one topic-level article, itself a *revisiting*; Europe PMC's 58 exact-phrase hits are clinical/rhetorical label use, not a measurement paradigm); (3) the "about 80% of people display normalcy bias" figure is a laundering chain — a technology blog's gloss on a 1994 paper about civilian response to missile attack, acquiring a universal percentage on the way. **Graded `folk`. The 80% figure is not printed**, and the file uses its provenance as a worked example of the laundering the KB exists to teach. The replacement construct — warning confirmation and **milling** — is graded `established` within its own field and carries the entry's defensive weight.
- **The reframe is a *better* manipulation mechanism than the folk version, and the file leads with that.** Two sweeps arrived at the same formulation independently: if people confirm ambiguous signals before acting, **the confirmation window is a manipulable interval**, and whoever supplies the second source controls the interpretation. That is grounded in the actual literature (Mileti & Sorensen; Wood et al.; Kuligowski's WTC interviews) rather than in "80% freeze," and it routes cleanly to `isolation-tactics`, `smear-campaigns`, `boundary-testing` and `coercive-control`. The evidentiary kicker the file prints: **longer, more complete warning messages *shorten* response delay** (Wood et al.) — delay falls when people are given more information, which is not what denial does, and which means the evidence-backed counter is better information and pre-set thresholds, never exhortation.
- **The ostrich effect has a published direct contradiction the steer did not flag, and the file names it.** Karlsson/Sicherman (−9.5% logins after market declines, large retirement-plan panel) versus Gherzi et al.'s "meerkat effect" (active online investors monitored *more* after declines, persisting for non-trading and weekend logins, moderated by neuroticism). Three sweeps independently said that citing Karlsson without Gherzi would be the exact failure the KB exists to prevent. Resolution adopted: **information avoidance** (Golman, Hagmann & Loewenstein 2017) is the parent construct and the ostrich effect a boundary-conditioned special case, with agency and salience as the boundary — routine bad news you cannot act on produces avoidance; a salient crisis, or an available action, produces hypervigilance. The 9.5% figure is printed as the honest calibration precisely because it is modest.
- **The financial-abuse bridge needed a direction correction, and it got a dedicated one.** The steer proposed "not opening bank statements under financial abuse." Two sweeps corrected the causal direction: the NGO evidence (Surviving Economic Abuse) describes **imposed** information deprivation as the dominant pattern — refusing account access, keeping information secret, debt built in the target's name — with surveillance running the other way (receipts checked, spending diaries, every purchase justified). No study applies information avoidance to an abusive-relationship context. The file therefore states the seam explicitly (`financial-abuse` owns the imposed half; this file owns only the self-avoidance layer above it), puts **"are you avoiding, or being kept out?"** into Recognition as a structural tell and into Resistance as a routing decision, and says plainly that telling a survivor they "should have looked" inverts the responsibility.
- **Zero-risk bias: the regulatory attribution the steer assumed does not exist, and the file carries the negative.** The strongest available regulator document (FCA MS14/1, general insurance add-ons) finds these markets "broadly not working for consumers" with "prices significantly above cost" — but attributes it to **attention and point-of-sale advantage**, not to a certainty premium. The draft states this as an honest negative: zero-risk bias is a plausible additional contributor with lab support (Viscusi et al.), and no regulatory attribution was located. Also adopted: Schneider et al. (2017) as the built-in caveat — the bias survives methodological variation but is strongly context-dependent, **does not correlate across tasks (so it is not a trait)**, and is "appropriate for dividing risks on objects … but not on persons," where fairness rather than bias drives the choice.
- **Pseudocertainty is graded `supported, inherited` and the file says the word "borrowed."** OpenAlex title search → 0; Crossref → 0 relevant; Europe PMC exact phrase → 1 hit, an unrelated qualitative farming study. It is one demonstration inside a 12,000–17,500-citation paper, derived from the certainty effect plus the isolation effect. The Many Labs 1 line is used **only as family calibration** (thirteen effects, 36 samples, N = 6,344, ten replicated consistently) with the explicit statement that pseudocertainty was not among the thirteen — one sweep's prior that the Asian-disease framing effect specifically was among the successful ten was flagged `[UNVERIFIED]` by that sweep and is **not asserted in the file**.
- **Omission bias: the canonical vaccination result *reversed* at replication (red-team-verified 2026-08-17), and the file prints the 10× figure only in the same breath as that correction.** Connolly & Reb (2003) is the single most important citation in the row and it appears inline, not in a footnote. Adopted alongside it: the authors' own fences (large individual differences; normality of the act; the asymmetry reversing where action is the expected role), and the normative point — calling the act/omission distinction a bias presupposes a consequentialist standard rejected by most deontological ethics, by medical ethics, and by essentially every legal system. Graded `supported` for the lab asymmetry, `contested` as an irrationality. The file also declines to present omission bias as *the* explanation for vaccine refusal.
- **The abuse application of omission bias was rated the highest-misuse-risk content in the topic, and it is fenced three ways.** Two sweeps said, in near-identical language, that framing a target's non-action as omission bias is one short step from victim-blaming and is empirically wrong in most abuse cases, where non-action reflects accurate threat assessment, financial dependence, immigration status, custody exposure, and prior experience of what happens when you act. The draft (a) leads with those rational explanations, (b) states in `## Safety notes` that **nothing in the file may be used to tell someone that staying was a cognitive error**, with separation named as the documented highest-risk window and `dv-safety-planning` given governance, and (c) keeps the defensible use entirely on the manipulator's side of the ledger — naming the rhetorical transfer of causal responsibility ("if you go, that's on you," "I never laid a hand on you") rather than pushing anyone to act.
- **Peltzman/risk compensation graded `contested`, with the direction of the error stated.** Robertson's reanalysis (data errors; the model "failed to predict fatality rates before regulation"); Cohen & Einav's direct test of the compensating-behaviour hypothesis, unsupported in its own words; Pless's mortality-trend rebuttal; a helmet meta-review finding benefit *greatest* in high-risk situations. The file refuses the over-correction too: partial adaptation is real where an actor has incentives (vaccine-era mobility; smugglers in the search-and-rescue study — note that it was the smugglers who adapted, not the protected individuals). **The primary manipulation content of this row is the *argument*, not the bias** — an unfalsifiable, magnitude-free compensation claim deployed against a safeguard, with "your safety plan will just provoke him" as the interpersonal miniature. Two sweeps independently reached that framing.
- **Ambiguity aversion: three fences, and one of them inverts the naive exploitation story.** (i) It is not universal — inverse-S pattern, with neutrality or ambiguity *seeking* for low-likelihood events; (ii) Fox & Tversky's comparative-ignorance boundary means much of the classic effect is a joint-evaluation phenomenon, so **a seller presenting only the ambiguous option faces no aversion at all — the exploit is preventing comparison**, which routes to `framing-anchoring`'s joint/separate machinery; (iii) Heath & Tversky's competence hypothesis means the aversion *reverses* with felt expertise, which makes flattery-into-felt-competence the manipulation-relevant direction (`flattery-ego`). One sweep also noted that some of the original effect may reflect warranted suspicion of an interested party's undisclosed odds — in a manipulation KB that is a feature, and the file says so.
- **Seam decisions.** `certainty-needs` owns need-for-closure and ambiguity intolerance (its alias list already carries "ambiguity intolerance", verified in-repo); this file takes only the decision-theoretic register, with the bridge sentence *"closure-need explains why you want an answer; ambiguity aversion explains why you will pay more for the option with its odds printed on it."* `probability-blindspots` already declares the routing from its side, verbatim in-repo: *"the risk-feeling family (normalcy, zero-risk, ostrich) belongs to [[risk-misperception]]"* — per the single-declaration rule the reciprocal is **not** re-declared here and the seam is stated in prose only. `fear-exploitation` owns threat inflation and fear-then-relief; `availability-salience` owns what comes to mind first; `framing-anchoring` owns gain/loss framing, reference points and JE/SE. **`betrayal-blindness` is the seam the steer did not name and it is the most important one**: it owns motivated not-seeing driven by dependence — the vulnerability-side account of the same observable behaviour, and the better frame whenever the person not-seeing needs the betrayer. It already carries `safety: dv-escalation`, which is a further argument for this file carrying one.
- **Numbers deliberately not printed:** the "80%" normalcy-bias prevalence figure (laundering chain, no primary); the "8–10 seconds to process new information" claim from the same chain (no primary cognitive-science source); WTC pre-evacuation delay figures from Galea et al. 2009/2011 (abstracts not retrieved); FCA GAP-insurance claims-ratio and commission figures (both press-release URLs 404'd).
