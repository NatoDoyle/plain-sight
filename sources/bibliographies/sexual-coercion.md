## Sources cited in tactics/sexual-coercion.md

1. CDC, National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey (NISVS) — "sexual coercion" (unwanted sexual contact after non-physical pressure) within sexual violence; Koss, M. P., et al., Sexual Experiences Survey (Koss & Oros 1982; Koss & Gidycz 1985; rev. 2007; SES-V) — hierarchical classes incl. a verbal/non-physical-pressure coercion class. Supported/established.
2. Affirmative-consent standard (freely given, reversible, informed, enthusiastic, specific; coercion negates consent) — campus Title IX policies and consent education (e.g., the "FRIES" framing, Planned Parenthood). "Sexual compliance" (freely agreeing to unwanted sex without pressure; Impett & Peplau) is distinct from coercion. Supported.
3. Reproductive coercion — Miller, E., et al. (2010), *Contraception* (birth-control sabotage, pregnancy pressure/coercion); ACOG Committee Opinion (2013). Supported.
4. Legal note (US, jurisdiction-labeled; not legal advice): most non-physical coercion is outside criminal rape statutes (which require force/threat/incapacity); some jurisdictions criminalize coercion/fraud-based offenses; campus/Title IX defines coercion more broadly ("unreasonable pressure"). Targeting incapacity or using force = assault (criminal). Resource: RAINN 1-800-656-HOPE (4673) / rainn.org/hotline.
