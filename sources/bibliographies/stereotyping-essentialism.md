# Bibliography log — stereotyping-essentialism

Sweep date 2026-08-17 (4 parallel lenses: academic/empirical, skeptic/replication, exploitation-in-the-wild, recognition/phenomenology). The session's WebSearch quota was exhausted (200/200) before any of the four sweeps began, so verification ran almost entirely through direct API access — OpenAlex, Crossref, Europe PMC (`resultType=core`), PubMed E-utilities, Semantic Scholar-by-DOI, Unpaywall — plus a small number of WebFetch calls against primary institutional pages. APA PsycNet (JS-only) and PubMed's HTML endpoint (cookie wall) blocked automated fetching and were routed around; several APA-published primaries (Meissner & Brigham 2001; Pettigrew & Tropp 2006; Jussim et al. 2015; Oswald et al. 2013) are fully paywalled with no OA copy anywhere, so their headline conclusions are verified from publisher abstracts while their internal effect sizes are flagged below. `sec.gov` returned HTTP 403 (not needed — affinity fraud is owned by `unity-ingroup`). Synthesis 2026-08-17.

**This is the most delicate file in the tier, and the delicacy is structural rather than tonal.** The literature it summarizes contains, in the same corpus, (a) a well-replicated finding that aggregate stereotypes correlate with group statistics and (b) a well-replicated finding that a widely marketed bias instrument cannot characterize an individual. Each is routinely laundered in opposite political directions. The file's design rule, adopted from all four sweeps independently: **every claim is about the perceiver's inference machinery, never about what any group is like**, and every finding that can be laundered carries its correction *in the same breath*, in body text, not in a footnote. Every content-level illustration in the file is a fictional category ("zarpies"), a structural description of a stereotype-content quadrant with no group named, or historical propaganda described as propaganda. Eight Cognitive Bias Codex rows route here (Stereotyping, Essentialism, Out-group homogeneity bias, Cross-race effect, Implicit associations, Implicit stereotypes, Stereotypical bias, Prejudice); `taxonomy/bias-codex-index.md` already names "IAT-based implicit association claims" among the entries this KB does not launder, and this file is where that promise is paid.

**Steer corrections adopted into the draft** (each flagged independently by two or more sweeps):

- **The essentialism→prejudice link is real but narrower than the steer assumed — the biggest correction in the sweep.** Haslam, Rothschild & Ernst (2000) find essentialism is *two independent dimensions* (natural-kind-ness; entitativity/reification) and state that it "is not a unitary syndrome of social beliefs, and is not monolithically associated with devaluation and prejudice"; their 2002 test of Allport's claim "received partial support but had to be qualified in important respects" — strong association with anti-gay attitudes, weak with sexism and racism, no consistent cognitive style across stigmatized categories, and *some anti-essentialist beliefs* also tracking prejudice. The file therefore never writes "essentialist beliefs cause prejudice" as a trait claim.
- **…but the experimental evidence is stronger than the correlational trait evidence, and that asymmetry is the manipulation-relevant fact.** Keller (2005), Williams & Eberhardt (2008), Mandalaywala et al. (2018) and Pehrson et al. (2009, cross-lagged) show *induced* essentialism raising bias, hierarchy endorsement, acceptance of inequity and willingness to act against an out-group — in members of advantaged and disadvantaged groups alike, and "generally independent of racial prejudice." An operator does not need to find essentialists; they can manufacture the framing. The file states the induced-state grade and the trait grade separately. **Red-team correction:** Keller carries a moderator — "particularly in persons holding chronic essentialist beliefs" — so that study selects on the trait and cannot by itself carry the ordinary-audience claim. The moderator is now restored in the body and the unselected-sample claim rests on Williams & Eberhardt and Mandalaywala, which support it. Pehrson is a cross-lagged panel, not an experiment, and the body no longer says "causing."
- **The causal delivery mechanism is grammatical and demonstrated.** Rhodes, Leslie & Tworek (2012, *PNAS*): generic language about a *novel* social category installs essentialist belief in 4-year-olds and adults, and induced essentialist belief makes parents produce more generics — a closed loop. This is the single most operationally useful finding in the sweep for Recognition, because it converts a vibe into a checkable linguistic marker, and it is why "count your own generics" appears on both sides of the Recognition section.
- **The accuracy debate is stated with Jussim's own co-authored data supplying the limits.** The abstract sentence ("one of the largest and most replicable findings in social psychology") is quoted rather than hidden; the three-part shape comes from Chan et al. (2012), on which Jussim is a co-author: consensual accuracy median r = .75, *personal* accuracy mean r = .34 with **not one of the 3,323 participants as accurate as the consensual profile**, and magnitudes inflated (perceived d = .90 vs actual .54; dispersion ratio 1.66, 1.34 after correcting for a possible contrast artifact). Wühr et al. (2017) reproduce "accurate in direction, but inaccurate in size." Whole classes fail outright (national character — Terracciano et al. 2005, "unfounded stereotypes"; partisan composition — Ahler & Sood 2018, ~5× and ~19× errors). Bordalo et al. (2016) supply the formal reconciliation: kernel of truth + representativeness + a **chosen reference group** = technically-correlated and wildly misleading, which is the machinery behind "the statistics speak for themselves." Dixon (2017) supplies criterion contamination; Williams, Sng & Neuberg (2016) supply the confounded-proxy result. The defensive line the file states plainly: aggregate accuracy licenses nothing about an individual, and the manipulation use *is* that leap.
- **The IAT split is graded exactly as the steer instructed, with better numbers than the steer had.** Phenomenon `supported` (Devine 1989's equal-knowledge dissociation; Correll et al. 2002's shooter studies, where bias tracked cultural-stereotype knowledge and contact but **not** personal prejudice and appeared equally in Black and White participants). Measure `contested`: the dueling predictive estimates are printed from the IAT camp's own reply — **r̄ = .236 vs r̄ = .148**, with the difference attributed by those authors to "different policies for including effect sizes"; test–retest **.54** vs .75 for explicit measures *despite comparable internal consistency* (Gawronski et al. 2017), .34–.48 in children (Rae & Olson 2018); Forscher et al. 2019 (492 studies, 87,418 participants) show movement in the measure not transferring to behavior and **not mediating** behavior change; Lai et al. 2016 show nine interventions all decaying within hours to days. The file notes that Nosek and Devine are authors on the decisive meta-analysis — this is the field self-correcting, which is what keeps the section from being quote-mineable as a debunking of discrimination.
- **The keystone framing finding is Correll, not the IAT.** Bias as a *knowledge* effect rather than a *hatred* effect is what makes this a mechanism file rather than a profile file, supplies the "bias ≠ bigotry" caveat, and explains why the file recommends procedural change over moral condemnation.
- **Contact-hypothesis publication-bias concern relocated.** The steer asked to note publication-bias concerns for Pettigrew & Tropp (2006); the sweeps found P&T explicitly tested for and rejected selection and publication bias *in their corpus*. The real indictment lands elsewhere and is carried instead: Paluck, Green & Green (2019) — 27 randomized-with-delay arms, 0.39 SD falling to **0.20** once disability studies are excluded, 0.25 [0.08, 0.42] for ethnic/racial/religious/immigrant targets, a file-drawer signature, and "no studies of ethnic or racial contact include participants over 25 years of age"; and Paluck et al. (2021) — 418 experiments, 76% light-touch, "troubling indications of publication bias." Plus Barlow et al. (2012) on negative contact's asymmetry, and the generalization ceilings in Mousa (2020) and Scacco & Warren (2018). Contact stays as the best-evidenced counter and is shipped with all of it.
- **Implicit-bias training is deliberately NOT listed as a counter.** Forscher 2019 (no mediation, trivial behavioral change), Lai 2016 (no durability), Chang 2019 (preregistered field, active placebo: one-offs "unlikely to be stand-alone solutions"), Bezrukova 2016 (attitudinal effects decay). Listing it would have been the easy, wrong move.
- **Out-group homogeneity does NOT appear in minimal groups.** Ostrom & Sedikides (1992) is the opposite of the likely drafting instinct, and it is load-bearing twice over: it makes the effect largely an information/exposure artifact (a genuine innocent explanation for the Caveats section) and it cleanly dissociates this file from `unity-ingroup`, where minimal-group *favoritism* is robust. Simon & Brown (1987) supply the reversal under minority threat.
- **The cross-race effect is a perceptual/memory effect that operates independently of prejudice, and the file says so three times.** Meissner & Brigham found no support for racial attitudes as a driver; contact moderates only weakly (r ≈ −.13 → −.15 in Singh et al. 2022, strongest for childhood contact); Kelly et al. (2005) show newborns with no own-race preference and 3-month-olds with one. This is the file's cleanest demonstration that category-based misperception is not made of hostility — and its most consequential, since it convicts people.
- **Routing, enforced.** Illusory correlation as the stereotype-*formation* route (Hamilton & Gifford; Mullen & Johnson; Fiedler's information-loss and Costello & Watts' rational accounts) stays in `mechanisms/pattern-illusions.md`, which already carries the "stereotype manufacture at scale" weaponization bullet — this file links and adds one boundary sentence. In-group favoritism, minimal groups, social identity theory, forced teaming and affinity fraud stay in `mechanisms/unity-ingroup.md`; this file contributes only affinity fraud's *out-group-facing* half (stereotype content in the pitch; under-reporting to avoid confirming an out-group stereotype). The ultimate/intergroup attribution asymmetry stays in `mechanisms/attribution-errors.md`, which already grades it honestly. Per the single-declaration rule, this file declares only `countered-by: [inoculation-prebunking, manipulation-audit]` and `distinguished-from: [ordinary-categorization]`; the seams with `pattern-illusions`, `unity-ingroup`, `attribution-errors`, `cognitive-overload-confusion`, `certainty-needs` and `fluency-familiarity` are stated in prose from this file's side. `fluency-familiarity` already routes familiarity's effects on *category* judgments here.
- **Stereotype threat is not imported.** The performance-decrement literature has substantial replication and publication-bias problems and no sweep could resolve the meta-analytic figures. Nothing in the file rests on it; the phenomenology in Recognition (representation weight, the flinch, self-monitoring) is presented as felt experience, explicitly not as a performance claim.
