Synthesis 2026-08-17.

**This file's honesty problem is the opposite of `fluency-familiarity`'s.** There, the headline effect was real and the mirror claim collapsed. Here, **three of the ten codex rows routing to this file are materially weaker than their billing, one has no research base at all, one is a logic label, and one is a critique of research practice miscategorized as a cognitive bias** — while the two strongest results (the moral-decline illusion at n = 12.5M, and intertemporal-choice anomalies replicating across 61 countries) are unusually good. A blanket grade would have been dishonest in both directions, which is why the file leads its Evidence base with an explicit frontmatter-vs-prose mismatch note and grades every entry separately. **Frontmatter grade adjudicated at red-team (2026-08-17): `established` → `supported`.** The reviewer's reasoning, accepted: the lead member and first alias (hyperbolic discounting / present bias) is `supported` as a regularity and `contested` as a model; the file's own Lever section concedes that the unification of the members "is this file's framing, not a finding," so no established core construct exists for the rubric to attach to; and because `evidence:` is machine-readable, an alias hit on **well-traveled road effect** — a verified null — must not return a file stamped `established`. Declinism's descriptive result still grades `established` **inline**, as the exception rather than the basis for the file grade. Ten Cognitive Bias Codex rows route here (well-traveled road effect, declinism, impact bias, pessimism bias, planning fallacy, time-saving bias, pro-innovation bias, projection bias, hyperbolic discounting, appeal to novelty); "present bias" is carried as an alias of hyperbolic discounting rather than as an eleventh row.

**Steer corrections adopted into the draft** (each flagged independently by two or more sweeps):

- **The hyperbolic-vs-quasi-hyperbolic dispute is worse — i.e. more interesting — than "which functional form."** All four sweeps converged: (i) the founding review (Frederick, Loewenstein & O'Donoghue 2002) is a *skeptical* document about measurement, not a coronation; (ii) two independent meta-analyses with different elicitation protocols (Cheung, Tymula & Wang 2026; Imai, Rutter & Camerer 2021) find present bias real, heterogeneous, and inflated by selective reporting, with **β ≈ 0.938 for money against ≈ 0.750 for nonmonetary rewards**; (iii) the money-based lab paradigm is substantially discredited as a measure of time preference (Andreoni & Sprenger 2012 ×2; Augenblick et al. 2015; Cohen, Ericson, Laibson & White 2020 — with Laibson himself co-signing); (iv) heuristic models out-predict discounting models (Ericson et al. 2015) and a 2024 experiment finds *precrastination*, the opposite sign, with no model fitting (Freeman & Laughren 2024). The file therefore writes the regularity and refuses the model: "people frequently over-weight an immediate benefit against a diffuse later cost" is printed; "people discount hyperbolically" is refused. **Manipulation relevance survives the skepticism intact** and the file says so — payday/BNPL/act-now closes require only that a salient immediate benefit outbid a diffuse later cost.
- **The planning fallacy's universality is qualified by the best available review, not by a contrarian.** Halkjelsvik & Jørgensen (2012, *Psychological Bulletin*) was named by three sweeps: underestimation is domain-conditional (engineering/management yes, psychology literature no), the small-over/large-under rule may be a **statistical artifact** of random error, and performance-time and completion-time predictions are conflated across the literature. **No meta-analytic point estimate exists** — the review declines to give one and no sweep located a planning-fallacy meta-analysis. The file prints no percentage.
- **Reference-class forecasting is the actionable counter, and Flyvbjerg's own thesis is that half the problem is not cognitive.** "Error or Lie?" (Flyvbjerg, Holm & Buhl 2002) is quoted verbatim: underestimation across 258 projects "cannot be explained by error and is best explained by strategic misrepresentation, that is, lying." The file makes this the entry's load-bearing defensive claim — **"planning fallacy" is the charitable reading and must not be the default one when the error is one-directional and benefits the estimator** — and carries RCF's own mixed record (Batselier & Vanhoucke 2016 for; Themsen 2019 against, n = 1 case study).
- **The impact-bias dispute ran three rounds, not two, and the KB was carrying only two.** The skeptic sweep retrieved the missing rejoinder: Levine et al. (2013), *JPSP* 105(5), 749–756, "Like Schrödinger's cat, the impact bias is both dead and alive," which reports that the pattern survives Wilson & Gilbert's recommended recoding **and claims to rule out focalism** — a direct hit on the mechanism `framing-anchoring.md` leans on. Per the consistency instruction, this file reuses `framing-anchoring.md`'s existing hedge ("the core is safe; precise magnitudes are not"), adds round three, and does not re-litigate the exchange. **Recommended follow-up for the maintainer (not done here, to avoid editing a `complete` file mid-topic): extend `framing-anchoring.md` source [10] with the 2013 rejoinder and soften its caveat line to note the exchange is unresolved.**
- **Projection bias survived the skeptic sweep intact** — no failed replication and no artifact critique located, which the skeptic lens states explicitly as a positive finding. Its evidence is unusually good for a "bias" (40M+ vehicle transactions; catalog returns), and the file carries the honest methodological limits instead: quasi-experimental designs, salience not excluded by the authors themselves, modest magnitudes, and heavy lab overlap with the hot–cold empathy gap. The single best material the sweeps surfaced is that **cooling-off periods are the source literature's own policy recommendation**, which makes the file's central counter the original authors' prescription rather than a folk remedy.
- **Pessimism bias is not a distinct construct, and its best-known anchor failed a preregistered replication.** Moore & Fresco (2012) meta: overall depressive realism *d* = −.07, with **both** groups positively biased (dysphoric .14, nondysphoric .29) and the effect concentrated in the weakest designs; Dev et al. (2022, *Collabra*) found "no evidence that depressive symptoms relate to illusory control or to overconfidence." Two sweeps recommended not authoring it as a standalone row at all. The file grades it `contested`, routes the substance to `optimism-overconfidence`, and keeps only the manipulation-relevant inversion: pessimism **installed** about the target's alternatives ("no one else would put up with you," "you'd never manage on your own") is a targeted-expectation manipulation, not a standing bias. Also carried: Harris & Hahn (2011) as a caution that the *mirror* construct has its own artifact critique.
- **Declinism got much stronger descriptively and much weaker causally.** Mastroianni & Gilbert (2023, *Nature*, n = 12,492,983, ≥60 nations, ≥70 years) is the anchor, including its two moderators — the illusion is attenuated, eliminated or reversed when people are asked about **people they know well** or people who lived before they were born — which the file converts directly into a counter. But van Prooijen et al. (2022) is the fence: nostalgia correlates with populist attitudes across three preregistered studies while **the experimental manipulation failed to move them**. The file therefore describes decline framing as "a framing that finds an audience, not a technique that manufactures one," and does not claim nostalgic messaging causes political realignment. The label "declinism" itself has almost no dedicated primary literature; the entry is assembled from adjacent findings and says so.
- **Time-saving bias is real, thin, and has no documented exploitation.** Two sweeps independently reported the same shape: Svenson's construct, replicated by Peer and extended from scenarios to a driving simulator, with a debiasing programme — but a Europe PMC exact-phrase query returns **twelve records**, the literature sits in two research groups, and the applied work is uniformly **protective** (road-safety debiasing), not exploitative. The file grades it `supported`, prints no effect size, states the absence of an exploitation literature plainly, and labels the everyday extensions (speed upsells, "10× faster" pitches) as the KB's own inference.
- **Well-traveled road effect: recorded as a null, not written up.** Three sweeps searched Crossref bibliographic, OpenAlex title and Europe PMC exact-phrase and found **zero** relevant records — no primary study, no review, no originating author. Two sweeps recommended dropping it; the file keeps a short honest stub so `bias-codex-index` can explain the absence, routes the adjacent real phenomenon to retrospective-duration work in `memory-retrieval-quirks` / `fluency-familiarity`, and manufactures no citation.
- **Appeal to novelty: `n/a`, and the general psychology runs the other way.** No experimental literature exists under the name; index hits are the ordinary English phrase. The skeptic lens added the point the file adopts: there is no general "newer = better" tendency — mere exposure and neophobia both cut against it — so writing it up as a bias would be exactly the folk-psychology laundering the prime directives forbid. It is carried as a rhetorical form with its mechanisms routed elsewhere.
- **Pro-innovation bias is miscategorized by the codex and the file says so.** Rogers coined it as a bias in *diffusion research itself*; the strongest empirical treatment (Karch et al. 2016) is a selection/survivorship problem in scholarship. No lab paradigm, no effect size. Graded `supported` as a critique of research and institutional decision-making, explicitly **not** established as an individual cognitive bias, with Talke & Heidenreich's innovation-resistance framing as the counter to "non-adoption is a defect in you."
- **Anti-condescension is a structural requirement of this file, not a caveat afterthought.** Three findings force it and all three are printed: Ruggeri et al. (2022) — across 61 countries lower-income individuals were **not** significantly more present-biased, with the variance in inequality and circumstances; Carvalho, Meier & Wang (2016) — measured present bias moved before payday while decision quality, risk-taking and cognitive performance did not; Mann (2013) — **about 60% of payday borrowers accurately predict** their repayment horizon. The file's line is "being squeezed is not the same as being fooled," and Mann is cited *because* it cuts against the KB's natural direction.
- **Seam decisions.** `memory-self-editing.md` line 121 already declares "[[time-distortions]] owns declinism and forward-looking misprediction"; per the single-declaration rule the reciprocal is stated in prose only. `emotional-flooding.md` keeps the hot–cold empathy gap (Loewenstein 2005; Ariely & Loewenstein 2006) and this file takes only the forecasting register. `framing-anchoring.md` keeps focalism, the focusing illusion and the Levine/Wilson-&-Gilbert citation, reused identically. `scarcity-urgency.md` / `manufactured-urgency.md` keep deadlines, countdowns and FOMO. `probability-blindspots.md` line 87 already declares "pennies-a-day sits on the seam with [[time-distortions]]" — this file takes the temporal half and leaves the arithmetic half there. `optimism-overconfidence.md` keeps optimism bias and overconfidence, and its existing caveat ("Don't flip to pessimism or paranoia") is now empirically backed by this file's pessimism entry. `future-faking.md` keeps the tactic; this file is the mechanism underneath it. The exploitation sweep proposed routing personal promises to a `promises-and-timelines` file — **no such id exists in the repo or ROADMAP, and the route was redirected to `future-faking` + `documentation-practices`.**
- **Open tension flagged rather than resolved:** the money/nonmoney split in present bias (β ≈ 0.94 vs ≈ 0.75) implies that the strongest form of the lever lives in consumption and effort, yet the best-documented commercial exploitation (payday, BNPL) is nominally *monetary*. The file's resolution — that these are consumption decisions dressed as monetary ones — is the academic sweep's reading and is stated as such, not as a finding.
