## Sources cited in tactics/timeshare-playbook.md

1. FTC Consumer Advice, "Timeshares, Vacation Clubs, and Related Scams" (consumer.ftc.gov) — "free"/discounted vacation offers used as bait for a sales presentation; "only good if you buy today" pressure; resale/exit-scam warnings; report at reportfraud.ftc.gov. Regulatory (US; not legal advice).
2. Consumer journalism and former-salesperson accounts on timeshare presentations (e.g., ACA Group write-ups; Rick Pons, *Lying For A Living*) — the gift hook, multi-hour overrun, turn-over closers, drop close, and fatigue-as-tactic. Insider/journalistic; the *functions* are documented, the labels are jargon.
3. Former-salesperson exposés documenting the turn-over ("T.O.") system and liner/closer sequence — industry-insider, present as jargon not regulator terms.
4. ARDA (American Resort Development Association), *2025 State of the Vacation Timeshare Industry* — ~10 million U.S. households own a timeshare; average annual maintenance fee ~$1,480 (2024), up from ~$1,000 (2018). **Industry trade-body** data (useful, not neutral).
5. FTC press release, "Court Orders Operator of Timeshare Exit Scheme to Pay $140 Million" (April 2026) — Christopher Carroll / Square One Development Group; $95M redress + $45M civil penalty; scheme took $90M+, mostly from older adults, $5,000–$80,000+ fees. Regulatory/established.
6. Consumer guidance on how presentations wear prospects down (fatigue/duration as tactic) — journalistic/advocacy.
7. DOJ Office of Public Affairs, "United States and State of Wisconsin Obtain Over $140M Judgment … Timeshare Exit Services Aimed at Elderly Consumers" (2026) — corroborates [5]. Established.
8. AARP, "How Time-Share Exit Scams Work — and How to Avoid Them" — Washington case ($2.61M restitution, 2021); a California couple lost ~$900,000 to Mexico-based scammers (2024, via NYT); points owners to ARDA-ROC's responsibleexit.com. Cite figures with these attributions/years; do not merge.
9. FBI New York, "FBI Warns of Scams Targeting Timeshare Owners" — Mexico-based call-center fraud targeting owners (secondary; specifics unverified directly).
10. State timeshare rescission ("cooling-off") statutes — typically ~3–15 days, in writing, generally non-waivable (e.g., FL 10, CA 7, NV 5, AK/DC 15). Distinct from the FTC Cooling-Off Rule (16 CFR Part 429, off-premises sales, 3 business days). Jurisdiction: US, state-specific; not legal advice.
