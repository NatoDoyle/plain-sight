## Sources

1. "Tone argument" — Geek Feminism Wiki (derailment / concern-troll move); "Tone policing" — Wikipedia overview (framed as a species of ad hominem). Folk/activist origin; the emotional register of a claim is logically independent of its truth.
2. Robot Hugs ("K") (2015). "No, We Won't Calm Down — Tone Policing Is Just Another Way to Protect Privilege." *Everyday Feminism* — popular essay that spread the term. Folk.
3. Lorde, A. (1981). "The Uses of Anger: Women Responding to Racism." NWSA keynote; *Women's Studies Quarterly* 9(3):7–10; repr. in *Sister Outsider* (1984).
4. King, M. L., Jr. (1963). "Letter from Birmingham Jail" — the "white moderate … devoted to 'order' … prefers a negative peace which is the absence of tension to a positive peace which is the presence of justice."
5. Higginbotham, E. B. (1993). *Righteous Discontent: The Women's Movement in the Black Baptist Church, 1880–1920.* Harvard University Press — respectability politics (the scholarly root of "police your delivery to be heard").
6. Brescoll, V. L., & Uhlmann, E. L. (2008). "Can an Angry Woman Get Ahead? Status Conferral, Gender, and Expression of Emotion in the Workplace." *Psychological Science* 19(3):268–275. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02079.x. `supported` — the empirical kernel.
7. Salerno, J. M., & Peter-Hagene, L. C. (2015). "One Angry Woman: Anger Expression Increases Influence for Men, but Decreases Influence for Women, During Group Deliberation." *Law and Human Behavior* 39(6):581–592. `supported`.
