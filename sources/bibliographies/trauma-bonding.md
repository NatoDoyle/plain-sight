## Sources cited in dynamics/trauma-bonding.md

1. Dutton, D. G., & Painter, S. L. (1981). "Traumatic Bonding: The development of emotional attachments in battered women and other relationships of intermittent abuse." *Victimology* 6(1–4), 139–155; and (1993) "Emotional Attachments in Abusive Relationships: A Test of Traumatic Bonding Theory," *Violence and Victims* 8(2), 105–120 (empirical test, n=75; the two conditions: power imbalance + intermittency). Supported (modest empirical base).
2. Carnes, P. (1997). *The Betrayal Bond: Breaking Free of Exploitive Relationships.* — "betrayal bond"; bonds formed through repeated cycles of betrayal interwoven with reward, in relationships where trust/power is exploited. Clinical/popular.
3. Operant conditioning — intermittent/variable-ratio reinforcement produces the most persistent, extinction-resistant responding (the canonical gambling case). The mechanistic engine; see [[intermittent-reinforcement]]. Established behavioral science.
4. "Stockholm syndrome" — coined by N. Bejerot after the 1973 Norrmalmstorg robbery; not a DSM/ICD diagnosis; ~5–8% prevalence in FBI hostage data; Namnyak et al. systematic review ("psychiatric diagnosis or urban myth?"); critiqued origin (diagnosis made without meeting the hostages; arguably victim-discrediting). Contested. See *stockholm syndrome*.
5. Concept-creep / social-media inflation: "trauma bonding" widely misused to mean bonding over *shared* hardship, contrary to its meaning (attachment to an abuser); documented trauma-term creep on TikTok (cf. #trauma content analyses). Folk/inflated usage. See *shared hardship bonding*.
