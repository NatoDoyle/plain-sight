# Future-Faking

> Buying present compliance with a vivid imagined future the promiser has no intention — or no ability — to deliver. The marriage, the move, the promotion, the trip "once things settle down": you pay now, in patience and investment, against a future that keeps not arriving.

## Definition

Future-faking is **making vivid promises about a shared future to extract present investment, patience, or compliance, with no genuine intent or capacity to deliver**. The named tactic is folk/clinical — popularized by clinician Dr. Ramani Durvasula, with **no peer-reviewed literature under the term** — but the mechanisms it pulls are well-established. **Affective forecasting** research shows people systematically *overweight* imagined futures (the impact bias: anticipated feelings loom larger than realized ones) [1], and **construal-level theory** shows distant/hypothetical futures are represented *abstractly* [2] — which is exactly why a grand future promise is cheap to make and hard to check: it lives at the abstract, emotionally-amplified, unverifiable distance where the promiser pays nothing and the target's imagination does the work. The damage is real and durable: trust violated by *deception* (a false promise) recovers far worse than trust damaged by mere unreliability — even after apology and sustained good behavior [3]. The diagnostic is not the promise but the **gap between words and the concrete steps** — and what the promise is being used to *buy now*.

## Variants & aliases

- **The relationship future** — moving in, marriage, children, "our house someday" — deployed to secure exclusivity, investment, or tolerance of present bad behavior ([[love-bombing]]'s promised tomorrow; [[attachment-needs]]).
- **The perpetually-promised raise/promotion** — the carrot that funds unpaid extra work and retained loyalty; the scholarly home is *psychological-contract breach* (unfulfilled employer promises) [7] ([[workplace-bosses]], [[moving-goalposts]]).
- **Sales/vaporware** — the feature, the delivery, the return that's always coming; where the promise is a specific objective claim without basis, it crosses from puffery into actionable deception [5] ([[sales]]).
- **Romance-scam futures** — marriage talk and plans to visit that never materialize, a documented feature of the grooming-into-sting sequence [6] ([[romance-scam-arc]], [[mirroring-false-identity]]).
- **The reform promise** — "I'll change, we'll get help, it'll be different" deployed to prevent leaving (the hoovering and abuse-cycle staple — [[hoovering]], [[abuse-cycle]]).

## How it works

Three levers, all carrying present cost on a future that's deferred indefinitely. **Hope-greed**: the vivid future is a phantom the target fixates on, discounting present reality against it ([[hope-greed]]'s phantom fixation; affective forecasting makes the phantom feel *more* real than the eventual letdown would [1]). **Attachment-needs**: when the promise is relational — the wedding, the family, "forever" — it hooks belonging hunger directly, and leaving means losing not just the person but the imagined future with them ([[attachment-needs]]). **Commitment-consistency**: present investment made *toward* the promised future becomes sunk cost ("I've waited three years, it has to pay off"), and each renewal of the promise resets the meter ([[commitment-consistency]]; [[escalation-entrapment]]). Construal-level theory explains why it keeps working: the future stays abstract and bright, the broken track record stays concrete but gets re-narrated, and "once things settle down" is always one horizon away [2].

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- **Promises consistently outrun delivery** — the grand future is detailed and vivid; the concrete near-term steps toward it never quite happen.
- **The promise arrives when something is needed *now*** — exclusivity, money, patience, tolerance of bad behavior — and recedes once secured.
- **Re-promising after each failure** rather than accounting for it: the timeline resets, the vision refreshes, the deficit goes unaddressed ([[moving-goalposts]]).
- **Words-deeds gap**: enthusiasm for the destination, no movement on the path (no ring after the ring-talk, no posting-date after the promotion-talk, no ticket after the visit-talk).
- **Specifics dissolve under questioning**: "when, exactly?" and "what's the next step?" produce vagueness, deflection, or irritation rather than a plan.

### Typical phrases

"When we're married, everything will be different." · "I'm going to take you to [place] — we'll do it properly." · "You're next in line for the promotion, just hold tight." · "Once this deal closes, we'll finally…" · "I just need a little more time and then we'll start our life." · "I've already picked out the ring / the house / the names." · "This time I really will change."

### Felt-sense indicators

- Living substantially in a future that never becomes present — narrating your life by what's *about to* happen.
- The recurring deflation when another promised milestone passes unmarked, quickly papered over by a fresh vision.
- Defending the relationship/job to others with the *future* ("it's going to be amazing once…") rather than the present.
- A quiet tally you try not to look at: the gap between how long you've waited and how much has actually moved.

### Escalation signs

The promised future used to extend the timeline indefinitely while extracting more now (money, relocation, foregone opportunities — [[escalation-entrapment]]); reform-promises specifically deployed to defeat exits ([[hoovering]]); in scams, future-faking accelerating toward the ask ([[romance-scam-arc]], [[hope-greed]]).

## Where it appears

Intimate relationships (the promised commitment that buys present tolerance); employment (the dangled advancement — psychological-contract breach [7]); sales and fundraising (vaporware, "coming soon" — [[sales]]); and scams, where the constructed future (love, riches, rescue) is integral to the grooming [6] ([[romance-scam-arc]]). It overlaps every domain that runs on anticipation.

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

A core component of [[love-bombing]] (declared there: the promised future is part of the flood) and [[hoovering]] (declared there: "I've changed, it'll be different" as the reform-promise). Pairs with [[mirroring-false-identity]] (the fabricated person makes fabricated promises) and [[moving-goalposts]] (the timeline that keeps receding), and runs on [[hope-greed]]'s phantom. In scams it's a scripted stage; in relationships it's the bridge between idealization and the next disappointment.

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Weight deeds, not words** ([[detection-heuristics]]): treat promises as data only when matched by concrete near-term steps. "Great — what's the first step, and by when?" The *response to that question* is diagnostic ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]: a sincere planner welcomes specifics; a future-faker deflects).
- **Don't pay now for later**: decline to make present sacrifices (money, exclusivity, foregone options, tolerated mistreatment) *against* a promised future — let the future earn the present by arriving in stages.

### Structural / long-term

- **Run the track-record test** ([[verification-rituals]]): how many previously-promised futures has this person actually delivered? Past delivery predicts future delivery far better than present enthusiasm; a pattern of vivid-promise-then-nothing is the answer.
- **Set checkpoints with dates** ([[documentation-practices]]): convert the vision into near-term, dated, verifiable steps ("if we're moving in spring, we're viewing places by March"). Future-fakers resist concretization because the concrete is checkable.
- **Cap the wait**: pre-decide how long you'll invest against an undelivered promise before you act on the present reality instead — and count from the *first* promise, not the latest renewal ([[commitment-consistency]]'s sunk-cost reset is the trap).
- **In employment**: get advancement promises in writing with criteria and timelines; an employer who won't commit specifics is answering the question [7].

## Caveats & false positives

### Not future-faking when…

- **Genuine aspiration** ([[genuine-aspiration]]): people sincerely share hopes and plans they *intend* and take real steps toward — and sometimes life intervenes and they don't pan out. Honest future-talk is most of love and ambition. The discriminators: **intent and steps** (real near-term movement toward the vision), **not extracting present payment** against it, and **present conduct consistent** with the promise — versus vivid words that buy something now and never concretize.
- **Plans that fail for real reasons**: the promotion that fell through in a restructure, the trip cancelled by money or illness — circumstance defeating a sincere plan is not deception. The tactic-grade requires a *pattern* of promise-to-extract-then-recede, not a disappointment.
- **Optimism and dreaming together**: couples and teams imagine futures aloud as bonding and motivation, with no manipulation; shared daydreaming is not a contract being faked.
- **Your own impatience**: wanting milestones faster than a sincere partner can deliver is not their deception ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; run the symmetric check before concluding intent).

### Base rates & severity calibration

Most unkept promises are life, optimism, or ordinary flakiness — not strategy. The tactic-grade conjunction: **vivid promise + used to extract something present + words-deeds gap + re-promising rather than delivering, as a pattern** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; one unfulfilled plan is rung-1 evidence). Intent is inferred, not observed — so this file reads *behavior* (the extraction, the deeds-gap, the re-promising) rather than claiming to know the promiser's heart, and reserves the label for the pattern.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `folk`: the *named* tactic has no research base of its own, popularized by a single clinician. But its **component mechanisms are well-supported-to-established** — affective forecasting and the impact bias [1], construal-level theory [2], and the durable trust-damage of deceptive (vs merely unreliable) promise-breaking [3] — and the regulatory line on false future claims [5] and the romance-scam grooming evidence [6] anchor specific domains. The honest synthesis: future-faking-as-label is folk, but the lever is real and the harm (especially the deception-specific trust damage [3]) is measurable. The open edge is intent, which behavior can only infer.
