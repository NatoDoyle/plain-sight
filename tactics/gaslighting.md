# Gaslighting

> A sustained campaign to make a person distrust their own perception, memory, or sanity — so that the manipulator's version of reality becomes the only one available. Not a disagreement about what happened; a war on the target's ability to *know* what happened.

## Definition

Gaslighting is the **systematic, directional denial of a target's accurate experience** — "that never happened", "you're imagining it", "you're crazy" — repeated across time within a relationship of trust or dependence, until the target's confidence in their own reality-testing erodes. Three properties separate it from ordinary disputed memories: it is **patterned** (not one disagreement but a standing policy), **directional** (corrections always favor the gaslighter's interests), and **identity-targeting** (the attack lands on your *capacity to perceive*, not just on one claim). Sociologically it runs on power: it works best where inequality and dependence give one party's reality more institutional weight [3].

The name comes from Patrick Hamilton's 1938 play *Gas Light* (filmed 1944, with Ingrid Bergman): a husband secretly searches the attic for hidden jewels, the house's gas lights dim as a by-product — and when his wife accurately notices, he insists nothing has changed, hides objects, and blames her, grooming her toward believing she is going mad [1].

## Variants & aliases

- **Countering** — disputing the target's memory of events they remember clearly.
- **Withholding/stonewalling-as-denial** — refusing to engage: "I'm not listening to this again."
- **Trivializing** — reframing the target's perceptions as oversensitivity: "you always overreact."
- **Diverting/blocking** — changing the subject or questioning the target's competence to raise it.
- **Forgetting/denial theater** — performed amnesia about promises and incidents ([[history-rewriting]] is the past-editing sibling).
- **Coalition gaslighting** — recruiting others to confirm the false reality ("everyone thinks you've been acting strange" — [[proxy-recruitment]], [[triangulation]]).
- **Institutional and medical gaslighting** — organizations or clinicians systematically discounting accurate reports (symptoms, complaints, harms), with marginalized patients disproportionately affected [6].

## How it works

The substrate is [[memory-fallibility]]: human memory is reconstructive and yields to confident, repeated suggestion — no weakness in the target required. The pressure point is [[certainty-needs]]: chronic manufactured doubt is intolerable, and the gaslighter offers the only available certainty — their version. [[trust-mechanics]] supplies the channel (we calibrate reality against intimates by default — epistemic dependence on a partner is normal, which is what makes its abuse effective), and [[attachment-needs]] raises the exit cost of concluding that the person you love is doing this deliberately. Recent theory formalizes the loop: gaslighting exploits normal predictive-learning machinery — the target keeps updating toward the trusted source's testimony even against their own perception [9]. Victims report the documented endpoint: diminished sense of self, pervasive self-doubt, and mistrust of their own judgment that outlasts the relationship [4].

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- Your accurate observations **reliably** meet denial, not discussion — and the denials always run in the direction of their interests.
- Performed certainty against evidence: flat "that never happened" where a sincere person would say "I remember it differently."
- Evidence avoidance: anger at texts being quoted, notes being kept, witnesses being consulted ([[memory-fallibility]]'s record-hostility tell).
- Your emotional reactions reframed as the problem ("you're hysterical") while the triggering behavior goes undiscussed ([[darvo]] adjacency).
- Third parties fed a parallel narrative about your instability ([[smear-campaigns]] in slow motion).

### Typical phrases

"That never happened." · "You're imagining things — again." · "You're being crazy/paranoid/dramatic." · "I never said that, you're putting words in my mouth." · "Everyone agrees you've been off lately." · "You have a terrible memory, remember?" · "I'm worried about you — you're not well." · "You always twist things."

### Felt-sense indicators

- Chronic confusion **localized to one relationship**: clear-headed everywhere else, foggy with them.
- Apologizing constantly without being able to say what for; rehearsing conversations in advance and re-litigating them after.
- Secret reality-checking: keeping notes "just in case", screenshotting conversations, relief when a third party confirms your memory.
- The question "am I crazy?" arriving on a schedule — after interactions with one specific person.
- A shrinking sense of being able to get *anything* right.

### Escalation signs

Doubt-seeding widens from disputed incidents to your general competence ("you can't be trusted with money/the kids/decisions"); isolation deepens ([[isolation-tactics]]) so calibration sources disappear; medical/psychiatric framing appears ("you need help", weaponized therapy referrals); the pattern integrates into full [[coercive-control]] — at which point this is a safety issue, not a communication problem.

## Where it appears

- **Intimate relationships** — the canonical arena; embedded in coercive control's degradation tactics [7].
- **Families** — parents denying childhood events; the family "official story" enforced against a scapegoat's accurate memory ([[golden-child-scapegoat]], [[family-parents]]).
- **Workplaces** — assignments denied, conversations rewritten, "that was never the deadline"; paper-trail-hostile managers ([[workplace-bosses]], [[pip-weaponization]]).
- **Institutions** — complaints reframed as complainant instability (the 1969 clinical cases were engineered psychiatric commitments [2]); medical gaslighting of accurately reported symptoms [6].
- **High-control groups** — doctrine outranking perception ("doubt your doubts"), [[thought-terminating-cliches]] doing micro-gaslighting work ([[cults-high-control]]).

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

Gaslighting rarely travels alone: it pairs with [[isolation-tactics]] (removing calibration sources is a *precondition* for reality control), [[darvo]] (when confronted, deny → attack → claim victimhood), [[history-rewriting]] (the past-tense form), and [[circular-conversation]] (exhaustion softens reality-testing — [[cognitive-overload-confusion]]). It typically arrives *after* warmth establishes trust ([[love-bombing]] first, doubt later) and escalates toward [[coercive-control]]. In recovery timelines, expect [[hoovering]] with a re-written history of the relationship.

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Don't litigate reality live** — the argument *is* the tactic's home turf ([[jade-avoidance]]). State once, without defending: "My memory of it is different, and I trust it." Exit the loop.
- Refuse the frame-shift to your sanity: "We're discussing what happened, not my mental health" ([[boundary-scripts]]).
- Note the *form* of the denial: "I remember it differently" is human; performed certainty plus character attack is data ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s response-to-resistance test).

### Structural / long-term

- **Documentation is the counter** ([[documentation-practices]]): contemporaneous notes, confirmation emails, message threads — stored off shared devices (see Safety notes). Reconstructive memory is the attack surface; records remove it ([[memory-fallibility]]).
- **Keep calibration sources alive**: trusted third parties who hear events *when they happen*, not after the narrative war starts; resist the isolation that always accompanies this tactic ([[isolation-tactics]]).
- **Track the pattern, not the incident**: a dated log of disputes-and-directions converts "am I crazy?" into checkable data ([[manipulation-audit]], [[epistemic-guardrails]]'s confidence ladder).
- Therapy with a clinician experienced in coercive control (and alert to the manipulator co-opting the therapy: "even your therapist thinks…" claims deserve verification).

### When to exit or escalate

Sustained gaslighting inside an intimate relationship is a recognized component of [[coercive-control]] — couples-communication fixes do not repair a reality war. When the pattern is established (documented, directional, escalating), the question shifts from "how do I make them see?" to "how do I leave safely?" ([[no-contact-exit-planning]], [[dv-safety-planning]]). You will likely doubt yourself most right before exit — that is the tactic working, not evidence you're wrong.

## Caveats & false positives

### Not gaslighting when…

- **Two sincere memories differ** — the universal default (*memory divergence*): both parties misremember honestly, in both directions, constantly. A disputed memory, even a heated one, is rung-1 evidence of nothing.
- **Someone disagrees with your interpretation** — "I don't think they meant it that way" or "I experienced that conversation differently" is *honest disagreement*, even when it stings.
- **Genuine forgetting** — ADHD, stress, alcohol, and ordinary fallibility produce denied promises without design (*neurodivergent communication*, *incompetence not malice*); the discriminators are directionality and pattern.
- **Reassurance that misses**: "I'm sure they didn't mean anything by it" is (sometimes clumsy) comfort, not reality-denial.

### Base rates & severity calibration

The word's 1740%-lookup explosion made it the flagship case of concept creep ([[epistemic-guardrails]]): most relationship conflict involving disputed memories is *not* gaslighting, and the scholarly construct requires power asymmetry plus sustained directional denial [3]. Run the conjunction — patterned + directional + identity-targeting + record-hostile — at calibration-ladder strength before the label. And note the symmetric trap: **"you're gaslighting me" deployed against honest disagreement is itself a control move** ([[therapy-speak-weaponization]]); the accusation requires the same evidence standard as the behavior.

## Safety notes

Gaslighting embedded in control, surveillance, or violence is [[coercive-control]] territory: the severity override governs ([[epistemic-guardrails]]). Keep documentation off shared devices, accounts, and premises — discovered records are a known escalation trigger ([[memory-fallibility]]'s safety note). Exit planning belongs with professionals: **US:** National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 / thehotline.org · **UK:** 0808 2000 247 · standing list in `README.md`; protocol at [[dv-safety-planning]]. If psychiatric commitment or custody is being weaponized, document early and get counsel.

## Evidence & debates

The construct's empirical base is young but converging: a landmark sociological treatment [3], qualitative victim studies documenting tactics and sequelae [4], a theoretical model grounding it in normal predictive learning [9], clinical lineage back to 1969 [2], and a clinical-popular stage model (disbelief → defense → depression) [5]. Graded `supported` rather than `established`: no prevalence estimates exist beyond weak polling [8], measurement is nascent, and the term's popular usage vastly outruns the validated construct — which this file's caveats exist to manage.
