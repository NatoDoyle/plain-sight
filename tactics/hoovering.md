# Hoovering

> Named after the vacuum: the campaign to *suck a target back in* after they've left or gone quiet. The escaped person is most dangerous to the manipulator's control precisely when they're nearly free — so the contact spikes exactly there. The intensity is not renewed love. It's the system fighting extinction.

## Definition

Hoovering is **the pattern of renewed contact a manipulator deploys to re-establish a relationship the target has ended or withdrawn from** — apologies, manufactured crises, flattery floods, "I've changed," holiday and anniversary messages, contact via mutual friends, and engineered run-ins. The term is folk: it comes from narcissistic-abuse recovery communities (the Hoover vacuum metaphor) and circulates clinically, but has **no peer-reviewed literature under the name** — graded `clinical` here on the strength of its academic neighbors, not the label. Those neighbors are solid: relationship-science documents **cyclical ("on-again/off-again") relationships** as common — roughly two-thirds of one college sample — and reliably *worse* than non-cyclical ones, with each renewal predicting more negatives and fewer positives [1]; and post-breakup **obsessive relational intrusion** maps a continuum from ordinary pursuit through unwanted contact to stalking [2]. Hoovering sits where those two literatures meet: renewal attempts, often unwanted, deployed strategically.

## Variants & aliases

- **The "I've changed" return** — sudden insight, therapy claims, apology — promises of transformation with no behavioral track record yet ([[future-faking]]; the change is asserted, not demonstrated).
- **Manufactured crisis** — an emergency (illness, accident, "I'm in trouble," a death) that makes not-responding feel cruel ([[guilt-leverage]], [[pity-plays]]).
- **The flattery flood / re-bombing** — [[love-bombing]] redeployed: the warmth that started the relationship, replayed to restart it.
- **Proxy contact** — messages relayed through mutual friends, family, or children ("your mother's worried about you") — [[proxy-recruitment]], [[triangulation]].
- **Ambient hoovering** — likes, views, "accidental" run-ins, anniversary and holiday texts, "saw this and thought of you" — low-cost pings keeping a channel warm.
- **Casual-as-if-nothing** — re-contact that simply ignores the rupture, normalizing return and skipping accountability.

## How it works

Hoovering runs on the engine documented in [[intermittent-reinforcement]]: a relationship trained on unpredictable warm/cold cycles built powerful attachment, and when the target finally withdraws, the manipulator's intensified campaign is — behaviorally — an **extinction burst**: when reinforcement stops, response *escalates* before it fades, and bursts are likeliest after exactly the intermittent schedule abusive relationships run [3]. The target's side supplies the rest: [[attachment-needs]] (the bond hunger doesn't vanish at separation), [[hope-greed]] (the phantom of the changed person — the relationship you wanted, finally delivered), and the sunk costs of [[commitment-consistency]] ("maybe this time"). Each successful hoover also re-teaches the lesson that leaving is reversible — which is why a *single* readmission can reset months of recovery.

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- **Contact spiking right after you withdraw** — silence during the relationship's calm, intensity the moment you pull away (the extinction-burst signature [3]).
- **Words, not changed conduct**: declarations of transformation arriving *before* any sustained different behavior — the promise substituting for the track record ([[future-faking]]).
- **Crisis timing**: emergencies that surface precisely when you've gone quiet, calibrated to make non-response feel heartless.
- **Multi-channel persistence**: blocked on one channel, they appear on another; through friends; through your family; at your gym ([[proxy-recruitment]]).
- **The cycle has happened before**: prior splits ended in return-then-relapse — the renewal-and-deterioration pattern the research documents [1].

### Typical phrases

"I've been in therapy — I finally understand what I did." · "I know I don't deserve another chance, but…" · "Something's happened, I need you, there's no one else I can call." · "I was just thinking about you — remember [happy memory]?" · "I'll never do it again, I promise, this time is different." · "Can we just be friends? I can't lose you completely." · "Happy birthday — I hope he's treating you better than I did."

### Felt-sense indicators

- The lurch of hope at their name on your screen — followed by the old dread.
- Re-litigating the breakup in your head, their best moments suddenly vivid and their worst ones fuzzy ([[memory-fallibility]] working for them).
- Guilt engineered by their crisis: feeling responsible for their wellbeing after leaving.
- Exhaustion at the persistence — the sense that staying gone requires constant active defense.

### Escalation signs

Contact escalating in frequency or intensity despite clear "no"; threats woven into the appeals ("I can't live without you" — [[self-harm-threats]]); surveillance or showing up uninvited; proxy contact widening to your workplace or family; the pursuit acquiring the features of [[stalkerware-monitoring]] or stalking [2]. **Post-separation is the highest-risk window in abusive relationships** — see Safety notes.

## Where it appears

Intimate relationships (the canonical arena, after a breakup or no-contact); families (the estranged parent or relative's re-contact campaigns — [[family-parents]]); and high-control groups, where defectors are hoovered back by love-bombing, guilt, and crisis ([[cults-high-control]], [[cult-exit-support]]). It is the standard sequel to [[love-bombing]] → [[isolation-tactics]] → discard cycles, and the predictable response to [[no-contact-exit-planning]].

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

Hoovering is the **re-entry phase** of the abuse cycle: it redeploys [[love-bombing]] and [[future-faking]], leans on [[guilt-leverage]] and [[pity-plays]] for the crisis variant, and uses [[proxy-recruitment]] and [[triangulation]] to reach a target who's blocked direct contact. Success funds [[trauma-bonding]] (every readmission deepens it) and resets the [[intermittent-reinforcement]] schedule. It is, specifically, the predicted reaction to a target enacting [[no-contact-exit-planning]] or [[gray-rock]] — which is why those defenses must plan for it.

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Expect the burst, pre-decide the response** ([[no-contact-exit-planning]]): name in advance that escalation *confirms* the pattern rather than reopening the question — "their intensity is the system fighting extinction, not evidence they've changed." The extinction-burst frame [3] turns the hardest moment into predicted data.
- **Don't reward any channel**: intermittent response is the *worst* outcome — it teaches that persistence pays and resets the schedule. Consistency (no contact, or flat [[gray-rock]] where contact is unavoidable, e.g., co-parenting) is what extinguishes.
- **Route crises to the right responder**: a genuine emergency goes to family, friends, or services — not to the person who left. "If you're in danger, call 911/Samaritans" both meets a real crisis and declines the role of sole rescuer.

### Structural / long-term

- **Demand demonstrated change over time, never promised change** ([[verification-rituals]]): if reconciliation is ever genuinely on the table ([[genuine-reconciliation]]), it shows as sustained different *conduct* across months with accountability — not a flood of words at week one.
- **Document the contact** ([[documentation-practices]]): dates, channels, content — both to see the pattern clearly and because persistent unwanted contact may be legally actionable (harassment/stalking; jurisdiction-dependent, see Safety notes).
- **Brief your network** against proxy contact: tell mutual friends and family you don't want messages relayed; close the [[triangulation]] channels in advance.

### When to exit or escalate

If contact persists after a clear "no," involves threats, surveillance, or showing up in person, it has crossed from hoovering into harassment or stalking — a safety and legal matter, not a relationship question. Preserve evidence, consider a protective order, and use the resources in Safety notes.

## Caveats & false positives

### Not hoovering when…

- **Genuine reconciliation** ([[genuine-reconciliation]]): real repair attempts exist — and they look different: they take responsibility without conditions, accept "no" gracefully, respect stated boundaries, move at the pace the hurt party sets, and show *changed behavior over time* rather than a burst of words. The discriminator is conduct-over-time vs words-on-arrival, and acceptance-of-no vs escalation-on-no ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s resistance test).
- **Sincere grief and reaching out** ([[honest-hurt]]): people honestly miss each other and sometimes say so; one wistful message is not a campaign. The tactic-grade is the *pattern* — timing (spiking at withdrawal), persistence past "no," multi-channel pursuit, and the prior renewal-relapse history [1].
- **Co-parenting and unavoidable contact**: legitimate logistics (the kids, shared finances, shared work) are not hoovering even when unwelcome; the tell is content-creep — logistics that keep becoming relationship bids.
- **Both people choosing to try again**: mutual, clear-eyed reconciliation is a valid choice; the research that on-off relationships fare worse [1] is information for that choice, not a prohibition.

### Base rates & severity calibration

Breakups are messy and ambivalent; missing an ex is universal, and many people send one regretted text. The tactic-grade conjunction: **contact timed to your withdrawal + words-not-conduct + persistence past clear "no" + a prior return-and-relapse cycle** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; one sad message is rung-1 evidence). Severity is graded `high` because the failure mode — readmission — can erase months of recovery in a night, and because the post-separation context carries genuine physical risk.

## Safety notes

Leaving is the most dangerous period in an abusive relationship: research on intimate-partner femicide finds the majority of victims were stalked or harassed in the year before being killed, and separation is a documented risk peak [5]. If hoovering includes threats, surveillance, showing up uninvited, or self-harm framing ([[self-harm-threats]]), treat it as a safety matter, not a relationship decision — the severity override governs ([[epistemic-guardrails]]). Preserve evidence; consider a protective order; plan with professionals. **US:** National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 / thehotline.org · **UK:** 0808 2000 247 · standing list in `README.md`; protocol at [[dv-safety-planning]].

## Evidence & debates

Graded `clinical`: the *named* tactic is folk/practitioner with no peer-reviewed studies, but the pattern rests on real empirical neighbors — cyclical-relationship research (renewals common and harmful) [1], obsessive-relational-intrusion's pursuit-to-stalking continuum [2], and the behavioral extinction-burst finding [3] (itself bounded — bursts occur in a minority of cases, so their *absence* proves nothing; see [[intermittent-reinforcement]]). The femicide-stalking statistic [5] traces to the McFarlane et al. femicide research and should be cited to the primary source when used in print; the broader "separation is peak risk" point is robustly established regardless.
