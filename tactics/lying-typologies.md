# Lying & Deception Typologies

> Not all deception is lying, and not all lying is malicious. This file is the KB's reference map of the *kinds* of deception — commission, omission, paltering, equivocation, bullshit — because naming the form is the first step to catching it, and because the most effective deceptions are the ones that never state a falsehood at all.

## Definition

Deception is **intentionally causing another to hold a false belief**; lying is the narrower act of *asserting* something you believe false [3]. The gap between those two is where most skilled manipulation lives — you can deceive thoroughly while saying nothing literally untrue. The major forms:

- **Commission (bald lies)** — asserting a falsehood ("I was at the office"). The prototype, and the one people overweight when scanning for deception.
- **Omission** — withholding material truth ("forgetting" to mention the lawsuit, the spouse, the prior owner). Judged less harshly than commission — the **omission bias** [4] — which is exactly why it's safer for the deceiver.
- **Paltering** — using *technically true* statements to create a false impression [2]: the car "starts right up" (true; it also stalls at every light). Palterers rate it as more ethical than lying; their *targets* judge it as unethical as outright lies [2].
- **Equivocation** — ambiguity and evasion that lets the listener infer something false while the speaker stays "accurate" (Buller & Burgoon's third deception form, with falsification and concealment) [6].
- **Bullshit** — speech with *indifference* to truth, neither asserting nor concealing it, aimed at impression rather than accuracy; Frankfurt argued this indifference makes it "a greater enemy of the truth than lies are," since the liar at least tracks the truth in order to oppose it [1].
- **Bald-faced lies** — falsehoods both parties know are false, asserted anyway (the defiant "I never said that" to someone holding the receipt) — deception of the *record*/audience more than the listener.

Underneath, McCornack's framework reframes all of these as **covert violations of the conversational maxims** we assume others obey — quantity, quality, relation, manner [6]; the deception works because we *default to believing* (truth bias), which is what makes it efficient and what makes it hard to catch ([[trust-mechanics]]).

## Variants & aliases

- **Selective truth / cherry-picking** — true facts arranged to mislead (paltering's cousin; [[framing-anchoring]], card-stacking in [[propaganda-devices]]).
- **The misleading implicature** — saying X so you'll infer Y, where Y is false and X is "defensible."
- **Lies of omission in disclosure** — the undisclosed conflict of interest, side effect, prior history ([[information-asymmetry]]).
- **Continued / covering lies** — the cascade of further lies needed to protect the first.
- **Bullshit at scale** — marketing and political speech optimized for effect over truth-value [1] ([[propaganda-politics]]).

## How it works

Two levers. **Trust-mechanics / truth bias**: humans default to believing what they're told — communication would be impossible otherwise — so deception piggybacks on the cooperative presumption that speakers mean what they say ([[trust-mechanics]]; this is also why demeanor-based lie detection fails — see Recognition). **Information-asymmetry**: the deceiver knows the truth-value and the target doesn't; paltering and omission specifically exploit the target's inability to verify in the moment, letting the deceiver later claim "I never lied" ([[information-asymmetry]]). The sophistication gradient runs from commission (easiest to catch, easiest to morally condemn) toward paltering and bullshit (technically defensible, harder to pin, and — per the research — rated by the deceiver as not-really-lying even as targets experience them as betrayal [2]).

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- **Technically-true answers that leave a false impression** — precise wording that dodges the actual question ("Did it ever fail?" → "It runs great"). Paltering's signature [2].
- **Material omissions surfacing late** — the crucial fact you "would have wanted to know" appearing only after commitment.
- **Indifference to accuracy** — claims made with no apparent stake in whether they're true, shifting freely when challenged (bullshit) [1].
- **Verifiable claims that resist verification** — reluctance to put it in writing, provide the document, or let you check.
- **The cascade** — one claim requiring an expanding scaffold of further claims to stay standing.

### Typical phrases

"I never *said* that" (paltering on the literal words). · "Technically, I didn't lie." · "You never asked." (omission defense). · "It's basically true." · "I didn't *promise*, I said I'd try." · "Trust me, you don't need to see the paperwork." · "Everyone knows that" (bullshit standing in for evidence).

### Felt-sense indicators

- The retrospective "wait — that was *technically* true but I was completely misled."
- Discovering a material fact late and realizing its omission was the point.
- Arguing about *wording* rather than substance, and losing on a technicality you know is dishonest in spirit.
- The slipperiness of a claim that won't hold still long enough to check.

### Escalation signs

Isolated convenient lies hardening into a *pattern* of strategic deception; lies escalating to protect prior lies; deception fused with [[gaslighting]] (lies about your reality), [[history-rewriting]] (lies about the past), or fraud ([[con-anatomy]], [[scams-fraud]]) where deception is the entire enterprise.

## Where it appears

Everywhere — but the *forms* cluster by domain: paltering and omission dominate **sales** and **negotiation** (where outright lies are legally riskier — [[bad-faith-negotiation]]); bullshit dominates **advertising** and **politics** [1] ([[propaganda-politics]]); fabrication and false identity dominate **fraud** ([[scams-fraud]], [[mirroring-false-identity]]); and the full range appears in **intimate relationships** (affairs, finances, the cover story). The legal line: under the FTC's net-impression standard, even a *technically true* claim is deceptive if the overall impression misleads a reasonable consumer [8] (US; not legal advice).

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

The substrate beneath many tactics: [[gaslighting]] and [[history-rewriting]] are lies about reality and the past; [[future-faking]] is a lie about intentions; [[mirroring-false-identity]] is a lie about the self; [[smear-campaigns]] traffic in fabrication and distortion. Paltering and omission are the deception-forms most other "deniable" tactics rely on, because they preserve the "I never lied" defense.

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Ask the direct, closed question** ([[verification-rituals]]): paltering and omission survive on vague or unasked questions — "Has it ever failed to start? Yes or no." "Is there anything material you haven't told me?" Specificity collapses the technically-true dodge.
- **Get it in writing**: a claim someone won't repeat in writing, or won't let you verify, is answering you ([[detection-heuristics]]). Written claims also convert paltering into something checkable later.
- **Watch the net impression, not the literal words**: ask "what do they want me to *conclude*, and is that true?" — the FTC's own test [8], applied personally.

### Structural / long-term

- **Verify, don't intuit** ([[verification-rituals]]): demeanor-based lie detection runs at ~54%, barely above chance [7] — so do *not* rely on "I can tell when someone's lying." Rely on documents, independent sources, callbacks, and consistency-over-time instead.
- **Track the pattern**: one convenient lie is human; a *pattern* of strategic deception — especially paltering and omission, which the deceiver rationalizes as "not lying" [2] — is the diagnostic ([[manipulation-audit]]).
- **Reward honesty, including unwelcome honesty**: people deceive more when the truth is punished; making it safe to tell you bad news reduces the incentive to palter ([[detection-heuristics]] at the relational level).

## Caveats & false positives

### Not malicious deception when…

- **Prosocial / white lies** ([[prosocial-lie]]): "dinner was lovely," "you look great," sparing a dying patient — lies that *benefit the recipient* are judged more ethical than harmful truths, and can even build warmth-trust [5]. They carry a real integrity cost [5], but they are not the predatory deception this file targets. Tact, kindness, and social lubrication are not cons.
- **Privacy and benign concealment** ([[benign-concealment]]): you are not obligated to disclose everything; keeping private matters private, planning a surprise, or declining to answer are not lies. Concealment ≠ deception unless it's *material and exploitative*.
- **Honest error and changed minds**: being wrong isn't lying (no intent to deceive), and revising a position with new information is integrity, not a "cascade."
- **You can't reliably catch liars by vibe** [7]: the felt-sense markers here describe deception *you later confirmed*, not a license to accuse on demeanor. Suspecting everyone is its own harm, and the base rate of ordinary honesty is high ([[epistemic-guardrails]]).

### Base rates & severity calibration

Most people are mostly honest most of the time, and white lies grease normal social life. The tactic-grade conjunction: **material falsity or misleading-by-design + intent to deceive + benefit to the deceiver at your expense + (often) a pattern** — not a single tactful fib or an unasked-for omission about something trivial ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; one white lie is rung-1 evidence of someone being kind). Severity is graded `medium` as a general descriptor — deception spans the trivial (white lies) to the catastrophic (fraud), and the band is set by stakes and pattern, not by the mere presence of a lie.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `established`: this is one of the better-grounded files in the KB. The lying/deception distinction and taxonomy are mature philosophy [3]; paltering [2], omission bias [4], prosocial-lie effects [5], and the ~54% detection ceiling [7] are solid empirical findings; Frankfurt's bullshit analysis [1] is canonical; and IMT/IDT [6] are established communication frameworks. The live debates are definitional (what *counts* as a lie — the assertion condition, whether bald-faced lies are "really" lies [3]) rather than about whether these forms exist. The honest takeaway for a defense agent: name the *form* precisely, verify rather than intuit, and never let the existence of this file turn ordinary tact into suspicion.
