# Mobbing

> A group — or a leader plus recruited colleagues — systematically ganging up on one person to degrade, isolate, and ultimately drive them out. Unlike one-on-one bullying, the *collective* nature makes the target look like the problem ("if everyone says so…"), and management usually sides with the crowd. **The mental-health toll is severe and well-documented — see Safety notes.**

## Definition

Mobbing is **systematic, hostile, unethical communication directed by a group (or a leader and recruited others) mainly at one target, repeatedly and over a long period, pushing them into a helpless, defenseless position** — Heinz Leymann's "psychological terror at work," operationalized as hostile acts occurring **at least weekly for at least six months** [1]. Leymann's **LIPT** inventory catalogs ~45 such acts across five domains (attacks on communication, social relations, reputation, occupational situation, and physical health). It is the *collective* form of workplace bullying (Einarsen's research frames bullying by **repetition + power imbalance**) [2] — and that collectiveness is the engine: a crowd against one reads as consensus that the target deserves it. The diagnostic line is **coordinated, persistent hostility aimed at degrading and expelling someone who can't defend themselves**, distinguished from legitimate collective concerns or ordinary conflict ([[legitimate-collective-feedback]]).

## Variants & aliases

- **Top-down (bossing)** — a manager leads the campaign and recruits the team.
- **Bottom-up** — subordinates collectively target a manager.
- **Horizontal** — peers gang up on a colleague.
- **Bystander recruitment** — the core escalator: others join the pile-on or stay silent, so the target faces a wall ([[proxy-recruitment]] dynamic).
- **Institutional mobbing** — the same pattern in non-work institutions (academia, religious organizations, volunteer groups).

## The mobbing sequence

Leymann described a characteristic five-phase progression [1]:
1. **Critical incident** — an original conflict or trigger.
2. **Mobbing & stigmatization** — systematic hostile acts begin (exclusion, ridicule, rumor, sabotage, withholding information).
3. **Management involvement** — leadership is drawn in but typically hears the group's narrative first.
4. **Branding / "incorrect diagnosis"** — the *target* is reframed as the problem ("difficult," "paranoid," "mentally unwell"), legitimizing the campaign.
5. **Expulsion** — the target is forced out: termination, a managed exit, prolonged sick leave, or resignation.

The cruel turn is phases 3–4: when the target finally seeks help, the institution mis-attributes the dysfunction *to them*, which both deepens the harm and supplies cover for the expulsion.

## How it works

Mobbing exploits **social proof** and **in-group/out-group** dynamics ([[social-proof]], [[unity-ingroup]]). The collective nature is doing the manipulation: observers (and management) reason that if *many* people treat someone as the problem, the someone must *be* the problem — manufactured consensus standing in for evidence. And the in-group bonds by defining itself against the scapegoat, so joining the mob earns belonging and refusing it risks becoming the next target. The target, facing a crowd rather than an individual, has no one to appeal to — every avenue is occupied by the mob or by a management that has already absorbed the group's framing. This is why mobbing is so destructive: it removes not just safety but the social reality-check that would let the target (or anyone) name what's happening.

## Recognition

(A *coordinated, persistent pattern* — not ordinary unpopularity or fair criticism; see Caveats.)

### Behavioral markers

- A group consistently excludes, ridicules, undermines, or sabotages one person, repeatedly and over time.
- Rumors and reputation attacks circulate; the target is isolated from information and social contact.
- Work is sabotaged or set up to fail; the target is criticized for things others do freely.
- When the target raises it, management treats *them* as the problem (the phase-3/4 reframe).
- New colleagues are recruited into the hostility, or stay silent for fear of becoming the next target.

### Felt-sense indicators

- Facing a wall — no allies, every avenue occupied by the group or a management siding with them.
- Dreading work; hypervigilance; the sense that you've been collectively cast as "the problem."
- Severe stress symptoms (see Safety notes): sleeplessness, anxiety, depression, trauma responses.

### Escalation signs

The campaign widening (more participants, more domains of attack); management formalizing the target-as-problem narrative (a sudden [[pip-weaponization]], disciplinary action); reputation destruction extending beyond the workplace ([[smear-campaigns]]); the target's health collapsing toward sick leave or breakdown. Mobbing's trajectory is *toward expulsion* — recognizing it early matters because the later phases are designed to make leaving look like the target's failure.

## Where it appears

Workplaces (the core arena — [[workplace-bosses]]) and other tight institutions (academia, religious and volunteer organizations). It concentrates where exit is costly, hierarchy is rigid, and management is conflict-averse or complicit.

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

Mobbing is built from [[smear-campaigns]] (the reputation-destruction component) and [[isolation-tactics]] (the ostracism component) — both declared here — and recruits bystanders via [[proxy-recruitment]] dynamics. It frequently culminates in [[pip-weaponization]] (a formal managed exit backed by the manufactured "underperformer/difficult" narrative) and overlaps the family-systems [[golden-child-scapegoat]] scapegoating logic at organizational scale.

## Counter-strategies

- **Document the pattern, with specifics** ([[documentation-practices]]): dates, acts, participants, witnesses; keep records off employer systems. Because the mob's power is manufactured consensus, a concrete, dated record of *acts* (not feelings) is what counters the "they're just difficult" reframe.
- **Find any ally and an outside reality-check** ([[detection-heuristics]]): even one person who sees it, and support from outside the workplace, breaks the totalizing sense that "everyone agrees." Name it to yourself as mobbing — a recognized pattern, not your defect.
- **Escalate strategically (with realism about HR)**: raise it through formal channels with your documentation, understanding HR serves the organization ([[workplace-bosses]]); where protected-class or retaliation angles exist, consult counsel.
- **Protect health and weight exit**: given the documented toll (Safety notes), prioritize wellbeing; because institutions rarely reverse a mob and the trajectory is expulsion, a planned exit is often the healthiest outcome — not a defeat.

## Caveats & false positives

### Not mobbing when…

- **It's legitimate collective feedback or fair criticism** ([[legitimate-collective-feedback]]): several colleagues independently raising genuine, fair concerns about real conduct or performance is not mobbing — nor is an unpopular person being held to the same standards as everyone else, or ordinary team conflict. The tactic is *coordinated, persistent hostility aimed at degrading and expelling*, against someone with little power to defend themselves.
- **The target's perception alone isn't sufficient** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]): feeling disliked or criticized doesn't establish mobbing; behavioral co-occurrence (systematic acts, over time, by a group) does. And mobbing is genuinely hard to distinguish from a badly-but-legitimately-managed performance exit — which is exactly why documentation and the *systematic/coordinated/power-imbalance* test carry the weight.
- **Not every group disagreement is a campaign**: being outvoted, getting hard feedback from a team, or being unpopular for real reasons is normal work life. The flag is the *conjunction* (group + persistence + hostility + expulsion trajectory + power asymmetry), not friction.

### Base rates & severity calibration

Prevalence varies widely by definition and method — a large meta-analysis put workplace-bullying exposure at roughly **11–18%** depending on measurement [2] (and that's bullying broadly; the coordinated *mobbing* form is a subset). The tactic-grade signature: **a group's systematic, persistent, hostile campaign to degrade and expel a target who can't defend themselves**, progressing through stigmatization → management-reframe → expulsion. Severity is `high`: the documented mental-health harm is severe (see Safety notes), and the trajectory is loss of the job and often of health.

## Safety notes

`safety: crisis-escalation`. Mobbing's health toll is severe and well-documented: anxiety, depression, sleep disturbance, somatic illness, and **trauma symptoms Leymann compared to PTSD from war or captivity** [1]. Leymann further estimated (from early-1990s Swedish data — a *dated, single-author estimate*, not a current epidemiological finding) that workplace mobbing was a factor in a notable share of suicides. Treat the toll seriously: if you or someone you know is in crisis, **US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (call/text 988); reach for support outside the workplace, and prioritize health over "winning" a campaign that institutions rarely reverse. A planned, supported exit is a legitimate and often healthiest response.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `supported`: mobbing is a well-established construct with a named originator (Leymann), a validated instrument (the LIPT), and a large follow-on literature (Einarsen's NAQ-R; a meta-analysis across ~130,000 respondents giving the method-dependent ~11–18% bullying-prevalence range) [1][2]. The health associations, including PTSD-comparable symptoms, are robustly documented (though there's a live diagnostic debate about PTSD vs. adjustment disorder, since the "trauma" criterion isn't always met). Honest flags the file carries: Leymann's model is a *clinical/theoretical* phase schema, not an experimentally validated causal sequence; the suicide figure is his *dated estimate*, not current data; and the legitimate-conflict-vs-mobbing line must be drawn at the systematic/coordinated/power-imbalance test, since a target's perception alone doesn't establish it.
