# Moving the Goalposts

> Success criteria that shift the moment you meet them. The point was never the standard — it's the striving: a finish line engineered to recede extracts permanent effort, permanent fault, and permanent debt from a target who keeps almost arriving.

## Definition

Moving the goalposts is **the retroactive or serial revision of success criteria so that completed performance never counts** — "do X and we're good" becomes, upon X, "well, X isn't really the issue." The term comes from argumentation, where it names an informal fallacy (demanding ever-higher evidence after each demand is met — "raising the bar"); as a *manipulation construct* it has no dedicated academic literature, and this file is honestly graded `folk` [1]. Its mechanics, however, are well-evidenced neighbors: unattainable contingent standards are documented in psychologically controlling parenting (conditional regard escalating into socially prescribed perfectionism in children) [3], in abusive relationships (Bancroft's clinical observation that the controlling partner's standards are *insatiable by design* — "if the goal were peace and quiet, giving in would work") [5], in high-control groups' endless transformation ladders [4], and the closest workplace finding is striking: **variably fair treatment produces more physiological stress than consistently unfair treatment** [2] — unpredictable standards hurt more than hard ones.

## Variants & aliases

- **The retroactive raise** — you did X; X is reclassified as "not what I meant" or "the bare minimum" ([[history-rewriting]]'s contract-editing cousin).
- **The receding promotion** — workplace criteria that grow a new clause each cycle ("just need you to own one more thing first"), terminating in [[pip-weaponization]] when convenient [2].
- **The unwinnable domestic test** — Bancroft's documented pattern: the partner who "can never do enough," where each met demand spawns a successor because the demand's function is control, not satisfaction [5].
- **The spiritual ladder** — the next level, course, purification, or donation as the perpetually penultimate step; the canonical journalistic case is Scientology's graded, costly "Bridge," with promised levels dangled for decades [4] ([[cult-conversion-funnel]], [[mlm-lifecycle]]'s rank systems).
- **Kafkatrapping** — the unfalsifiable variant: your denial or compliance itself becomes the new evidence of fault (folk-grade label; [[double-binds]] is the structural sibling).
- **Perfectionism installation** — the developmental form: approval contingent on standards that rise to meet every achievement [3] ([[conditional-love]]).

## How it works

Three verified engines under a folk label. **The near-miss machine**: a goal that recedes keeps you permanently almost-there — the documented motivational sweet spot ([[hope-greed]]'s near-miss findings; [[intermittent-reinforcement]]: occasional partial credit on a variable schedule is the strongest persistence glue). **The sunk-cost compounder**: each met-then-voided standard is invested effort that [[commitment-consistency]] converts into reasons to continue ("I've come this far"). **The fault generator**: under shifting standards you are *always currently failing* — a standing deficit that funds [[guilt-leverage]]-style extraction and justifies whatever treatment follows. And the unpredictability itself is a stressor: justice-variability research shows inconsistent fairness outstresses consistent unfairness [2] — the target can't even adapt to a stable bad rule.

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- **Criteria revise upon completion, reliably** — the pattern is the diagnostic: across months, met standards get reclassified rather than credited.
- **Credit never lands**: completions meet silence, "finally," or the next gap — never the promised state (the promotion, the peace, the approval).
- **Standards resist writing**: requests to pin criteria down ("so we're agreed: X by Friday means done?") get evasion, irritation, or vagueness — the move needs deniability ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]'s response-to-resistance).
- **Your record and their account diverge**: you hold a list of completions; they hold a narrative of your perpetual inadequacy.
- The goal's *function* showing: demands that multiply fastest when you're closest.

### Typical phrases

"That's not what I meant and you know it." · "Well, that was the bare minimum." · "Yes, but now the real issue is…" · "If you'd done it without being asked, it would count." · "You're almost there — just one more thing." · "I shouldn't have to tell you what would make me happy." · "When you reach the next level, it'll all make sense."

### Felt-sense indicators

- Hamster-wheel exhaustion: working harder than ever with a finish line that's never closer.
- The flinch at completing things — bracing for the new clause instead of expecting credit.
- Keeping secret score ("I *did* do that — didn't I?") because the official record keeps being revised ([[gaslighting]] adjacency when your memory of the agreement is itself denied).
- Hope renewing on schedule: each new standard feels, briefly, like *the real one at last* (the near-miss working).

### Escalation signs

Standards weaponized in formal processes (documented "performance concerns" that mutate per cycle — [[pip-weaponization]]); the domestic version fusing with [[conditional-love]] and consuming ever-more life domains (appearance, friends, schedule — [[coercive-control]]'s micro-regulation); ladder costs escalating in money or commitment ([[escalation-entrapment]]).

## Where it appears

Workplaces (promotion and approval ladders; the manager whose "one more thing" is load-bearing [2]); intimate relationships and families (the insatiable-standards pattern [5]; parental approval that rises to meet every grade [3]); high-control groups and MLMs (the perpetually penultimate level [4]); negotiations (terms reopened after agreement — [[lowballing]]'s cousin, [[bad-faith-negotiation]]); and arguments (the evidential bar that climbs each time it's cleared — [[bad-faith-argumentation]]'s home version [1]).

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

Pairs with [[conditional-love]] (the standard guards the affection supply) and [[pip-weaponization]] (the standard guards the job); feeds on [[history-rewriting]] (yesterday's agreement must be editable) and feeds [[guilt-tripping]] (the permanent deficit as permanent debt). In groups, it is the retention mechanic between [[cult-conversion-funnel]] stages. The receding goal is also how [[future-faking]] stays plausible — the promised future stays purchasable.

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Pin it in writing, kindly, every time** ([[documentation-practices]]): "So we're agreed — X by Y constitutes done?" Then, on completion: "X delivered as agreed on [date]." The move requires fog; receipts are floodlights. The *reaction to pinning* is itself the test ([[manipulation-vs-influence]]).
- **Name the move once, neutrally**: "The target's moved — that's fine if requirements changed, but let's acknowledge the change and re-scope" (*evolving requirements* is the legitimate frame; offering it flushes out whether change or control is operating).
- **Bank the credit yourself**: state completions as facts, not requests for approval ("that's done; what's next?") — withholding acknowledgment loses force when you stop bidding for it.

### Structural / long-term

- **Run Bancroft's insatiability test** [5]: if meeting standards has *never once* produced the promised state — across months and many completions — the standards are not the point. Stop optimizing the unwinnable game; decide about the game itself.
- **Keep the completion ledger** ([[manipulation-audit]]): dated agreements vs dated revisions. The pattern, on paper, ends the "am I just underperforming?" spiral one way or the other — including honestly against you (see Caveats).
- **Cap your ladder exposure**: in any system selling the next level (job, group, program), pre-decide your total spend of money/time/effort *before* climbing, and audit at each rung against the original promise ([[verification-rituals]], [[escalation-entrapment]]'s counter).

### When to exit or escalate

A game whose rules guarantee your fault is not a performance problem — it's a structure, and structures don't negotiate ([[coercive-control]] adjacency in relationships; managed-exit machinery at work). Exit calculus replaces optimization: in jobs, that means documented completions + external options; in relationships, the insatiability finding is the exit-relevant fact; in ladders, the sunk cost is sunk ([[commitment-consistency]]'s zero-base rule).

## Caveats & false positives

### Not moving the goalposts when…

- **Requirements genuinely evolve** (*evolving requirements*): markets shift, projects re-scope, parents recalibrate to a child's growth. The honest version is *announced, symmetric, acknowledges the change, and credits prior work* — change with a changelog.
- **The standard was misunderstood, not moved** (*honest disagreement*): two sincere readings of "done" diverge constantly; the first revision is communication, not tactics.
- **Your performance genuinely misses** — run the symmetric check before concluding: a neutral third party reviewing the agreement and the work ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; the completion ledger cuts both ways, and sometimes it convicts the keeper).
- **Anxious perfectionism isn't strategy**: bosses and parents transmitting their own unappeasable anxiety produce shifting standards without design (*incompetence not malice*); harmful either way, but the response differs.

### Base rates & severity calibration

Everyone has reneged on a stated standard; organizations re-scope constantly. The tactic-grade conjunction: **revision-upon-completion as a pattern + credit never landing + resistance to written criteria + the promised state never arriving** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; one re-scoped project is rung-1 evidence). The construct itself is `folk` — borrowed from argumentation, undocumented as a named manipulation tactic [1] — though every engine inside it is independently verified; treat the *label* as vocabulary and the *pattern log* as the evidence. And the accusation reverses: "you keep moving the goalposts" is itself a standard move for dismissing someone whose standards *legitimately* rose with new information ([[therapy-speak-weaponization]]) — it owes the same conjunction evidence it demands.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `folk`, plainly: no dedicated empirical literature exists on goalpost-moving as a manipulation construct [1]. What's verified: the justice-variability finding (inconsistency outstresses consistent unfairness) [2], conditional-regard parenting → socially prescribed perfectionism [3], the high-control ladder documentation [4], and Bancroft's clinical insatiability observation [5] — plus the mechanism files' established near-miss, reinforcement-schedule, and sunk-cost findings. The honest statement: the pattern is real and its parts are evidenced; the named construct awaits study.
