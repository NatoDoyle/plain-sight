# Propaganda Devices

> The classic rhetorical moves of persuasion-at-scale: slap a damning label on the enemy (*name-calling*), wrap your side in shining virtue-words (*glittering generalities*), borrow a flag's prestige (*transfer*), parade a trusted endorser (*testimonial*), pose as just-plain-folks, show only your best cards (*card stacking*), and insist everyone's already on board (*bandwagon*). Useful to **recognize** — but their real-world power is routinely **overstated**, and "propaganda!" is itself a label people weaponize.

## Definition

Propaganda devices are **the recurring rhetorical techniques used in systematic, agenda-driven persuasion** — most famously the **seven** named by the **Institute for Propaganda Analysis (IPA, 1937–42)**: *name-calling, glittering generalities, transfer, testimonial, plain folks, card stacking,* and *bandwagon* [1]. Propaganda itself is, in Jowett & O'Donnell's standard definition, "the deliberate, systematic attempt to shape perceptions, manipulate cognitions, and direct behavior to achieve a response that furthers the desired intent of the propagandist" [2]. Two honesty constraints sit at this file's center. First, the IPA seven are a **useful historical heuristic, not a validated scientific taxonomy** (the categories overlap and were never rigorously developed) [1][5]. Second, propaganda's **effects are routinely overstated**: the "all-powerful media" model is discredited, and persuasion mostly *reinforces and mobilizes the already-sympathetic* rather than converting opponents [3][4]. The diagnostic is **deliberate, systematic persuasion with a concealed source or agenda**, distinguished from the open, honest persuasion and advocacy that fill ordinary life (*healthy persuasion*).

## Variants & aliases

The **IPA seven** [1]:
- **Name-calling** — attach a damning label to provoke rejection without examination ("radical," "thug," "elite").
- **Glittering generalities** — wrap a claim in vague virtue-words ("freedom," "the people," "common sense") that resist scrutiny.
- **Transfer** — project the prestige/emotion of a revered symbol (flag, cross, science) onto your cause.
- **Testimonial** — a liked/respected (or hated) figure endorses or condemns to lend borrowed credibility.
- **Plain folks** — present the source as ordinary and relatable, "just like you."
- **Card stacking** — selective presentation: stack the favorable facts, suppress the rest.
- **Bandwagon** — "everyone's already doing it; join before you're left behind."

**Modern companions** (well-documented but later): the **big lie**, repetition/the *illusory-truth effect*, fear appeals, scapegoating, and the demonization/dehumanization of out-groups. Many overlap [[bad-faith-argumentation]] and [[thought-terminating-cliches]].

## How it works

The most pervasive lever is **framing-anchoring** ([[framing-anchoring]]): name-calling, glittering generalities, and card stacking all *set the interpretive frame* before you can evaluate the substance — a loaded label or a curated set of facts does the thinking for you. **Authority** ([[authority]]) drives testimonial and transfer (borrowed prestige). **Social proof** ([[social-proof]]) is the engine of the bandwagon ("everyone agrees"). **Unity-ingroup** and **liking-similarity** ([[unity-ingroup]], [[liking-similarity]]) power plain-folks and the us-vs-them charge in name-calling. Crucially, these devices work **best under repetition, near-monopoly of the information space, and pre-existing disposition** (Ellul) — and *least* as one-shot mind-changers on a skeptical or cross-pressured audience [2][3].

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- A **loaded label** standing in for an argument (the thing is bad because it's been *called* bad).
- **Virtue-words with no content** ("for the families," "real Americans") you're meant to applaud without asking what's actually proposed.
- A **revered symbol** (flag, faith, "the science") draped over a specific contested claim.
- **Only one side's facts** presented, with the obvious counter-facts missing (card stacking).
- "**Everyone** knows / everyone's switching / be on the right side of history" (bandwagon).

### Typical phrases

"Only an extremist would oppose this." · "We stand for freedom and the future." · "Real, hardworking people like you know the truth." · "Nine out of ten experts agree." · "Don't get left behind — everyone's already on board."

### Felt-sense indicators

- A pull to **cheer or boo before you've understood the actual claim**.
- Discomfort that disagreeing would put you with the "bad" labeled group.
- A sense the case is **all one-sided** — too clean, no trade-offs acknowledged.

### Escalation signs

Devices stacked and **repeated across many channels at once**, an information environment where the other side is hard to hear, and demonization escalating toward dehumanization of an out-group ([[smear-campaigns]], [[disinformation-playbooks]]) — the conditions under which propaganda's otherwise-modest effects compound.

## Where it appears

War and statecraft, electoral politics ([[propaganda-politics]]), advertising and PR ([[advertising-marketing]]), and activism/advocacy of every stripe — the devices are **rhetorical forms**, not the property of any one side, and they appear in honest speech as well as dishonest.

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

It co-occurs with [[disinformation-playbooks]] (the device-level companion to coordinated falsehood — declared there) and travels with [[smear-campaigns]], [[thought-terminating-cliches]], and [[bad-faith-argumentation]]. It exploits [[framing-anchoring]], [[authority]], [[social-proof]], and [[unity-ingroup]].

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Name the device** ([[detection-heuristics]]): "that's name-calling," "that's a glittering generality" — labeling it restores the gap between the cue and the claim.
- **Ask what's being smuggled past you**: a loaded label, a virtue-word with no content, borrowed prestige, or a one-sided fact set — then ask for the *actual* proposition and evidence.
- **Seek the omitted side** (the direct card-stacking counter): what facts would the other side add?

### Structural / long-term

- **Inoculation / prebunking** ([[inoculation-prebunking]]): learning the techniques *in advance* — even via short videos or games — measurably improves people's ability to spot them (van der Linden & Roozenbeek; McGuire's inoculation theory) [4].
- **Lateral reading**: check who's speaking and what they want before evaluating the message.
- **Diversify your information sources** so no single frame holds a monopoly — the condition propaganda most needs.

### When to exit or escalate

For organized demonization or coordinated falsehood (not mere spin), treat it as [[disinformation-playbooks]] / [[smear-campaigns]] and apply those counters; protect deliberation by slowing the share and widening the sources.

## Caveats & false positives

### Not this tactic when…

- **Persuasion, rhetoric, and advocacy are legitimate and unavoidable** (*healthy persuasion*): vivid language, sincere testimonials, appeals to shared values, and making your best case are normal honest communication — not, by themselves, propaganda. The devices appear constantly in ordinary speech.
- **"Propaganda" is itself a weaponizable label.** Dismissing any message you dislike as "propaganda" — while exempting your own side's identical techniques — is a bad-faith move (and is itself a name-calling/transfer play). Reserve the term for *deliberate, systematic, agenda-serving* persuasion, usually with a concealed source or deceptive selectivity.
- The line is **intent, concealment, and systematicity**, not the mere presence of a rhetorical device.

### Base rates & severity calibration

**Effects are routinely overstated** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]): the discredited "magic-bullet" picture of propaganda as mind control is folklore, and the honest finding is *small average persuasive effects* (see Evidence). But don't overcorrect into "propaganda never works" — sustained, repetitive, near-monopoly campaigns on a disposed audience *do* shift margins, and margins decide close contests. Severity is **medium**: a single device on a skeptical audience is low-harm; massed and monopolistic, propaganda can do real damage (up to incitement). Treat the IPA seven as a *recognition aid*, not a detector of guilt.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `supported`, with two load-bearing honesty flags. (1) **The IPA seven are a heuristic, not validated science**: the categories overlap, were thinly defined, and ignore audience differences and source credibility; scholars (Sproule; Hobbs & McGee) treat them as a pedagogical tool, and even IPA-associated researchers later disavowed them for serious analysis [1][5]. They remain genuinely *useful for recognition* — just not a scientific taxonomy. (2) **Persuasion effects are limited**: the "hypodermic needle / magic bullet" model is discredited; the limited-effects tradition (Lazarsfeld et al., *The People's Choice*, 1944, and the two-step flow; Klapper, 1960) showed media largely *reinforces* prior attitudes [3]; **Kalla & Broockman (2018, APSR 112(1):148–166)** found the average persuasive effect of campaign contact in general elections ≈ **zero** [4]; and Mercier (*Not Born Yesterday*, 2020) argues humans are far more resistant ("open vigilance") than the propaganda-panic assumes [4]. Inoculation/prebunking counters are `supported` but their effects are modest and decay over time. The honest synthesis — propaganda **reinforces and mobilizes more than it converts**, and works through repetition, monopoly, and disposition — is well-grounded; the boundary from *healthy persuasion* is *deliberate, systematic, concealed-agenda* persuasion, not the use of rhetoric.
