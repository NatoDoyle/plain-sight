# Smear Campaigns

> Destroying a person's reputation before — or because — they can tell the truth about you. The smear is preemptive insurance and retaliatory weapon at once: control the audience's belief about the target, and the target's account becomes unhearable in advance.

## Definition

A smear campaign is **the orchestrated spreading of damaging, distorted, or false information about a target to destroy their credibility, relationships, or standing** — typically to preempt their disclosures, to retaliate for them, or to win a conflict by disqualifying the other side. It is the malicious deployment of what aggression research calls **indirect / relational aggression**: harming circuitously through rumor, exclusion, and reputation-damage rather than directly — a well-established and measured aggression form [1]. It weaponizes a normally *prosocial* mechanism: **gossip** ordinarily polices reputations and warns the vulnerable about exploiters [2] — the smear mimics that warning while inverting its truth-value. In abuse contexts it is **preemptive [[darvo]]**: the "reverse victim and offender" beat, taken to an audience and launched *before* the target speaks, so their later account lands as the predicted bitterness of a known villain [4].

## Variants & aliases

- **Preemptive smear** — discrediting a target *before* they disclose ("she's unstable, whatever she tells you about me is revenge") — inoculating the audience against the truth ([[darvo]], [[victim-posturing]]).
- **Retaliatory smear** — reputation destruction *after* a target leaves, reports, or resists — punishment and warning to others.
- **The distortion campaign** — post-separation DV: portraying the leaving/protective partner as crazy, vindictive, or abusive, often via alienation allegations (practitioner-named; overlaps legal-systems abuse [5]).
- **Workplace mobbing smear** — rumor and ridicule as documented mobbing tactics, isolating a target toward exit [3] ([[mobbing]], [[workplace-bosses]]).
- **Competitor / market smear** — FUD, planted reviews, whisper campaigns against a rival ([[fud-competitor-tactics]], [[fake-reviews-astroturfing]], [[competitors-business]]).
- **Political character assassination** — the propaganda form, scaled to mass audiences ([[propaganda-politics]], [[disinformation-playbooks]]).

## How it works

Two verified levers. **Social proof**: a claim repeated across multiple mouths reads as consensus truth — "everyone says she's crazy" manufactures agreement the target can't outvote ([[social-proof]]; the smear's power scales with how many sources seem to independently confirm it, which is why [[proxy-recruitment]] is its delivery system). **Trust-mechanics**: credibility is the currency of being believed, and the smear spends the target's down to zero *preemptively* — by the time the target speaks, the audience has been pre-loaded against them ([[trust-mechanics]]; flat denial and counter-accusation outperform truth here because the audience's priors are already set [4]). The just-world reflex helps: audiences who've accepted the target as the villain derogate them further to keep the story coherent ([[projection-blame-shifting]]'s observer-side amplifier). The cruelty is structural: the better the smear works, the more the target's accurate self-defense sounds *exactly like* what a guilty person would say.

## Recognition

### Behavioral markers

- **Your reputation arrives before you do**: new mutual contacts treat you oddly; people you've never wronged are cool or hostile, primed by a story you didn't tell.
- **A coordinated narrative**: the same specific characterization ("unstable," "a liar," "abusive") reaches you from multiple directions — the mark of orchestration, not organic opinion ([[proxy-recruitment]]).
- **Preemption timing**: the campaign intensifies right as you're about to disclose, leave, report, or compete — discrediting you *before* your account can land.
- **Distortion, not just disclosure**: real events stripped of context, private information weaponized, or outright fabrications — the content fails verification.
- **The smearer plays victim while smearing** ([[victim-posturing]], [[darvo]]): "I don't want to badmouth them, but I have to warn you…" as the opener to badmouthing.

### Typical phrases

"I probably shouldn't say this, but you should know what they're really like." · "Everyone agrees they've become impossible." · "I'm only telling you because I care about you." · "Whatever they say about me, consider the source." · "They're not well — be careful believing anything they tell you." · (Workplace:) "I'd watch your back around them."

### Felt-sense indicators

- The bewildering chill from people who have no first-hand reason to dislike you.
- Discovering you've been "defending yourself" against a charge no one made to your face.
- The trap-sense: knowing that the more you protest the smear, the more you confirm it ("see how worked up she gets").
- Isolation creeping in as the campaign reaches your shared network ([[isolation-tactics]] by reputation).

### Escalation signs

The campaign moving from private whispers to written/public channels (raising defamation exposure but also reach); recruitment of more proxies ([[proxy-recruitment]]); migration into institutions (HR complaints, false reports, custody filings — [[institutions]], legal-systems abuse [5]); fusion with [[isolation-tactics]] (the smear *is* the severing of your network). In DV contexts, post-separation smear can accompany genuine danger — see Safety notes.

## Where it appears

Intimate and post-separation contexts (the distortion campaign [5]); families (the scapegoat smeared to the wider family — [[golden-child-scapegoat]], [[triangulation]]); workplaces (mobbing's reputation attacks [3]); markets (competitor smears — [[fud-competitor-tactics]]); and politics, where character assassination is a standing instrument ([[propaganda-politics]]). It is endemic wherever someone's account threatens someone else's standing.

## Typical sequences & co-occurrence

The public arm of [[darvo]] (declared there) and the social face of [[projection-blame-shifting]] and [[victim-posturing]] (both declared in their files). It is delivered by [[proxy-recruitment]] and structured by [[triangulation]] (declared there), and it functions as [[isolation-tactics]] by reputation (declared there) — severing the target's network from the outside. Preemptive smears front-run a target's disclosure; retaliatory smears follow [[no-contact-exit-planning]] or whistleblowing as punishment.

## Counter-strategies

### In the moment

- **Don't chase the smear in public** ([[jade-avoidance]]): frantic point-by-point rebuttal to a primed audience often confirms the "unstable" frame ("see how she reacts"). Calm, brief, factual — or strategic silence — usually beats hot denial ([[darvo]]'s observer research: pre-set priors resist live argument [4]).
- **Correct to the people who matter, not the mob**: invest in the relationships worth keeping with calm first-hand truth; concede the unreachable audience rather than exhausting yourself on it.

### Structural / long-term

- **Documentation is the counter** ([[documentation-practices]]): contemporaneous records, preserved messages, and a factual timeline turn "his word vs hers" into a checkable record — decisive in institutional and legal venues where smears migrate [5].
- **Pre-position the truth with key people** *before* a foreseeable smear (leaving, reporting): the first credible account anchors; don't cede that advantage by default ([[darvo]]'s counter).
- **Let conduct outlast the campaign**: smears decay against sustained contrary evidence; the people worth keeping update on your behavior over time.
- **Know the legal line**: a smear asserting *false statements of fact* may be defamation (US: libel = written, slander = spoken; **truth is an absolute defense**) — jurisdiction-dependent, document everything, get counsel for serious cases. Not legal advice.

### When to exit or escalate

When the smear migrates into institutions (false reports, vexatious complaints, custody filings) it is legal-systems abuse and needs records + counsel, not better explanations [5]. When it accompanies stalking, threats, or post-separation danger, safety planning takes precedence (Safety notes).

## Caveats & false positives

### Not a smear campaign when…

- **Someone is genuinely warning others about a harmful person** (*genuine warning*): the whisper network exists because warning the vulnerable about a real exploiter is *prosocial* — the mirror image of a smear, and its content is *true* [2]. The discriminators: **truth vs distortion/fabrication**, and **protective intent vs reputation-destruction for advantage**. Telling three friends "he was abusive to me, be careful" is a warning; orchestrating a network to destroy his standing with fabrications is a smear. (This distinction is the KB's own, grounded in the truth/falsity line of defamation and the prosocial-gossip research.)
- **Ordinary post-conflict talking**: both sides of a breakup or feud tell their friends their version; that's processing and support, not a campaign (*honest disagreement*). The tactic-grade is *orchestration + distortion + reputation-destruction intent*, not the existence of unflattering talk.
- **True accounts that happen to damage reputation**: accurately reporting what someone did — even publicly, even harmfully to them — is disclosure, not a smear. Reputational harm from the *truth* is the consequence of one's conduct, not an attack.
- **Believing you're being smeared when you're facing accountability**: someone facing true allegations may *experience* them as a smear; run the symmetric check ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; and note the abuser's move of crying "smear campaign" to discredit genuine accusers — [[darvo]], [[therapy-speak-weaponization]]).

### Base rates & severity calibration

People gossip, take sides, and badmouth exes constantly — most of it is organic, partly true, and not a campaign. The tactic-grade conjunction: **orchestration (a coordinated narrative via multiple mouths) + distortion or fabrication + reputation-destruction intent + preemption/retaliation timing** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]; one person bad-mouthing you to a friend is rung-1 evidence). Graded `high` severity because a successful smear can sever a target's entire support network precisely when they need it most.

## Safety notes

In post-separation abuse, smear/distortion campaigns frequently accompany escalation and genuine danger; the reputation attack and physical risk are not separate tracks. If a smear coincides with stalking, threats, or coercive control, the severity override governs ([[epistemic-guardrails]]) — preserve evidence, and plan with professionals: **US:** 1-800-799-7233 / thehotline.org · **UK:** 0808 2000 247 · list in `README.md`; protocol at [[dv-safety-planning]]. Smears migrating into false police/CPS reports or vexatious litigation are legal-systems abuse — seek counsel.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `supported`: the underlying behavior — reputation-damaging indirect/relational aggression — is well-established and measured [1], gossip's dual prosocial/weaponizable nature is documented [2], reputation attacks are a recognized mobbing tactic [3], and the preemptive-discrediting beat is anchored in the DARVO/institutional-betrayal literature [4]. The "smear campaign" label itself is colloquial, and the warning-vs-smearing distinction the file's caveats rely on is the KB's own operational rubric (grounded in defamation's truth/falsity line and prosocial-gossip research) rather than a single validated framework — flagged honestly.
