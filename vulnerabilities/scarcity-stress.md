# Scarcity & Stress

> Going without — money, time, security — narrows attention onto the immediate lack and taxes the mental bandwidth you'd use to spot a bad deal. It is a **cognitive load anyone would feel, not a defect of the poor or stressed** — the same person tests worse when resource-scarce than when not. Predators target the desperate precisely because the rescue fantasy plus a ticking clock is hard to refuse when you're underwater.

## The risk factor

**Scarcity** — of money above all, but also time or security — produces what Mullainathan & Shafir call **tunneling** (attention captured by the pressing lack, at the expense of the future and the periphery) and a **"bandwidth tax"** (scarcity consumes cognitive capacity and self-control) [1]. The decisive, anti-classist point: this is a **load, not a trait**. In the canonical study, the *same* sugarcane farmers scored worse on cognitive tests *before* harvest (poor) than *after* (rich), and lab inductions reproduced the effect — so the impairment tracks the *situation*, not the person [2]. Acute **stress** compounds it: under cortisol, decisions shift toward habit and the short term, attention narrows, and deliberation degrades — especially under time pressure [3]. (Keep this state, the *target-side* vulnerability, distinct from the manipulation *lever* it gets paired with — [[scarcity-urgency]].)

## Why it raises risk

- **Desperation makes the rescue fantasy compelling.** Financial stress primes **hope-greed** ([[hope-greed]]): the windfall, loan, or "opportunity" that *solves everything* is exactly what tunneling makes you fixate on. The FTC puts it plainly: people who need money are not only more vulnerable but more likely to believe the pitch [4].
- **Tunneling drops the future from view.** Long-term costs, fine print, and "what's the catch" are precisely the peripheral concerns scarcity crowds out — so payday/predatory loans, advance-fee scams, and get-rich pitches that would look absurd with slack look like lifelines under load.
- **It stacks with manufactured urgency.** A scarcity *state* plus a manufactured *deadline* ([[manufactured-urgency]]) removes the pause in which the deal would fall apart — and the bandwidth to use it.

## How manipulators find and exploit it

- They **target the financially stressed**: bad credit, low income, debt, recent shock (job loss, medical bills, eviction); the ad promises "credit regardless of history," "guaranteed," "no hassle."
- They **lead with the windfall or rescue** — the loan, the prize, the job, the high-return investment ([[ponzi-psychology]]), the "be your own boss" fix ([[mlm-recruitment-scripts]]), the easy-payment car deal ([[four-square-payment-packing]]).
- They **demand money up front** and **stack urgency** so there's no time to deliberate. **Universal red flag: a legitimate loan, job, prize, or grant never requires an advance fee** [4].

## Mitigation

- **Name the tunneling**: "Am I deciding from desperation?" Metacognition won't dissolve the load, but it flags decisions made inside it.
- **Build delay and slack**: impose a waiting period before any financial commitment made under stress; the deal that can't survive a day is the one to walk from.
- **Never pay upfront** for a loan, job, prize, or grant [4]; verify the lender/offer independently ([[verification-rituals]]).
- **Use legitimate assistance** (nonprofit credit counseling, benefits and assistance programs) and **get a trusted second opinion** before committing under pressure.

## Caveats

- **Scarcity-induced impairment is situational and universal — not low intelligence or a character defect** ([[epistemic-guardrails]]). It has been induced in well-off people in the lab; the science explicitly *refutes* the "poor people make bad choices because they're dumb/bad" trope. The bandwidth tax is a load, not a deficit of the person — never victim-blame the financially stressed for being targeted.
- **Don't overstate the magnitude.** The bandwidth-tax *effect size* is genuinely contested: a large pre-registered audit found scarcity-*priming* effects often fail to replicate (O'Donnell et al., 2021), the original authors dispute that audit (Shah et al., 2023), and the most robust piece is the within-subjects field result, not the lab manipulations [5]. So: "scarcity/stress impairs decision-making and raises exploitation risk" is well-supported; the precise "13-IQ-point" magnitude is **not** settled — don't cite it as fact.
- **Avoid the inverse error.** Most financially stressed people are coping rationally and are *not* being manipulated or making bad choices; this file describes *elevated susceptibility under exploitation pressure*, not a defect or an inevitability. Real, legitimate time-sensitive offers exist — the tell is the *upfront-fee / guaranteed-windfall / no-time-to-think* combination, not stress itself.

## Evidence & debates

Graded `supported`, with an explicit split: the **targeting patterns** (predatory lending, advance-fee, get-rich/MLM, Ponzi/affinity fraud aimed at the financially stressed) are well-documented by the FTC, CFPB, and SEC [4], and the broad claim that stress/scarcity degrades deliberation is supported [1][3]. The **specific cognitive bandwidth-tax mechanism** (Mani et al., 2013) is *contested in magnitude* — see the replication debate above [5]. The honest line: the manipulation-relevant core is solid; the headline cognitive figures are not, and the file rests on the documented targeting plus the situational, anti-classist framing, not on any disputed effect size.
